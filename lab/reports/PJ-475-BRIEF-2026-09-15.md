## The concept

**Put on a saved look from wherever you are standing — without opening the editor that made it.**

That is the whole horse. Three of his named looks appear in the command palette; Ctrl+P, a few letters, Enter, and the app is wearing it. Nothing else.

The palette survived the attack. Two of the three filed questions did not survive as questions, and one filed *feature* — the "active style" marker — did not survive at all: I measured that it cannot tell the truth for one of his three styles even in the instant after he applies it. What DID survive scrutiny, and changes the build, is that applying a Saved Style is not a cosmetic act. Measured on his live universe: applying "Eisa Default" silently discards 54 of the 174 CSS variables he has tuned, and the app has no undo of any kind. A one-keystroke door onto an irreversible write of his own design work is not shippable without a way back. So the feature is SMALLER than filed in what it displays, and slightly LARGER than filed in what it protects.

## The three filed questions — SETTLED, do not re-ask them


**Q: (a) Should the palette refresh when a style is renamed or deleted with the palette closed?**

- **Answer:** DISSOLVED — there is nothing to refresh once there is one owner. Two independent reasons, both verified. First, the palette is created fresh on every open: it lives inside `{#if showCommandPalette}` and is fed by `commands={getCommands()}`, a template expression re-evaluated from current data — it cannot carry a stale list across opens. Second, and this is the real work: today the preset list exists in exactly one place, a component-local `$state` inside the Style Setter, loaded in its own `onMount`. Move it to a shared store in `stylePresets.ts`, route the Setter's six write sites through that store, and rename/delete/save/update/import propagate by construction — no event bus, no listener, no invalidation policy. This is the project's own 'one source of truth, tested once, used many times' rule, not a design choice. EXPLICITLY REJECTED: re-loading the presets when the palette opens. It puts an `invoke()` on a surface that must feel instant, and because `commands` is a reactive prop the new rows would pop in a tick after the panel is already up, underneath a `selectedIndex` that resets whenever the filtered list changes — rows appearing under a moving selection is a defect, not a refresh.
- **Evidence:** One writer: `saveStylePresets` (src/lib/libraries/stylePresets.ts:152) has exactly one caller, `persistStyles` (src/lib/components/StyleSetter.svelte:1064), reached from saveAsStyle :1074, importStyle :1085, confirmRename :1099, removeStyle :1108, updateStyle :1119 — plus the rollback assignment at :1067. Component-local today: StyleSetter.svelte:727. Palette gated and rebuilt: src/routes/+layout.svelte:10458-10461. Hotkeys screen fed from the same call: :10607. Selection reset on filter change: src/lib/components/CommandPalette.svelte:36-39. Sole load call site: StyleSetter.svelte:1298.


**Q: (a-corollary) When do the presets load? The ledger says 'only when the Setter opens.'**

- **Answer:** THE LEDGER IS WRONG, and this corrects the entry as well as the brief. `<StyleSetter />` is mounted UNCONDITIONALLY, at one tab of indentation, as a direct child of `<div class="app">` inside the app-ready `{:else}` branch. The `{#if $styleSetterOpen}` guard sits after `</script>` and gates the MARKUP, not the lifecycle. So `loadStylePresets()` already fires at first paint on every boot — and again on every universe switch, because `handleUniverseSwitch` sets `appReady = false`, tearing down and remounting that whole branch. ## For the Boss (the plain-language version he was given)

**What you'll be able to do**

Press Ctrl+P from anywhere — inside a note, in Focus mode, wherever you are — type a few letters of one of your saved looks, and press Enter. The whole app puts that look on. No trip into the full-page Style Setter. Your three saved looks each get a row, and if you ever want one on a key of its own, you can give it one in Settings → Hotkeys like any other command.

**Two things worth knowing before you test it, both from your own files**

All three of your saved looks are *light*. Choosing any of them also sets the app to light — that's true today in the Style Setter too, it just becomes a lot easier to do by reflex. Until you save a dark look, this door can only hand you light ones.

And: your saved looks aren't all the same size. Your current look has 174 tuned colours and sizes in it. "تنسيق عيسى الرئيس" matches it exactly — putting that one on changes nothing. But "Eisa Default" only carries 120 of them, so choosing it quietly throws away 54 things you'd tuned, including your confidence colours and your Inspector-360 colours. "Eisa Default 02" throws away 17. That's been true every time you clicked a style in the Setter, but it took two navigations to get there; one keystroke is different. So this build also gives you an **Undo** — a small line that appears after you switch, with one button that puts everything back exactly as it was. It fades on its own after about fifteen seconds.

**What's left for you to decide: nothing.**

All three questions the backlog had filed against this job are settled — one by the app (the ids it needed already exist and already work), one by the build (keeping one list instead of two means there is nothing to "refresh"), and one killed outright. That third one was "mark which style is currently on." I went and measured it against your actual files rather than reasoning about it, and it can't tell the truth: because putting a style on *adds* to your link colours rather than replacing them, the tick mark would be permanently dark for "Eisa Default" even one second after you'd chosen it, while working fine for the other two. A mark that's silently impossible for one row in three is worse than no mark, so there is no mark. You'll know which look you're wearing because the whole app repaints in front of you.

The one decision genuinely still yours is the one already on your desk — the fight between the old light/dark switch and your Saved Styles, the thing you called the worst theme you'd ever seen. This job doesn't touch it and doesn't pre-empt it. It does make it cheaper to live with in the meantime: one keystroke back to a look you trust.
