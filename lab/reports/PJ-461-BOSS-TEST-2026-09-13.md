# Part 1 — What this is, and why it matters

## The feature

Constellation's appearance is built from **named settings**. A line of code does not say "paint this
grey"; it says "paint this the theme's border colour" — and the theme decides what that means in
light and in dark, and your Style Setter can override it. That indirection is the reason one choice
in Settings can repaint a hundred places at once.

## What was broken

**Seventeen of those names were spelled in a way that matches nothing the theme defines** — near
misses, mostly names borrowed from other apps. A name that matches nothing does not raise an error.
It fails quietly, in one of three ways:

1. **If the line had a spare value written beside it**, that spare value is used instead. It is a
   fixed number, so it ignores your settings and looks the same in light and in dark. Most of the
   seventeen behaved this way — which is why some things never responded to the Style Setter.
2. **If the line had no spare value**, nothing is painted at all. That is why one row in your sidebar
   has never had a highlight.
3. **If the name sat inside a combined instruction** — size, weight and typeface written on one line —
   the browser discards **the whole instruction**, not just the bad part. That is why a set of labels
   designed to run from 9 to 15 pixels have all been drawing at one single size.

## What this build does

It corrects **fifteen** of the seventeen, so each one reaches the setting you actually chose.

- **One is held back for a decision that is yours** — the Calendar's typeface. That is Stage 4, and
  it is a question, not a test.
- **One is deliberately left alone** — it belongs to the Style Setter's link-tooltip family and is
  reserved for a control that does not exist yet. Renaming it would break that family's pattern for
  no visible gain.

## Why it matters

Almost none of this is a redesign. Every change makes something start obeying a setting you had
already chosen and that was being silently ignored, and two of the five steps below are places where
**a selection you made was invisible** — a usability fault, not a cosmetic one. **The one exception is
Step 2**, where you asked for the diagram to use its empty space and approved a new layout for it.

## Step 1 — The divider between task rows  ✅ PASSED 2026-09-13

### What this is
The **Tasks** tab in the right-hand sidebar lists the checkboxes — done and not-done — **in the note
you currently have open**, with a filter above them that starts on "All". A thin line separates one
task row from the next.

### What was wrong
That line's colour was written under a name the theme does not define, and the spare value beside it
was **almost black**. So the divider has been far darker than any other divider in the app, in every
theme.

### Do this

**Before you start:** open a note that contains at least two checkboxes.

**Action 1:** look at the **right-hand** sidebar. Along its edge is a row of small tabs.

**Action 2:** click the tab whose tooltip reads **"Tasks"**.

**Action 3:** look at the thin horizontal line separating one task row from the next.

**You should see:** a normal, faint grey divider, the same weight as dividers elsewhere in the app.

**If instead you see** a near-black line, the fix has not taken — you are probably running an older
build.

**If the panel is empty:** open a different note. This panel shows only the open note's own tasks.

## Step 2 — The Note graph preview in the Style Setter  ⟳ RE-TEST

### What this is
The Style Setter shows a live preview of each surface you can restyle. One of them is the **Note
graph** — a centre box labelled "Note", and two fans of coloured wedges: incoming links on the left,
outgoing on the right. Each wedge's length is its weight; the order within a fan is its rank. You
pick Relationship colours on the right and watch the wedges recolour.

You said the diagram should use its empty space. The reason it could not was measured: the nine
names rode outward past their own wedge tips, so about a fifth of the card's width was text, and the
wedges could not grow while the names competed for the same room.

**The names have moved into two rows** — one along the top, one along the bottom — each with a small
coloured square and a thin line of the same colour running back to its wedge. All the freed width
went to the wedges, which are now **1.64 times longer** and cover about **2.6 times more area**. The
centre box, the dotted spine, the two fans, the colours and every meaning are unchanged.

### Do this

**Before you start:** be in the main window, with no full-page view open.

**Action 1:** click the **gear icon** (tooltip **"Settings"**).

**Action 2:** in the list down the left, click **"Style Setter"**. A full-page screen opens.

**Action 3:** in the **"Surfaces"** list on the left of that screen, click **"Note graph"**.

**Action 4:** look at the diagram, then at the two rows of names.

**You should see:** wedges reaching close to the left and right edges of the white card; the nine

**Action 5:** in the right-hand rail, change one Relationship colour.

**You should see:** that wedge, its connector line and its colour square all change together.

**If instead you see** names still sitting beside their wedge tips, the build has not taken — check

**Two details worth your opinion**, both deliberate and both reversible:

## Step 3 — The selected row in Template Studio  ✅ PASSED 2026-09-13

### What this is
Template Studio finds the kinds of note you keep writing. Its screen has two halves. **This step is
about the LEFT half only** — the narrow column where each row shows a **count and a name**, such as
**"11 Test"**. That column is the list of kinds, and you click a row in it to select that kind.

The right half — the "Notes like this" links, "What do you call these?", "What these notes carry" —
is the detail for whichever kind is selected. **Nothing in this step concerns the right half.**

### What was wrong
In that left column, the selected row was painted **the exact same shade as whatever row your pointer
is hovering over**. Since your pointer is on the row you have just clicked, the selection became
invisible the instant you made it.

### Why this step deserves the most attention
The first attempt at this fix **did not work**, and that was caught before you saw it. The colour was
corrected, but the rule was written in a way that still lost to the hover rule. It was rewritten and
rebuilt. This step is the check that the second attempt holds.

### Do this

**Note first:** in Scratch you have only **one** kind, "11 Test". That is enough — this test compares
that one row against itself in two states, which is actually the sharper check.

**Before you start:** be in the main window.

**Action 1:** click the dock button whose tooltip reads **"Template Studio"**. You will see
"Kinds of note you keep writing", with the kinds column on the left.

**Action 2:** click the row in that left column — the one reading **"11 Test"**.

**Action 3:** leave your pointer resting **on that row**, and note its background colour.

**Action 4:** move your pointer well away from it — down into the empty space below the column is
fine — and look at the row's background colour again.

**You should see: the same tint in both states.** A soft tint of your accent colour at Action 3, and
the identical tint at Action 4. The row's colour should not change at all when your pointer arrives
or leaves.

**If instead you see** the row go a plain grey while your pointer rests on it at Action 3, and only
turn to the accent tint after you move the pointer away at Action 4 — the second fix has not taken.
That difference between the two states is precisely the defect, and it is what this step exists to
catch. Please say so.

**A note on the colour:** your accent is a blue, not the app's default purple, so the tint you are
looking for is a pale blue.

**If the screen says** no repeating kind of note was found, skip this step.

## Step 5 — The saved text of a deleted note  ✅ PASSED 2026-09-13

### What this is
When a note is deleted, Constellation keeps the text it had indexed, so you can still see what was
lost. Settings lists those entries and lets you expand one to read its text.

### What was wrong
That text was meant to be shown in **the app's Code font** — the typewriter-style font used for
things you read character by character. The name was one of the seventeen, and the spare value beside
it was the browser's own generic typewriter face. The result looked deliberate, which is why it went
unnoticed for so long: it was monospaced, just not the font Constellation chose.

You have not set a Code font of your own, so what you will now see is Constellation's default Code
stack, which begins with Cascadia Code.

### Do this

**Before you start:** be in Settings. If you closed it after Step 2, reopen it with the gear icon.

**Action 1:** in the list down the left, click **"Universe & Libraries"**.

**Action 2:** scroll to the very bottom, to **"Deleted notes"**. If you see a **"Show"** button, click
it.

**Action 3:** find a row whose label reads **"… characters of indexed text kept"** — not "no indexed
text kept".

**Action 4:** click that row to expand it, and look at the saved text underneath.

**You should see:** the saved text in Constellation's Code font, not the generic typewriter face.

**To compare against a known-correct example:** open **Settings → Hotkeys** and look at the keyboard
shortcut keys, or look at a colour's hex value in the Style Setter. Both already use the Code font
and always have.

**Do not compare against** the file path shown on the deleted-note row itself. That path uses the
interface font and is not part of this change.

**Also on that same row:** its thin outline darkens slightly. That is a second corrected line from
this batch. On your machine it is a real, visible change — on a default theme it would be
near-invisible — because your Style Setter pins that border colour.

**If instead you see** no change at all to either the text or the outline, the fix has not taken.

## STAGE 4 — a decision, not a test. **Nothing in this build changes it.** *(held)*

Where you have chosen a Calendar font, it renders and always has — your daily universe is set to
"Dubai" and shows it. The broken part is the **fallback**: what the Calendar is supposed to use when
you have *not* chosen one. That fallback asks for a bundled Arabic calligraphic face, then — by
intent — your Note font, though it names that font by one of the dead spellings this job is about.
It is written in a form the browser rejects outright, so the whole instruction is discarded and the
Calendar inherits whatever font surrounds it. That is true today and **still true
in this build**: those two lines were deliberately left untouched, because every valid way of
rewriting them is a typeface choice, and that choice is yours.

Three options:

- **(a)** let the intended calligraphic face render, wherever you have not set your own Calendar font;
- **(b)** use your Note font instead;
- **(c)** keep exactly today's look.

Your ruling lands as its own separate commit. Note that seven of your nine universes already have a
Calendar font set ("Dubai"), so the difference would only show in **Eisa Universe** and
**Constellation Test**, the two that have none.
