# Suggested Connections

*(More like this — your note's unconnected relatives, ready to link)*

Constellation is a tool for **formulating** knowledge, not just storing it — and what makes knowledge is *connection*. **Suggested Connections** finds the notes already in your Library that are most related to the one in front of you — the relatives it *should* be linked to but isn't yet — and lets you turn any of them into a **typed link** in a single click. It answers the question every growing library eventually raises: *"what does this note belong with?"*

Think of it as **"more like this," but for thinking.** It doesn't just find similar notes; it offers to make each one a deliberate, reasoned link.

---

## Why every suggestion is *typed*

When you accept a suggestion, Constellation asks one short question: **how do these two notes relate?** Does this note **support** the other, **contradict** it, **exemplify** it, **derive from** it, and so on — or do they simply relate (**associative**)?

This is the heart of the feature. A link without a type is just a thread between two notes; a **typed** link is a piece of reasoning you can later read, search, and challenge. Suggested Connections never adds links in bulk and never adds an untyped link silently — every connection it helps you make is an act of formulation, not filing. (See **Knowledge Formulation** and **Properties**.)

---

## How it finds the suggestions

The candidates come **only from your own Library**, ranked against Constellation's live search index. It looks for the notes that share the most *distinctive* vocabulary with the current note — the rare, telling words, not the common ones — so the matches are meaningful rather than superficial.

Every suggestion shows the **shared terms** that explain *why* it surfaced. You are never asked to accept a black-box guess: you can see the words the two notes have in common before you decide.

---

## Where it appears

The same suggestion list is offered in five places, each suited to a different moment:

| Surface | When you'll use it |
|---|---|
| **The Reviewer** (🕐) | When it flags a note as an **orphan** ("connect me") or **fragile** ("shore me up"). |
| **Backlinks tab** (right sidebar) | While reading a note's existing incoming/outgoing links. |
| **360° Inspector** | Inside a note's full structural picture. |
| **Health tab** | Alongside a note's tensions and weak points. |
| **Sky View** (🌌) | Right-click any star → **Suggest connections…**. |

---

## Inbound vs outbound — and why you don't choose

Each surface suggests connections in the direction that fits its job, so you don't have to think about it:

- **Diagnostic surfaces** — the **360° Inspector** and the **Health tab** — suggest **inbound** connections: *which notes should point **here*** (who should cite or build on this note). These surfaces are about a note's standing in the web, so they ask "who should depend on this?"
- **General surfaces** — the **Backlinks tab** and **Sky View** — suggest **outbound** connections: *what this note should point **to*** (what it should build on or relate to).

The surface picks the direction; you pick the note and the type. (A future update will let you switch the direction yourself.)

---

## How to use it — step by step

1. Open any of the surfaces above. Under the heading **Suggested connections**, you'll see a ranked list of related notes — **closest first** — each with the shared terms that connect it.
2. Find one that genuinely belongs with your note. Click its **Link** button.
3. A small menu appears: **How do they relate?** Pick the relationship type (supports, contradicts, exemplifies, generalizes, derives-from, part-of, supersedes, or **associative** if they simply relate).
4. The link is created **instantly** and the suggestion drops off the list. The new typed link now lives in the note's **properties**, appears in its backlinks and outgoing links, and takes its place across the graph and Sky View.

---

## If nothing fits

If none of the suggestions truly belong, that is a perfectly valid outcome — leave them. In the Reviewer you can also mark the note a **deliberate standalone** ("this is meant to stand alone — it isn't an orphan"). Suggested Connections *proposes*; you always decide.

---

## Good to know

- **Local and private.** Suggestions are computed on demand, entirely from your own Library. Nothing is sent anywhere.
- **It never blocks your typing.** On a very large note the list may take a moment to gather, with a brief "Finding related notes…" spinner; your editor stays responsive throughout.
- **It speaks your language.** The suggestions, the shared-term hints, and the "how do they relate?" types all appear in your chosen interface language, and the layout mirrors correctly for right-to-left languages.
