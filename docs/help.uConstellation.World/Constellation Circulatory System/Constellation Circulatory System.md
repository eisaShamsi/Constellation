# Constellation Circulatory System (CCS)

> **الجهاز الدوري** — the pulse of your thinking.

Most tools hand you a filing cabinet. Constellation hands you a **living body of thought** — and CCS is
its pulse. Open it and you won't see a list of links. You'll see which ideas are **warm** with recent
thinking and which have gone **cold**; where your **convictions** are hardening into bedrock and where
your **doubts** are still alive; what you keep returning to, and what you connected once and quietly
abandoned. A connection you made and never walked again isn't understanding yet — it's a note you wrote
to yourself and forgot. CCS tells you, at a glance, whether your thinking is **still circulating**.

Your knowledge has a nervous system (**CNS** — how it's wired) and a circulatory system (**CCS** —
whether it's alive). They are siblings, and they answer the Connection question together: CNS reads
*structure* (communities, bridges, centrality); CCS reads *flow* (weight, traversal, lifecycle,
confidence). CNS never tells you a load-bearing bridge is going stale; CCS never tells you a worn link
is a bridge.

---

## Opening CCS

Three doors, all equivalent:

1. **The dock** — the pulse-line icon in the left dock, directly below CNS.
2. **The command palette** — type "Circulatory".
3. **Settings → Links → Related** — the "Circulatory System (CCS)" button.

CCS opens instantly regardless of how many links you have: it reads a snapshot that Constellation keeps
fresh in the background (the same engine Knowledge Health uses). The very first open after an update
may say *"Taking your knowledge pulse…"* for a few seconds — once, while the snapshot is first built.

The header shows the scope (**this universe**) and the total number of recorded links. The
**Knowledge Health →** button jumps to the count-cards overlay; Knowledge Health carries a matching
**Circulatory System →** button back. The two surfaces coordinate — CCS is the deep instrument,
Knowledge Health the at-a-glance cards.

## The seven registers

Each register is a question you already ask about your own thinking:

| Register | The question | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| **Living Connections** | *What am I actively thinking through?* | The links you've walked most, still warm (traversed within 90 days), with walk counts and last-walked dates. |
| **Load-Bearing Reasoning** | *What does my understanding rest on?* | The warm links with the highest earned weight — your arteries. Weight grows each time you traverse a link. |
| **Cooling Inquiries** | *What have I stopped returning to?* | Links you once walked but haven't touched in 90+ days, coldest first. An invitation, never an accusation — facts are allowed to rest. |
| **Conviction & Doubt** | *How settled is my thinking?* | The confidence spread (hypothesis → evidence → established → contested) plus the live contested links — your active disputes. |
| **The Life of a Connection** | *Where are my links in their lifecycle?* | The five-tier census: fresh (never walked) · emerging (1–2 walks) · established (3–9) · load-bearing (10+) · stale (90+ days idle). |
| **Retired Reasoning** | *What did I set aside — and can revive?* | Archived links, with a **Restore** button per row and **Show all** when there are more than twenty. |
| **The Acts of Inquiry** | *What kinds of thinking am I doing?* | The distribution of your typed links in the canonical order of the cognitive acts, plus an **Open inquiries** line — your untyped links, the questions still asking how they relate. |

Clicking any row opens the note where that link lives. Browsing CCS never alters the numbers it shows —
opening a note from a register does not count as walking the link.

## Restoring retired reasoning

1. Open CCS → **Retired Reasoning**.
2. Click **Restore** on the row you want back.
3. The row leaves the register; the link reappears in the note's Backlinks / Outgoing panels.

A restored link keeps its type, annotation, confidence, and full traversal history. Its weight restarts
at 1.0 and is earned again through use.

## The freshness rhythm

CCS reads a cached snapshot, refreshed in the background a couple of minutes after things change. If
you archive or restore a link and reopen CCS quickly, you may briefly see the previous numbers — they
update in place within moments. Nothing is recomputed while you wait; the panel is always instant.

## Where the old Link Dashboard went

The right-sidebar **Link Dashboard** tab retired into CCS. Its jobs moved to better homes: most-traveled
→ **Living Connections**; stale → **Cooling Inquiries**; archived + restore → **Retired Reasoning**;
most-connected stays in **Knowledge Health**; orphans live in the tension panel (the right-sidebar
health tab). The cross-library and broken-links lists were retired with the panel.
