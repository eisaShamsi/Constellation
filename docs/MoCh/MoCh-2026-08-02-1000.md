## 4 · **The steer that changed the outcome**

I began exploring for a solution. The Boss stopped it:

> *"Instead of exploring for a solution, try to see how we overcame the same issue before. We
> reach almost instant boot time."*

This redirected the work from open-ended profiling to reading our own boot-perf record — MIG-079
and Orientation §9.2, where the covering-index primitive is already written down. **The answer
was in our history, not in a new investigation.** The defect turned out to be a partial index
built for `WHERE cid_cn != ''` while the boot probe asks `WHERE cid_cn = ''` — its exact
complement, the one predicate the index is guaranteed *not* to serve. 109.7 s of the boot.

## 5 · "Take the standing rule"

With the index fixed, boot was still ~16 s because MIG-108's rewrite had left the database 21.5%
free pages. I raised the options; the Boss chose the general one over the particular one:

> *"Take the standing rule."*

Three words that specified the design: not "vacuum the database" but **"after any mass rewrite,
the database defragments itself."** A rule, gated by a tested predicate, that runs without being
asked. This is a recurring pattern in his direction — he consistently chooses the rule over the
action.

## 6 · The Boss watching it run, then finding the flaw in it

He pasted the console line at **10:54:59**:

> `[1785653699] [defrag] standing rule: freelist 106155 of 494728 pages (21.5%) — compacting`

Then, while it ran:

> *"I didn't sew the [defrag], or maybe I missed it."*

He had seen the *console* line but not any indication **in the app**. That is the real defect:
the vacuum took **33 minutes** (10:54:59 → 11:27:56) with the app frozen and silent. Earlier he
had closed it mid-run — which was the correct response, since an invisible multi-minute freeze
is indistinguishable from a hang.

Two fixes followed (`c3b7bdc1`, committed at 11:08 while the vacuum was still running): wire the
progress events to **MIG-041's `MigrationProgressStrip`** — *a surface that already existed and
that I had simply never wired* — and make an interruption cost **10 minutes** of cooldown rather
than the 24 hours a genuine failure earns. Punishing a user for closing their laptop the same way
you punish a real error is how a self-healing routine stops running.

## 7 · Next item

> *"Fix the tree-refresh bug next."*

The deleted note that stayed in the tree. Root cause: the delete handler resolved the note's
library by **first match**, then refreshed the tree only if that lookup succeeded. MIG-108 made
it reachable — every library now sits inside the universe root, so the root's path prefixes them
all. Six call sites were answering that one question three different ways; they now share one
resolver.

## 8 · The closing instruction

> *"Proceed with the ~27–34 s cold boot that predates today, three untriaged inspection
> registers, and then the orientation doc."*

An explicit ordering of three items. Result on the first: bucketing all 896 recorded boots by
idle-gap showed the historical 27–34 s **was** the cold-cache bucket (median **26.7 s** across
304 cold boots), and the first genuinely cold boot after the vacuum measured **1.2 s** — so the
month-old cost was the same disease and the standing rule had already cured it. Reported with
the honest caveat that n=1.
