## The question that changed what I was doing

> **"And how your fix solved this issue? How the app would deal with those phantom paths/entries?"**

He asked this after Step 2 shipped and the count appeared. It was the right question and I had not
volunteered the answer: **my fixes had removed nothing.** Every fix that day had been to a *guard*
that was blocking the count from appearing — the 603 were exactly as they had been that morning.

Answering it properly meant separating three things I had been letting blur together: what Step 2
did (count), what Steps 3 and 4 would do (remove, with consent), and what the app does to the
*files* (nothing — they are already gone). He was entitled to that distinction before being asked
to press anything.

## What I decided alone, and would have asked about if he were watching

- **Step 3 has no UI, on purpose.** The executor is registered but unreachable from any screen
  until Step 4. That keeps them separately revertible, which matters more than usual here because
  the action cannot be undone.
- **I did not commit Step 4.** He tests before commit, and this is the step that actually removes
  his data.
- **I fixed a hazard rather than warning about it.** `Enter` instantly confirmed the delete dialog.
  My first attempt keyed the fix to the "danger" flag, which would also have taken Enter away from
  his everyday note-delete confirmation — a change he never asked for, on a flow he uses daily, for
  an action that is recoverable from the trash. Narrowed it to an explicit opt-out used only where
  there is no way back.
