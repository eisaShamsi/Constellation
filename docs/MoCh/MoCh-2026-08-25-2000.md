## 2. What I told him, in order

**First, the shape.** Three complete copies of his `كون عيسى` universe sit inside `Eisa Universe`,
each with its own database. Constellation knows how to stay out of a universe he has *declared*; it
has no idea a folder that is *itself* a universe is off-limits. So it walks in and treats 16 of their
notes as `Eisa Universe`'s own.

**Then, the whole-ecosystem measure**: 8,006 identities claimed by more than one file across 16,073
files — but almost all of it inert, because the biggest population (`MIG108 Rehearsal`, 4.6 GB) is
federated to nothing and collides with nothing.

**Then a severity correction against myself.** An audit had flagged four commands that rename his
files as able to reach inside a nested universe. I checked before repeating it: three have no caller
in the app at all, the fourth is live but cannot currently fire. I told him the alarm was smaller
than it looked rather than let it stand — *"I'd rather tell you that than hand you an alarm the
evidence doesn't support."*

## 3. The finding I did not expect

The panel found, and I verified in `Eisa Universe`'s own migration journal, that **Constellation made
these copies itself**. The journal names the source universe, the destination folder, and the word
`"copy"`. The MIG-108 "One Universe, One Location" tidy-up treated another universe's root as if it
were a library — which it looks like, because a universe's default library *is* its root.

**And the hole is already closed** — a guard landed on 21 August, strengthened on the 22nd, that now
refuses exactly this. The copies date from 7–8 August, before it.

So the honest summary is three sentences: the generator is fixed, the debris is still there, and
about thirty places in the app still cannot tell a nested universe from a folder.

Tracing the backups myself then turned up a **second casualty of the same run**: a registered library
of his, `موسوعة عيسى`, was dropped from the registry between 16:53 and 16:54 on 7 August — and 589 of
his notes are still labelled with a library that no longer exists.

## 5. Where the panel overruled me

I briefed it that PJ-387's archive fix was a **prerequisite** for this migration, because cleaning up
the 16 rows would destroy 8 of them "with no record at all". Both halves were wrong: a durable line
*is* written to the diagnostics file for every skipped note, and — the part I should have seen,
having measured it myself hours earlier — **those 8 notes are not identity-less at all.** Their files
carry real identities. They are blank *in the index* because a duplicate claimed the identity first.
Which is to say: the duplication Eisa noticed is the *cause* of the blanks I had been investigating
since this morning. The prerequisite came out of the plan.

Five other errors of mine in the same brief were caught the same way, including "about forty surfaces
need fixing" — misleading, because roughly thirty close through two shared switches, and presenting
it as forty hand-edits would have argued for exactly the sprawl his own Whole-Ecosystem Fix Law warns
against ("a shared helper so they cannot drift again").
