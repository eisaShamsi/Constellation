## 2. What I reported back, and in what order

**First report — the two causes.** Ten of the thirteen are a deliberate refusal the app had been
logging in plain text all along: when two live files claim one identity, the indexer will not steal it
and files the second under a blank. The source is two folder copies of a whole universe sitting inside
`Eisa Universe`. The other three carry two stacked frontmatter blocks and the app reads only the first.
I also reported that the ledger's own proposed remedy — "reindex the 13" — cannot work for ten of them.

**Second report — a correction against myself.** The verifier broke two of my claims. I had told him
"fifteen of the twenty-five" rows in his daily universe carry change history a delete would end; eleven
of those fifteen are **Templates**, which are identity-exempt **by his own ruling of 19 July**. The
number was right and the impression was wrong. I also confessed the scan error: I had searched for
second frontmatter blocks with a pattern that only matches English property names, on a corpus that is
mostly Arabic — in the project whose founding principle is Language-First by Design. The count moved
from 4 to 12.

**Third report — a correction against my correction.** The panel overturned the single most
decision-relevant thing I had told him: that opening these notes writes an identity into them, so the
population was drifting. I checked it myself and reported the reversal — 232 of 234 would never be
written. Then the verifier broke *that* too, by running the shipping function against copies of all 234
files: **none of them, not 232.** The two I had named as exceptions fail for a second, different
reason.

The finding underneath it is the one worth remembering: **a note is permanently denied an identity
because it explains how identities work.** 219 of the 221 are copies of the orientation document, which
contains a worked example showing an identity line; the app scans the whole file, sees the example, and
concludes the note is already named.

## 5. The thing I did not go looking for

While tracing why the app could not repair three files, the panel and then the verifier established
that **there is live code today that creates this defect** — a disagreement between the Rust parser
(which tolerates a blank line before a note's properties) and the two frontend parsers (which do not).
Reproduced through the shipping save paths. Nothing in his libraries is currently in the vulnerable
state, but the Rust half of this exact class was fixed months ago and the frontend half was never
swept. Filed as **PJ-396**, Group 1.

That is the Whole-Ecosystem Fix Law catching a miss of itself.
