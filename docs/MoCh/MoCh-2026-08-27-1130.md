## 1. "How Obsidian approach similar issue? Lets find the right solution."

*(11:39)* One sentence, and it arrived **before** I had proposed anything — which is what made it
work. My instinct was already formed and already wrong: narrow the dot-skip so it applies to
directories only, since `.git`, `.obsidian` and `.trash` are all folders, and a dot-named *file*
that is real markdown could then be indexed.

Obsidian does not do that. It hides dot-prefixed **files** as well as folders, deliberately and
across every surface, and it has held that line through years of people asking for the exception.
The convention is older than either app: on the platforms this rule comes from, a leading dot means
"not for the file listing", and applications that honour it half-way produce files users cannot
find with their own tools.

So the research overturned my proposal rather than confirming it, and the fix shrank. **The rule is
right. The defect was one line where two doors disagreed** — the door that creates notes stripped
leading dots from a name; the door that *imports* them did not. A file arriving through the
importer could be born hidden, and nothing downstream would ever mention it again.

This is the fourth time WA#5 has changed a design rather than ratified one. It is also the cheapest
review in the project: he spent nine words.

## 2. "Approved" — three parts

*(13:20)* On the three-part solution:

## 4. What I told him today that was false

Two things, both corrected before they could act on anything, both mine:

- **"2 would have been 4."** I justified a de-duplication guard by saying the two files sit under two
  overlapping library roots and would otherwise be counted twice. The walk already de-overlaps its

- **"Renaming breaks nothing."** I checked by searching his notes for `[[.NET]]`. His app does not
  write links that way — it writes `[[supports::.NET]]`. My search could not have found them.
  **25 different notes point at these two — 28 links in all, two notes repeating one.**

The inspector caught the second one and drew the wrong conclusion from it — that renaming would
*orphan* 25 links. It would do the opposite. Those links are dead **now**: no note with either name
exists in the index, because the files are hidden. Constellation matches links by a note's **title**,
not its file name, and both files keep their titles inside them. Renaming the file makes the notes
visible and lets all 25 resolve — for the first time.

So the honest version of the sentence I got wrong is stronger than the sentence itself: renaming is
not merely safe, it is a repair.

**The pattern underneath all three errors is one thing, and it is worth more than the fixes:** each
