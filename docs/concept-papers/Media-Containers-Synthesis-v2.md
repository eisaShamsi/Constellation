## Why this document exists

The first synthesis was produced from **two of the four** tradition studies. The **European** and **South Asian + Hebrew** studies were truncated before delivery. The first pass said so honestly and flagged the gap as its top open question — but every conclusion it drew about page architecture came from two **flowing-margin** traditions only, and it therefore never saw the **fixed-frame** model that the two missing studies exist to describe.

This pass exists to (a) read those two, (b) correct one factual error the first pass propagated, (c) answer the fixed-frame vs flowing-margin question, and (d) re-base the whole survey on **secular** evidence.

**§2 (Scope) and §10 (What v1 got wrong) are the accountability sections.** Read those two if you read nothing else.

## 1. Headline

> **A fixed frame is not an alternative to a flowing margin — it is what a flowing margin BECOMES when its layers stop being personal and become canonical. The mechanism of that change is reproduction. Constellation has no reproduction layer: every Universe is a unique instance, exactly like a manuscript. Qusasah is therefore a flowing-margin feature by necessity, not by preference — and a fixed frame is an end-state a layer can EARN, never a starting architecture.**

The corollary, which is the buildable part: **in a fixed frame a layer's type is carried by its POSITION; in a flowing margin it must be carried IN the annotation itself.** You pay for the type information either way. The only decision is which channel carries it — and Constellation, having no fixed frame available to it, must pay in the annotation. That is why the Arabic secular manuscript page evolved explicit voice markers, self-signing glosses, and colour-coding, while a designed frame does not need them.

The v1 headline stands, confirmed now by all four traditions, and should be carried forward:

> Every historical container got its shape from physics for free. Digital affords nothing and denies nothing, so a note shape is a **synthetic container** — a deliberate re-imposition of constraints the substrate no longer supplies.

Its strongest proof, on secular evidence, is now the European **roll/codex split by purpose**: after the codex won for *books*, the roll persisted for *records* — the English Chancery and Exchequer kept rolls for centuries, "because a record series wants append-only sequence, not random access." The same culture ran two container families simultaneously because the two purposes differed. The European study calls this "Eisa's purpose-tracks-medium point in its sharpest European form."

## 2. Scope — the secular exclusion (what was removed and why)

**Ruled by the project owner mid-research: sacred, scriptural and liturgical texts are excluded as design evidence.** This survey is deliberately secular. It is not incomplete by accident, and a future reader should not try to "fill in" the material named below.

### 2.1 Rationale

1. **Wrong model.** A sacred text's layout is optimised for recitation, veneration, ritual validity and transmission-fidelity. It answers questions a personal knowledge system never asks. Constellation is a tool for one person working out their own thinking.
2. **It already produced a factual error.** The first pass generalised **Qurʾanic** mise-en-page — **آية** (*āya*) separators as rosettes and coloured circles, **سُورة** (*sūra*) headings, overlining — to all Arabic prose, and concluded Arabic might lack a paragraph. **That is false** (see §10.1). Excluding sacred texts removes the entire failure class rather than patching instances of it.

### 2.2 Precisely what this removed

| Removed | What the survey lost with it |
|---|---|
| **Qurʾan / مُصحَف (*muṣḥaf*)** | The muṣḥaf/qurʾān naming pair — v1's "strongest argument that shape is first-class." The pre-codex scrap media (**عُسُب** *ʿusub* palm-ribs, **لِخاف** *likhāf* stone flakes, **أكتاف** *aktāf* scapulae), which this research attests **only** through a scriptural-collection narrative. |
| **Torah scroll & Masoretic codex** | The scroll-vs-codex dual-container proof; the **masorah-as-checksum** import; the limit case of constitutive ceremony; the cleanest two-valued evidence for the *authority* dimension. |
| **Talmud page (Vilna Shas)** | **The corpus's single best fixed-frame specimen** — designed zones with named owners, the radial epistemic gradient (explanation → dialectic → cross-reference → practical consequence), the universal citation coordinate with frozen line-breaks, and the frozen-core/flowing-rim hybrid. |
| **Biblical *glossa ordinaria*** | The canonical exemplar for the detailed zone description of the Latin scholastic page. |
| **Buddhist material** | Sutra rolls and the liturgical driver behind the concertina (經摺裝 / 折本 *orihon*); the Tripitaka Koreana; the Dunhuang cache. |
| **Divinatory material** | Oracle bones 甲骨 (*jiǎgǔ*) as an endurance exemplar. |

### 2.3 What survives, and it is most of the layered-page evidence

Layered annotation is overwhelmingly attested on **secular** texts and stays fully in scope:

- **Arabic scholarly apparatus** — **حاشية** (*ḥāshiya*) / **تعليقة** (*taʿlīqa*) / **لَحَق** (*laḥaq*) glossing; the **مَتْن** (*matn*) vs **شَرح** (*sharḥ*) layout; **عَطْفة** (*ʿaṭfa*) anchor marks; **ضَرْب** (*ḍarb*) strike-throughs; collation marks (**بلغ** *balagha*); **تملّك** (*tamalluk*) ownership notes. **Load-bearing detail:** this research's source for *both* the matn-inviolable rule *and* the ʿaṭfa reference mark is the **National Library of Medicine's *Islamic Medical Manuscripts* glossary** — i.e. the two most important Arabic findings in this survey are sourced from **medical** manuscripts, not scripture.
- **Arabic secular genres throughout** — history (**خَبَر** *khabar* / **أخبار** *akhbār*, Ṭabarī), lexicography (*Lisān al-ʿArab*), chancery (al-Qalqashandī), poetry (**دِيوان** *dīwān* of al-Mutanabbī), and the personal-notebook family (**بَياض** *bayāḍ*, **سَفينة** *safīna*, **كُنّاش** *kunnāsh*, **مُسوَّدة** *musawwada*).
- **European** — Alexandrian **σχόλιον** (*scholion*) and the critical signs; the glossed ***Corpus iuris civilis*** and **Aristotle**; *apparatus criticus*; **variorum** editions; commonplace books; waste books; wax tablets; *pecia*; catchwords; manicules and reader marginalia.
- **East Asian** — **注疏** (*zhùshū*) layered commentary on secular classics; **評點** (*píngdiǎn*) editions of Ming–Qing **commercial fiction**; the late-Qing **Du Fu** edition; **頭注** (*tōchū*) head-notes; **訓点** (*kunten*), **返り点** (*kaeriten*), **振り仮名** (*furigana*); **書き入れ** (*kakiire*) reader annotation; 簡牘 bamboo slips; 巻/冊; 筆記 (*bǐjì*).
- **South Asian** — *pothī*; the grammatical-philosophical **सूत्र** (*sūtra*) and its commentarial ladder (**भाष्य** *bhāṣya* · *vṛtti* · **टीका** *ṭīkā* · *vārttika*); copper-plate charters as legal instruments.

**On سَماع (*samāʿ*, audition certificates):** kept, because attestation on a non-scriptural text exists in this research — the Leder case study is on a manuscript of **al-Kharāʾiṭī's *Iʿtilāl al-qulūb*** (Gotha Ms. Orient. A 627). *I believe that work to be adab (belles-lettres); the study does not characterise its genre and I have not verified it in this pass.* **[gap]** — whether the broader samāʿ corpus Hirschler analysed is predominantly religious or secular is **not established** in this research.

### 2.4 Gaps the exclusion creates — stated, not papered over

1. **No measured secular fixed-frame page.** The European study states that the glossed-page architecture "serves the glossed *Corpus iuris civilis* and Aristotle" — but every *detailed zone description* it supplied was written with the excluded biblical exemplar as its canonical case. So the secular fixed frame is **attested but not described** in this research. §4 proceeds on that basis and flags every place it matters.
2. **The Hebrew track now contributes no admissible design evidence.** Every Hebrew container surveyed — scroll, Masoretic codex, Talmud daf, Mikra'ot Gedolot — is sacred or canonical-scholastic. **Hebrew secular writing was never surveyed**: responsa (*she'elot u-teshuvot*), contracts (*shetarot*), community registers (*pinkasim*), and the vast documentary Genizah corpus of business letters and court records are all absent. **Unknown for secular texts in this tradition.** This is a real finding, not a footnote: had the exclusion not been applied, the trap of §10.1 would have been *unavoidable* on the Hebrew side, because there was no secular Hebrew material in the survey to correct against.
3. **The epistemic-distance ordering of layers** was attested only in an excluded source. §4.6 re-bases the recommendation on a gradient that *is* secularly attested in all four traditions.
4. **The frozen shared citation coordinate** loses its strongest specimen. It now rests on the general (and secure) European finding that print "fixes the page — and thereby makes PAGE NUMBERS usable as shared references, **which manuscript culture could not rely on**."

## 3. The affordance model

**Verdict: the five hold. Add one — authority. Two of v1's proposals are re-typed as system invariants rather than shape dimensions. Ceremony is not mis-typed so much as CONFLATED: two different things wearing one name.**

### 3.1 The five, re-tested

| Dimension | Confirmation from the two new studies (secular evidence only) |
|---|---|
| **Extent** | European: "the roll's physical length caps the ancient 'book'" — *which is why ancient works are divided into books at all*; the wax face caps the note; **the pre-ruled commonplace heading caps the entry**. South Asian: the palm leaf is 2–7 cm high, and the *sūtra* is compressed to the point that the style has its own name — ***laghutva***, "brevity" — a unit memorisable in one hearing that fits one narrow leaf. |
| **Ceremony** | See §3.3. |
| **Persistence** | European adds the **waste book** (German *Sudelbuch*, Lichtenberg's own term; Newton used the English term for the notebook in which he worked out the calculus): entries made fast and unordered "in the expectation of being posted elsewhere or discarded." The study calls it "the strongest historical warrant for a 'meant-to-be-processed' note kind." This **independently corroborates** the Arabic **مُسوَّدة → مُبيَّضة** (*musawwada* "blackened" draft → *mubayyaḍa* "whitened" fair copy). Two unconnected traditions, same shape, no contact. |
| **Attachment** | Confirmed and extended to **four** grades — §3.4. |
| **Signal** | European study, quotably: layers are distinguished by (a) script size, (b) script type, (c) position, (d) colour — "**these are the SAME signals a UI has: size, face, position, colour. There is no fifth trick.**" |

### 3.2 The sixth dimension: AUTHORITY / evidentiary weight

The European study proposes this itself, unprompted: "a sixth dimension is arguably missing from the five — **AUTHORITY/EVIDENTIARY WEIGHT**, which in this tradition is a first-class property of the container (bronze tablet vs. paper draft; **the university's corrected *pecia* exemplar vs. a private copy**) and is **not reducible to persistence**."

Secular corroboration from the Arabic side: a manuscript stating **قوبل على الأصل** (*qūbila ʿalā l-aṣl*, "collated against the exemplar") "is worth more than one that does not." Two traditions, same idea: **a container can carry a warrant.**

**Honest reduction after the exclusion.** I had a two-valued account of authority — *validity-for-use* × *warrant-for-belief* — and its cleanest evidence was an excluded dual-container case. On the surviving secular evidence:

- **Warrant-for-belief is well supported** (pecia exemplar; collation marks; the *sigla* and lemma of a critical apparatus).
- **Validity-for-use is thin.** **[gap]** — I have no strong secular case for a container whose ceremony makes it *valid to perform an act with*, as distinct from *credible to believe*. Do not build on it.

This is convenient rather than costly: warrant-for-belief is the half that matters to Constellation, and it names a live gap in the product. Constellation already ships warrant-for-belief **on links** — the four confidence levels (hypothesis → evidence → established → contested). It does **not** ship it on notes or note shapes. The historical finding is that authority is a **container** property, not merely a relation property.

### 3.3 Ceremony: conflated, not mis-typed

v1 concluded ceremony is "a proxy for irreversibility and cost, both ~zero in digital," and inferred it should be earned or dropped. **Right for one kind, wrong for the other.**

**(a) Tariff ceremony** — proportional to cost and irreversibility. Cast bronze **金文** (*jīnwén*), where the text must be finalised before the object exists and can never be corrected; the inscription; the capital act of binding. This kind **does** evaporate in digital, and manufacturing it is manufacturing friction. **v1 correct.**

**(b) Constitutive ceremony** — the procedure *is* the definition of the kind. Its clean, cheap, fully secular case is the **commonplace book** (*liber locorum communium*), codified by Erasmus in *De copia* (1512). Its ceremony is **exactly one thing — a heading** (*locus communis*) — and the study says that heading "is its whole discipline." It costs nothing, is fully reversible, and is still load-bearing: an excerpt without a heading is not a commonplace entry, it is a jotting.

> **Testable rule for every template Constellation ships: a required field is legitimate iff it is CONSTITUTIVE — without it the note is not that kind of note — and illegitimate iff it is a TARIFF on a costless, reversible action.**

That replaces "ceremony must be earned by irreversibility" with something that can actually be applied to a form.

### 3.4 Attachment: four grades, not three

v1 established three (interlinear/word · marginal/passage · whole-object). The South Asian material adds a fourth, and it is the digital-native one:

**Separate-document-linked-by-quotation.** The commentarial ladder on grammatical and philosophical *sūtras* — *bhāṣya* → *vṛtti* → *ṭīkā* → *vārttika* — was kept as **physically separate manuscripts**: root text and explanation were "structurally separate objects... rather than one edited whole." Attachment without co-location; the link is quotation of the root.

The Arabic tradition reaches the same terminus from the other direction: **ḥāshiya** as a *genre* is a full commentary that began as marginal annotation and became its own authored, citable work.

This grade matters because it is the **terminal state of overflow** (§4.5) and is buildable in Constellation today with existing typed links.

### 3.5 Re-typing v1's proposed sixth and seventh

v1 proposed **addressability** and **provenance-carrying** as dimensions. Both phenomena are real and better evidenced now. **Neither is a dimension of a note's shape:**

- **Addressability is a system-level guarantee.** No note shape is "more addressable" than another; the Universe either grants stable addresses or it does not. And the European study shows it is not even universal — it is a property **print created**: manuscript culture "could not rely on" page numbers. Addressability is downstream of **reproduction**, a system parameter. (This is exactly why the fixed frame's payoff is unavailable to Constellation — §4.4.)
- **Provenance-carrying is a universal constructor obligation.** Every layer signs itself, in every tradition, without exception (§8). Applying to every shape equally, it discriminates nothing.

**Final model: five + authority as per-shape dimensions; addressability and provenance as system-wide invariants that every shape depends on and none supplies.**

## 4. Fixed frame vs flowing margin — the primary question

### 4.0 The two architectures, on secular evidence

**FIXED FRAME** — a designed geometry allocating named zones to a known-in-advance, finite set of layers.

The **glossed scholastic codex**, mature c. 1150–1250. The European study describes the architecture and states that it "serves the glossed ***Corpus iuris civilis*** and **Aristotle**." Zones:

1. **Centre / base text** — largest script, ruled with lines **far apart specifically to leave room between them**.
2. **Interlinear gloss** — very short notes (a synonym, a one-word identification) in much smaller script *between* the base lines, sitting directly above the word explained. **Anchoring is purely positional; nothing marks the base text at all.**
3. **Marginal gloss** — longer exposition in smaller script, in **ruled columns flanking the block**, often above and below too, "so the base text becomes an island."
4. **Running titles and division marks** for citation.
5. **Later readers' layers** — added over centuries in the outer margin and any blank space: *nota* marks, manicules (☞), pen-trials, ownership inscriptions, collation marks, corrections. "Each hand is a distinguishable stratum."

> ⚠ **Evidence flag.** The study's canonical exemplar for this description was an excluded text. What survives is its explicit statement that the *same architecture* serves the legal and Aristotelian glossed books — plus the study's own admission that it described the architecture "from **secondary descriptions**, not from measured codicological studies of named shelfmarks." So: **the secular fixed frame is attested but not measured in this research.** Every downstream claim carries that discount.
>
> The coordinator's brief names **Accursius's *Glossa Ordinaria* on the *Corpus Juris Civilis*** as the primary secular exemplar. **That attribution is not in any study in this research pass**; I know it only from general knowledge (Accursius, Bologna, c. 1230s, whose apparatus superseded the earlier glossators' and was the standard gloss in printed Roman-law folios). **Treat as unverified here** and see §11.

**Two further secular fixed-frame specimens, both fully in scope:**

- **The *apparatus criticus*** — "this architecture printed": the established text on top, a horizontal rule, and beneath it the variants, each keyed by a **bold lemma** plus line number and identified by **sigla** (single letters for manuscript witnesses).
- **The variorum edition** — a **three-band page**: text / variants / commentary, three layers over one unaltered base, formally separated by rules.

Their ancestor is also secular: the Alexandrian **σχόλιον** (*scholion*), the marginal exegetical note descending from Aristarchus and his successors, which used a repertoire of **critical signs in the margin** — the *obelos* (÷) marking a suspected line, the *asteriskos* (※). A pure symbol-in-the-margin vocabulary.

**FLOWING MARGIN** — annotation accretes where there is room; no zone reserved in advance.

- **Arabic** — *ḥāshiya* in the outer and lower margins, written "at a slant or **spiralling around the corner** so that new notes can be added without colliding with old ones"; certificates go "wherever there is room."
- **East Asian** — **頭注** (*tōchū*) above the frame line, **傍注** (*pángzhù*) beside a column, **雙行小字** (*shuānghang xiǎozì*, half-size double columns) in-channel.

### 4.1 What a fixed frame SOLVES

1. **Position carries TYPE, for free.** A fixed zone per layer means the layer's identity costs no ink and no words. The eye sorts before the mind reads.
2. **Non-collision by pre-allocation.** No layer can crowd out another. In a flowing margin, one verbose early annotator consumes the space a later one needed.
3. **Scope legibility.** Interlinear = "this word means that word." Marginal = "about this passage." The gradient is spatial and instant.
4. **A shared coordinate, once reproduced.** Print fixes the page and makes page numbers usable as shared references — "which manuscript culture could not rely on." *(Weaker than it was: the corpus's strongest specimen of a deliberately frozen shared layout was excluded — §2.4.)*

### 4.2 What a flowing margin SOLVES

1. **Unbounded, unknown-in-advance annotation.** The only constraint is "is there room." No canon-formation, no committee.
2. **Per-instance individuality.** The Arabic study's decisive line: "the annotations belong to **THE COPY**, not to **THE WORK**. Two copies of the same text carry different lives." The European study, from the other side: "a glossed copy and an unglossed copy contain the **IDENTICAL** base text. The commentary is a separate object that happens to share a page."
3. **Proportional space.** A note takes exactly the room it needs; a fixed band starves a voluble layer and wastes space on a silent one.
4. **Zero design cost.**

### 4.3 The cost of each — and the design consequence

**The flowing margin's cost is that position carries no semantics** — which is precisely why the Arabic secular page evolved compensating machinery that a designed frame does not need:

- explicit voice markers **قوله** (*qawluhu*, "his statement:") and **أقول** (*aqūlu*, "I say:") in red at every switch of speaker;
- self-signing terminations — a gloss ends in **صح** (*ṣaḥḥ*, "verified") or the glossator's abbreviation;
- **overlining** to mark the lemma quoted from the base text;
- glosses shaped as spirals, triangles or rotated blocks "so its boundary is visible as an object."

> **The trade, stated once: fixed frame → type is carried by POSITION. Flowing margin → type must be carried IN the annotation. You pay either way; only the channel differs.**

v1 could not see this, because both traditions it received were flowing-margin — so the compensating machinery looked like ordinary good practice rather than **the price of an architectural choice**.

**The fixed frame's costs:** it requires the layer set to be finite and known; it forbids reflow (its whole payoff is a stable geometry); it pre-allocates space that may not fit the content; and — the commonplace book's documented failure, which is the same pathology in miniature — "**a head that fills up cannot be extended without spilling.**"

### 4.4 Which model fits digital annotation?

Constellation's qusasah is: **unbounded** in number · **arbitrary** in length · **unknown at layout time** · **personal** (2026-you over 2019-you) · over an **editable** host · in a system with **no reproduction layer**.

| Fixed frame requires | Constellation has |
|---|---|
| A finite, known-in-advance layer set | Unbounded, unknown |
| Named canonical layer owners | One author, or you-at-different-times |
| A frozen host | An editable `.md` on disk, also changed by watcher and sync |
| Identical reproduced copies | Unique per-user instances |
| A shared readership to justify a frozen layout | None |

**Verdict: flowing margin, unambiguously — for storage, accretion, and default rendering.** Borrow from the fixed frame as a *sort*, never as a *geometry*.

### 4.5 What each tradition did when annotation OVERFLOWED — five mechanisms

The question the physical page was forced to solve and digital most needs answered.

| # | Mechanism | Tradition & evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Rotate / spiral into the margin's unused geometry** | Arabic: glosses at a slant, spiralling around the corner, as triangles and rotated blocks — using the space's *other dimensions* rather than more space. |
| 2 | **Paste in a new physical surface** | Arabic: inserted material "must be squeezed into margins **or on pasted slips**." This is literally the qusasah — a slip added when the margin runs out. Related: **لَحَق** (*laḥaq*, "that which is caught up"), omitted text supplied in the margin and tied to its gap by an **عَطْفة** (*ʿaṭfa*), a curved stroke drawn at **both** ends. |
| 3 | **Move to another channel / add a surface** | East Asian: in-channel 雙行小字 → above-frame 頭注 → beside-column 傍注 are three distinct channels; a late-Qing **Du Fu** edition is documented printing **five commentators' layers plus readers' punctuation from six separate blocks, in different inks**. When one surface would not hold it, they added a printing pass. **[unverified]** — the study found no *stated* East Asian overflow convention; this is inferred from the channel architecture and the Du Fu case, and 頭注/書き入れ terminology is itself flagged unverified. |
| 4 | **Shrink the HOST's display, not the annotation** | European, and the inversion worth stealing: the zones are **planned before writing**, and "in the mature form the base text is written **LAST** or written to fit an already-measured commentary block"; "the marginal columns are wider and carry far more text than the [base text] they surround — **the base text can occupy a small fraction of the written area**." The *apparatus criticus* and the variorum three-band page do the same: the band grows and the text band yields. ⚠ Carries the §4.0 evidence discount **twice** (secondary description, excluded canonical exemplar). |
| 5 | **Secede — become a separate document, linked by quotation** | South Asian: the *pothī* "denies... wide margins for commentary," so the commentarial ladder lived as **separate manuscripts**. Arabic: *ḥāshiya* as a genre becomes its own authored work. Two independent traditions, same terminus. **[unknown]** — the South Asian study explicitly could not establish whether pothī manuscripts developed *any* systematic marginal-commentary layout. Do not assert either way. |

And the **failure** mode: the **palimpsest** (παλίμψηστος, *palimpsēstos*, "scraped again"). When the surface itself runs out you scrape and reuse — **and the erasure is never total.** The Archimedes undertext survived because a later era thought it disposable and overwrote it. Its lesson is about *delete semantics*, not overflow (§9).

### 4.6 The hybrid

**Yes, and it has two halves.**

#### Half A — Fixed LANES, flowing CONTENT

A small, fixed lane set makes position semantic; unbounded lane *contents* keep the flowing margin's capacity.

> **Re-based after the exclusion.** I had ordered lanes by **epistemic distance** (explanation → dialectic → cross-reference → practical consequence). That gradient was attested only in an excluded source, and I am not going to substitute a religious example and relabel it. The gradient that **is** attested secularly, in all four traditions independently, is **SCOPE**:

```
  ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │  HOST (unaltered — never written into)       │
  └──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
     · WORD scope      — interlinear: "this word means that word"
                         (Latin interlinear gloss · Arabic interlinear ·
                          振り仮名 furigana)
     · PASSAGE scope   — marginal: "about this passage"
                         (ḥāshiya · marginal gloss · 頭注 tōchū)
     · NOTE scope      — about this thing as an object
                         (tamalluk ownership · collation marks · reader marks)
```

Scope is the same axis as the **attachment** dimension (§3.4), which means the lane geometry falls out of the affordance model rather than being bolted on — a good sign.

**A second, optional lane axis is KIND** — but note honestly that the traditions supply **three conflicting ordering principles** and none is universal (§7.4). Make it a setting, default off.

#### Half B — Crystallisation (the lifecycle)

A qusasah is **born flowing** — personal, unstructured, in the margin. When one proves load-bearing (repeatedly consulted, cited by other notes, carried across host revisions), it can be **promoted into a fixed band**.

This is the documented trajectory of the layered page: the *glossa* corpus "accret[ed] over generations," and "the standardised 'ordinary' layout is a 12th–13th c. achievement, **not present from the start** — earlier glossed books look different." Personal marginalia → repeated consultation → canonical status → typographic fixture. **[contested]** on authorship of the glossa corpus generally; *(the parallel crystallisation of Accursius's legal apparatus out of the earlier glossators is general knowledge, unverified here — §11.)*

It maps onto machinery Constellation already has: the *musawwada → mubayyaḍa* promotion gesture and the `supersedes` typed link.

**And the same structure answers overflow.** The five mechanisms of §4.5 reduce to a build order:

1. **Grow the lane.** Default. *(The glossed codex's own answer: a designed core with reader layers accreting in the outer margin and any blank space over centuries.)*
2. **Shrink the host's display, not the annotation.** Host **content** immutable; host **display extent** negotiable. This is the thing responsive digital does that parchment could only do at design time — the single most transferable overflow behaviour in the corpus, carrying the §4.0 evidence discount.
3. **Secede.** Terminal state: the qusasah **becomes a note**, linked to its host. Attested independently in South Asia and Arabic; buildable today.

**Refuse** mechanisms 1 and 3 (rotation, channel-hopping): physical-space tricks with no digital referent.

## 5. Container → shape table

Scored on the six dimensions. Weighted to the two traditions v1 never saw. Sacred containers are absent by ruling, not by oversight.

| Container | Extent | Ceremony | Persistence | Attachment | Signal | Authority | Digital analogue |

## 6. Qusasah lessons — concrete and buildable

### 6.1 Anchoring — v1 got this wrong, and the fix is 2,200 years old

The European study names the primitive outright:

> "The **LEMMA** is the tradition's universal anchor primitive. A note about a text does not live inside that text; it lives beside it and **carries a QUOTATION of the words it attaches to**. That is the قصاصة mechanism, already solved, in use continuously from Alexandrian scholarship to the modern critical edition."

**Three** anchoring models, not two:

| Model | Mechanism | Survives host edit? |
|---|---|---|
| **Positional** (East Asian 頭注; Latin interlinear) | Pure alignment. No mark, no number. *Position is the link* | **No** — destroyed by any reflow |
| **Referential mark** (Arabic **عَطْفة** *ʿaṭfa*) | A curved stroke at the gap in the line **and** at the head of the marginal supplement | **No** — still a mark at a *place*; durable only because parchment does not reflow |
| **Quotational** (European **lemma**) | The note opens by quoting the base-text words it comments on, underlined or rubricated | **Yes, by construction** — it carries a copy of what it points at |

**Build the *apparatus criticus* pairing:** store the **lemma** (quoted span) **and** a positional address; **when they disagree, the lemma adjudicates and the position is repaired.** The printed apparatus does exactly this — bold lemma + line number, where the number is the brittle half and the quotation is the durable half.

**Render** with East Asian immediacy: the remark appears beside its passage with **no visible footnote number, no superscript, no marker cluttering the host**. Store explicitly; display spatially. The user never sees the plumbing.

### 6.2 Page architecture

1. **The governing law, absolute:** annotations go **outside** the host's text block, never inside it. The **مَتْن** (*matn*, lit. "the firm ground, the back") is inviolable — and this survey's source for that rule is a **medical**-manuscripts glossary, not a scriptural one. **A qusasah never enters the host's body — including "just this once for convenience."** The moment one does, the guarantee is gone and the user can no longer trust what they are reading.
2. **Authority through architecture, not locking.** Nobody forbade writing in the *matn*; the page's architecture made it obviously wrong. Do not make the host read-only — make the qusasah **obviously not the host**.
3. **Fixed lanes by SCOPE, flowing content** (§4.6 Half A).
4. **Real margin, budgeted before layout.** 袋綴じ *manufactures* margin structurally. A token gutter with small type is a margin in name only — users will write in the body instead and the feature dies. If the viewport cannot afford real margin, change the primitive (overlay, facing pane, reveal). Do not ship a token strip.
5. **Host display extent is negotiable; host content is not** (§4.5 #4).

### 6.3 Visual layer distinction

**Size · face · position · colour. There is no fifth trick.** Concretely:

- **Size** does most of the work alone — half-size means commentary, in every tradition independently. The East Asian **雙行小字** is the most elegant instance: where the base runs one column of large characters, the gloss runs **two columns of half-size characters inside the same column width** — occupying the same channel as the glossed word, displacing nothing, requiring no reference mark.
- **Face** — formal book hand vs cursive gloss; small apparatus type.
- **Position** — the lane.
- **Colour** — rubrication; **朱** (*zhū*, vermilion) is the conventional ink for reader annotation in East Asia; matn in red and *sharḥ* in black, or the reverse.
- **Bounded shape** — Arabic glosses written as spirals or rotated blocks "so its boundary is visible as an object." The digital equivalent is a **card boundary**, not a rotation.

**And a universal worth naming:** in the Arabic tradition a container's identity propagated into a **typeface** — **خط الرقعة** (*khaṭṭ al-ruqʿa*), the fast informal script named after the **رُقْعَة** slip it was written on. A note shape may legitimately carry its own typography. Historical norm, not decoration.

### 6.4 Provenance

**Every layer signs itself, in every tradition, without exception.** A gloss terminates in **صح** (*ṣaḥḥ*) or the glossator's abbreviation. Collation marks state what was collated against what, and when. European hands are distinguishable strata that palaeographers routinely date and separate. Japanese **乎古止点** (*okototen*) conventions differ by scholarly lineage, so the annotation layer records *which tradition* read the text.

The strongest secular design precedent is Arabic historiography: **خَبَر** (*khabar*, pl. *akhbār*) — a discrete narrated report carrying its own chain of attribution. Ṭabarī's history is literally a sequence of *akhbār*, **each independently attributed, sometimes contradicting each other, laid side by side without the historian resolving them.** An atomic, sourced, deliberately non-reconciled unit of record. That is a remarkable design precedent for a knowledge system built on *tension* as a first-class act.

**Build:** every qusasah is stamped with who/when/in-what-context **by construction**, not as optional metadata — because 2026-you annotating 2019-you is exactly the Boss's case.

### 6.5 Kinds

A remark's kind matters and the kinds are not interchangeable. The secular apparatus distinguishes at minimum: **gloss** (explains) · **objection** · **variant reading** · **correction** · **collation mark** (*balagha* — progress addressed to a future self) · **critical sign** (*obelos* "I doubt this") · **ownership**. Note that several are about **process and provenance**, not meaning.

**Correction works by addition, never by editing.** **صح** (*ṣaḥḥ*) written above a word means "this reading is correct, **do not 'fix' it**" — a *defensive* mark protecting an unusual but genuine reading from a future well-meaning copyist. Struck-through text (**ضَرْب** *ḍarb*) **stays legible**. Erasure (**كَشْط** *kashṭ*, scraping) was the exception and a scraped patch is treated by codicologists as *suspicious*.

**A checksum kind, re-based after the exclusion.** I had drawn this from an excluded source. The surviving secular instances are (a) **collation marks**, whose evidential purpose is to prove the copy was verified, and (b) the **stichometric count** written at a text's end recording how many lines it contains — which is exactly a checksum, since it makes omission detectable. **[contested]** — that stichometric counts *primarily governed scribal payment* is an interpretation and is debated; the counts themselves are real. Constellation analogue: a qusasah kind that **pins a fact about the host** so later drift becomes visible — *"on 2019-04-11 this note asserted X."*

**Ship a small set and instrument which are used.** A professional scholarly culture with institutional incentives sustained an eight-way distinction; whether one person in a personal system does is an empirical question no historical source can answer.

### 6.6 Overflow — the build order

1. **Grow the lane** (default).
2. **Shrink the host's display, not the annotation.**
3. **Secede** — the qusasah becomes a note, linked to the host.

**Do not pre-allocate fixed space per lane.** That is the commonplace book's documented failure.

## 7. Cross-cultural divergences — where ONE default would be wrong

**7.1 Margin geometry is not one shape rotated.** Arabic: outer + lower, an **L-band**. East Asian: **above** the frame (頭注; 眉批 *méipī*, "eyebrow comments," the upper margin being 書眉 "the book's eyebrow"), plus **傍注** beside a column. Latin: **flanking columns both sides**, plus above and below — the base text becomes an island. A surface that hard-codes "notes go right" is silently Latin-typographic; "outer" is Arabic; "above" is Sinographic. **The zone must be a function of script flow direction.** Constellation ships `detectDir()` and is Language-First by design — it has no excuse.

**7.2 Anchoring** — three models, §6.1. Choosing one and calling it universal would be a real flattening.

**7.3 What the annotation is MADE OF.** In the Arabic tradition layers are overwhelmingly **textual and authored** — a gloss is prose by a named person. The Japanese **訓点** (*kunten*) layer is something else entirely: **返り点** (*kaeriten*) instruct the reader to jump backwards and re-order Chinese SVO into Japanese SOV; **送り仮名** (*okurigana*) supply inflections Chinese does not write; **乎古止点** (*okototen*) encode a particle purely by **where a dot sits** around a character. A complete *reading* — word order, grammar, morphology — superimposed on a text that is **not touched**, deliberately kept at a different scale and off-axis so the original stays legible through it. **Not every annotation is a remark.** Some are transformations of how the host is to be *processed*. If qusasah is defined as "a textual remark," it forecloses the reading-instruction class (a translation layer, a lens, a "read this in light of X") that an entire literate civilisation found indispensable.

**7.4 The ordering principle of the layers — three secular answers, and they conflict.**
- **Arabic madrasa stack:** by **commentary genealogy** — *matn* → *sharḥ* → *ḥāshiya* → *taqrīr*, each commenting on the layer above, and expressed as **separate authored works**, not page zones.
- **East Asian 注疏:** by **historical stratum** — base text + Han-era 注 + Tang-era 疏, three periods visible simultaneously.
- **European apparatus / variorum:** by **evidentiary function** — text, then variants (is the text right?), then commentary (what does it mean?).

None is universal; the fourth candidate (epistemic distance) came from an excluded source. §4.6 therefore recommends **scope**, which all four traditions share. Any kind-ordering must be a **setting**, not a default.

**7.5 How much may annotation crowd the host?** The glossed scholastic page lets the base text shrink to a fraction of the written area. Other traditions keep the host dominant. Make it a **per-shape property**, not a global constant.

**7.6 Is a collection of jottings a FINISHED FORM?** v1 framed this as East Asia (**yes** — **筆記** *bǐjì* and **随筆** *suihitsu*, "following the brush": discrete entries, each a **條** *tiáo* or **則** *zé* with its own head and scope, accumulated over a scholar's life and published *as a book* without ever being argued into a treatise; Sei Shōnagon's 枕草子, Kenkō's 徒然草) vs Arabic (**no** — a *bayāḍ* or *kunnāsh* is not a work and "dies with its owner unless someone redacts it into a book"), and left the fork open to the Boss.

**The European material resolves it.** Europe holds *both* positions at once, and states the discriminator: the **commonplace book** is a legitimate lifelong instrument, while the **waste book** is "by definition not the record of record." The difference between them is **one thing — whether entries are filed under a heading at the moment of capture.**

> **Structure applied at write time is what converts a jotting-heap into a work.** Not volume, not polish, not later synthesis.

Directly actionable, and it maps onto machinery Constellation has (a *locus communis* is a tag/MOC/heading applied at capture). It also aligns with **Rule 8, Write-Time Derivation** — the principle the codebase already applies to indexes turns out to be the historical answer for note collections too.

**7.7 Repair granularity.** Islamic codices commonly quinions (5 bifolia); the Latin West quaternions; 袋綴じ is stab-sewn single leaves, repairable one leaf at a time. **[contested]** — the standard generalisation, varies by region and period; not a universal rule.

## 8. Universals — safe to build once

1. **The base text is never altered.** All four traditions, every century, on secular texts. **Hard architectural invariant, not a default.**
2. **Layers are ranked graphically before they are read** — size, face, position, colour, and there is no fifth trick. One mechanism serves every kind and every script.
3. **Every layer signs itself.** Authorship and time are properties of **the layer**, not of the document.
4. **Erasable → drafting.** The wax tablet, the scraped parchment, the *musawwada*, the *Sudelbuch*. **No counter-example in four traditions.** This licenses an expiring/draft tier as a genuine cognitive instrument, and says the draft→permanent transition should be a real event producing a real change of shape.
5. **Extent is set by the container and is VISIBLE.** One column per bamboo slip; one roll-length per ancient "book"; one wax face; one heading per commonplace entry; 2–7 cm per palm leaf. Users could see how much room a thought was being given **before writing**.
6. **Additive-only correction.** Struck text stays legible; omissions are appended (*laḥaq*), never interpolated; the apparatus records variants rather than choosing silently; *ṣaḥḥ* defends a genuine reading against future correction. **And the palimpsest proves erasure is never total anyway.**
7. **Containers coexist; new ones take the traffic they suit.** Wax tablets stay in ordinary use for **over a millennium** after parchment and paper are available. Papyrus continues in the papal chancery. The roll survives for administrative records for centuries after losing the book. Slips and silk ran together. **Nobody was waiting for the next medium.** → Constellation's note shapes are a **coexisting set chosen per cognitive act**, never a migration path where a new shape deprecates an old one.
8. **A container's identity can live outside its content** — the title on the back of the first slips, the colour-coded binding cord, the shelf tag — **and can propagate into typography** (*khaṭṭ al-ruqʿa*).
9. **A logical address decoupled from the physical unit.** 巻 vs 冊 survived a total change of container. A note's citable identity must be independent of file, folder, library and layout.
10. **Purpose selects the container, and the DENIALS are as load-bearing as the affordances.** The roll-for-records / codex-for-books split is the sharpest secular case: one culture, two container families, simultaneously, because the purposes differed.

**One thing that is NOT universal** — and v1 would have listed it, having seen only manuscript traditions: *"annotations belong to the copy, not the work."* True in Arabic manuscript culture ("two copies carry different lives") and in European ("a glossed copy and an unglossed copy contain the IDENTICAL base text"). **It breaks under print**, where canonical layers belong to the *work* and appear in every copy. The property is an artefact of **manuscript production** — which is precisely the reproducibility argument of §3.5 and §4, and precisely why Constellation, a manuscript culture, gets the flowing-margin answer.

## 9. Cautions — specific temptations

1. **A designed multi-zone commentary frame as the qusasah UI.** The temptation survives the exclusion (the glossed law folio is just as seductive as what was removed). Refuse it for the §4.4 reasons: unbounded layers, no reproduction, an editable host, and a reader who will not pay a parsing cost a trained scholarly readership paid willingly over decades. **Take the architecture — zones own layers, base untouched, size ranks the layer, scope orders them. Refuse the picture.**
2. **The manicule ☞ and the critical signs as UI iconography.** The *obelos* and *asteriskos* are worth importing as a **class** (a zero-length "I doubt this" mark — §5). The **glyphs** are not: ☞ signals *oldness*, not *kind*. Same class as parchment texture.
3. **The palimpsest as a delete metaphor.** Irresistible and wrong. Its lesson is a **warning about delete semantics** — erasure on a valuable substrate is never total, and what one era considers disposable is what a later era wants — not a feature. Do not build "palimpsest view." Do let it decide whether Constellation's delete is a tombstone or a shred.
4. **The pecia.** Gorgeous system, wrong problem.
5. **Pre-allocating space per category.** The subtlest trap, because it *looks* like good structure. The commonplace book documents the failure: "a head that fills up cannot be extended without spilling." It will appear in any panel that reserves a fixed region per kind.
6. **Aged-paper texture, "parchment" backgrounds, torn-edge قصاصة graphics, faux-stitched 袋綴じ borders, rotated "pinned" sticky notes.** Carried forward from v1, reinforced: these signal *oldness*, not *kind*, and cost contrast and legibility across 15 languages. **Form-Aligns-To-Purpose.**
7. **Claiming قصاصة as a heritage term.** Carried forward unchanged — this is the BASIC RULE case. قصاصة is genuine Arabic — from **قصّ** "to cut," a clipping or cut-off scrap (Ibn Manẓūr, *Lisān al-ʿArab*) — but the Arabic study found **no evidence it functions as a technical term in classical codicology**. The attested terms are **تعليقة** (*taʿlīqa*, "a thing hung/attached" — where the etymology *is* the design spec), **حاشية**, **لَحَق**, **رُقْعَة**. Ship قصاصة as a **deliberate, well-motivated coinage in the spirit of the tradition**. Presenting it in 15 languages as "the historical Islamic term for marginalia" would be fabrication.
8. **Ownership/custody analogues** (*tamalluk*). Institutional mechanisms for a physical object passing between owners across centuries. One owner, one copy. The one portable idea — *an annotation can change the object's permissions rather than its content* — belongs in a permissions design, not a note shape.
9. **The scroll.** Its defining affordance is a denial, and it survives digitally only as the accidental default of a scrolling viewport.
10. **Importing dead containers because their names are beautiful.** **دِيوان** (*dīwān*), **سِجِلّ** (*sijill*), 龍鱗裝 (dragon-scale binding — **[contested]**, not even reliably reconstructed; the study says explicitly "do not build a design argument on this container"). A container earns a slot only if its six-dimension profile differs from every shape already shipped. **Naming is the last step, not the motivation.**

## 10. What the first synthesis got wrong or missed

### ★ 10.1 — THE FACTUAL CORRECTION: Qurʾanic mise-en-page generalised to all Arabic prose

v1 propagated the claim that the classical Arabic page may have had **no paragraph unit**, citing separation by rubricated heading, overlining and coloured circle rather than by indentation — and then **promoted it to an open question**: *"Did the classical Arabic manuscript page have a paragraph unit at all? … That has real consequences for what a قصاصة can be anchored to. Needs a codicologist."*

**The project owner — a native Arabic scholar and the authority here — has corrected this. Arabic writing DOES have a paragraph.**

What that evidence describes is the **Qurʾan's** mise-en-page: **آية** (*āya*) separators as rosettes and coloured circles, **سُورة** (*sūra*) headings, overlining — the layout of a specific **recitational and liturgical** text, whose divisions are units of *revelation and recitation*, not units of *prose argument*. It was wrongly generalised to all Arabic writing.

**Corrected position:** ordinary Arabic prose manuscripts — scholarly, literary, administrative — **paragraph normally**. The paragraph is available as an anchor unit in Arabic exactly as in every other tradition here. v1's derived worry about what a qusasah can anchor to is void.

**This error is the reason for the §2 exclusion.** It is not an isolated slip; it is the signature failure of using sacred layout as general design evidence, and the exclusion removes the class rather than patching the instance.

### ★ 10.2 — The same trap on the Hebrew side, and how the exclusion resolved it

I was asked to watch for the identical trap in the Hebrew material — a sacred-text layout generalised to a whole writing culture.

**Finding: the study does not commit the error in its framing** — it is careful and explicit that the scroll/codex split is deliberate and rule-governed, and that the scroll is the liturgical class specifically. Credit where due.

**But the trap was structurally unavoidable**, because **every Hebrew container surveyed is sacred or canonical-scholastic**, and Hebrew *secular* writing — responsa, contracts, community registers, the documentary Genizah — was never surveyed at all. A reader would come away believing Hebrew writing culture **is** its liturgical and scholastic apparatus. That is the same generalisation shape as §10.1, arrived at by **silence** rather than by assertion.

Under the exclusion this resolves cleanly rather than needing a patch: **the Hebrew track contributes no admissible design evidence to this survey, and its secular corpus is unsurveyed. Unknown for secular texts in this tradition** (§2.4.2).

*(I had drafted a note on Hebrew sacred-text paragraph units as a symmetric counter-example. It concerned an excluded container, so it is withdrawn rather than carried — recording the withdrawal here so a later reader knows it was considered and dropped by rule, not by oversight.)*

### 10.3 — It never saw the fixed-frame model, so its architecture conclusions were underdetermined

v1's own open questions admit it. Its conclusions **survive** — but the fixed-frame contrast is what reveals the actual trade (§4.3: *position carries type, or the annotation carries it*), and that trade is the design decision.

### 10.4 — It recommended the wrong anchor as "durable"

v1: "the storage model must be the Arabic one (a durable anchor surviving re-layout)" — the **عَطْفة**. But the *ʿaṭfa* is a **positional mark at a gap in a line**; it is durable against re-layout only because parchment does not reflow. The genuinely durable primitive is the **lemma** (§6.1).

### 10.5 — It declared a solved problem novel

On host editing: *"No tradition faced this. It is a genuinely novel problem and I have no historical guidance to offer."* Partly wrong. True that no tradition faced a host that edits itself *under* the annotation — but the primitive that solves it (an anchor carrying a copy of what it points at) is 2,200 years old, and the *apparatus criticus* is the worked example of pairing it with a positional address.

### 10.6 — It had no answer to overflow

The question the physical page was forced to solve. **Five mechanisms** are recoverable (§4.5); most from the two studies it never received.

### 10.7 — It proposed the wrong sixth dimension

It proposed *provenance-carrying*; the European study proposes **authority/evidentiary weight** explicitly (§3.2). Provenance is real but is an invariant applying to every shape equally, not a discriminating dimension (§3.5).

### 10.8 — It over-generalised on ceremony

Diagnosis right, inference too strong. "Cost and irreversibility are ~zero in digital, so ceremony has no physical source" misses **constitutive** ceremony — the commonplace book's single required heading — which never depended on physics and survives digitisation intact (§3.3).

### 10.9 — It missed the crystallisation lifecycle

Which is the hybrid, and which is historical rather than invented (§1, §4.6).

### 10.10 — It left resolvable forks open

The "is a jotting-collection a finished form?" fork is resolved by the European material: the discriminator is **structure at write time**, not culture (§7.6).

### 10.11 — Its headline proof was a sacred text

v1's "strongest historical argument that note shape is first-class" was the *muṣḥaf*/*qurʾān* pair. Excluded. **Two secular replacements are stronger for our purposes:**
- *volumen* and *codex* are both **named for the act that makes them** — "the container is named for its **manipulation**, not its material." A direct precedent for naming note shapes after **the cognitive move**, which is exactly what template-as-captured-move claims.
- **خط الرقعة** — a container's identity propagating into a script's name.

### What v1 got RIGHT and should be carried forward unchanged

All four traditions confirm these on secular evidence; nothing in the two new studies weakens any:

- The **headline** (synthetic container).
- **The matn is inviolable** — now confirmed in four traditions rather than two, and sourced here from *medical* manuscripts.
- **Graded attachment** (extended to four grades).
- **Generous margin as an engineering commitment made before layout.**
- **Addition over editing as a first-class intellectual act.** The Boss's use case — *"I wrote a note long ago, I revisited it, but I don't want to edit it"* — is the normal mode of a thousand years of serious secular scholarship, not a workaround.
- **Per-layer provenance.**
- **Kinds are not interchangeable**, and several are about process rather than meaning.
- **قصاصة is a coinage, not a heritage term.**
- The **"none — do not import"** verdicts: inscription, file/*milaff*, quire-as-note-shape, the scroll.

## 11. Open questions — need a specialist, not a search

1. **A measured secular fixed-frame page.** The single most important gap. This research attests that the glossed-page architecture serves the ***Corpus iuris civilis*** and **Aristotle**, but supplies **no measured zone description of a secular exemplar** — the detailed accounts were of an excluded text, and were themselves from secondary descriptions. **Needed:** a codicological study of named Roman-law shelfmarks, and confirmation of the **Accursius** attribution (Bologna, c. 1230s), which enters this document from the coordinator's brief and my general knowledge, **not from any study in this pass**.
2. **Does the §4.5 #4 inversion hold for secular glossed books?** My strongest overflow recommendation — *the base text is written last, or written to fit an already-measured commentary block* — carries a **double** evidence discount. Verify against measured studies before freezing a design on it.
3. **Non-Qurʾanic Arabic paragraph conventions.** With §10.1 settled, the legitimate remaining question is narrow: what marked paragraph divisions in scholarly, literary and administrative Arabic manuscripts, across which periods and regions? Needs Gacek's and Déroche's actual pages, not a web search.
4. **طُرَّة (*ṭurra*).** **[unverified]** in the Arabic study itself. Real word (edge, border, fringe); well attested in West African ʿajamī scholarship for marginal commentary; its technical scope in the Eastern Arabic tradition is **[unknown]**. Needs a direct read of Gacek's *Glossary of Technical Terms*. **Do not build UI vocabulary on it.**
5. **Does قصاصة have any technical usage a native codicologist would recognise?** "I searched and found nothing" is weaker than "a specialist confirms there is nothing," and this word may ship in 15 locales as a feature name. Cheap now, expensive later. Fallback with better pedigree: **تعليقة** (*taʿlīqa*).
6. **Is the samāʿ corpus secular or religious?** §2.3 keeps audition certificates on the strength of one adab case study whose genre I have not verified. If the corpus is overwhelmingly religious-transmission, the "engagement log" import weakens and should be re-based on collation marks alone.
7. **Hebrew secular writing — entirely unsurveyed.** Responsa, *shetarot*, *pinkasim*, documentary Genizah. If a secular Hebrew track is ever wanted, it is a fresh study, not a re-read.
8. **South Asian marginal commentary.** **[unknown]** — whether pothī manuscripts developed *any* systematic marginal-commentary layout. Bears on how strong the "secede" precedent is.
9. **Whether users sustain graded annotation kinds** or collapse them to "comment." No historical source can answer this — a professional scholarly culture with institutional incentives is not a sample of one person. **Ship a small set and instrument which are used.**
10. **Whether synthetic irreversibility reads as weight or as obstruction.** A prototype and a Boss test, not research.
11. **Does a qusasah travel with an exported or federated note?** In manuscript culture the answer is decisive — annotations belong to the **instance** (**نسخة** *nuskha*), never the work. In a single-user PKM work and copy collapse. **But §8's closing note shows that collapse is conditional on there being no reproduction layer** — and cUniverse federation, shared libraries, and export are exactly a reproduction layer. **Decide before the storage shape is fixed, not after.**
12. **Can the rendering stack express a script-conditional annotation zone** (outer/lower for Arabic, above for Sinographic, flanking for Latin) rather than a mirrored constant? Engineering unknown; not investigated.
