# Concept Paper — The Question Family: A Distinct Interrogative Home for `problematizes / answers`

**Advocate's brief for R4 (PJ-067, Living Link Relationship Model v2)**
**Option defended: OWN QUESTION FAMILY** — a distinct, typed *interrogative* family, **not** counted among the 8 cognitive acts.
**Status:** Advocacy / steelman. Decision pending Eisa's ruling.

## 0. The function in hand

> Working on: **the typed home of the directed note-to-note question-relation** (`problematizes / answers`) proposed in PJ-067's Tension family — deciding whether it joins "the 8," gets its own family, or stays untyped.

This paper argues that the question-relation belongs in **its own Question/interrogative family** — a *second seed-family* of the typed vocabulary, sibling to the Cognitive-Act family (the 8), the Structural family (the parent/TOC link, PJ-065), and the proposed Thematic family — **never a ninth peer of the 8, and never left to a generic untyped link.**

---

## 1. The horse — the one-sentence concept

> **A question is not a kind of answer, so it cannot live in the catalogue of answers — the eight cognitive acts are all *declarations* (X *supports* Y, X *contradicts* Y), and the directed interrogative edge (X *problematizes* Y) is the one relation in the whole vocabulary whose grammatical mood is interrogative, not declarative; therefore its right home is its own family, where its distinct mood is the family's defining contract.**

The eight acts answer "*what does X assert about Y?*" The question-relation answers a categorically different prompt: "*what does X **ask** of Y?*" Mixing the two in one set is a category error at the level of *grammatical mood* — the most fundamental cut a vocabulary of inquiry can make. Inquiry has exactly two moods: it **asks** and it **answers**. Constellation already has a magnificent home for the eight ways of answering. It has no typed home for the structured ways of asking. The Question family is that home.

---

## 2. Philosophy backing — the ratified paper *wants* this option

This is the decisive section, because the ratified Living Link Concept Paper (v1.0, RATIFIED 2026-05-30) does not merely *permit* a separate question family — **its load-bearing §4 logically entails one** the moment a directed question-relation is admitted at all.

### 2.1 §4 already drew the asker/answerer line — in ink, as ratified law

§4 ("The Question is the Key") is the philosophical spine of the entire document. Its exact, ratified words:

> "The eight typed names are all **declarations** — *X supports Y*, *X contradicts Y*. They record the **answers**. But the generative moment — the connection you *sense* but cannot yet name — is a **question**, not an assertion."

> "Typed links are therefore **how an inquiry resolves** — or honestly refuses to."

Read this as a definition, not a flourish. **The paper *defines* the 8 by their declarative mood** — "they record the answers." It then *defines the question as the complement* — "a question, not an assertion." This is not my framing imported from outside; it is the ratified text's own framing. The eight-membered set is *constituted* as the set of answer-acts. To insert an interrogative relation into that set is to break the very definition that §4 ratified: the set would no longer be "all declarations." **The NINTH option doesn't extend §4 — it falsifies §4's defining sentence.**

The Question family, by contrast, *honours* §4 exactly: it keeps "the 8" as the pure set of answer-acts §4 says they are, and gives the asking-mood its own typed surface — which §4 already told us is a *different kind of thing*.

### 2.2 The untyped link and the typed question are not rivals — they are two registers of the same mood

The obvious worry (the UNTYPED option's whole case) is: "§4 already says *the untyped link IS the question* — so a typed question-relation contradicts the paper." This reading collapses a distinction §4 itself draws, between the *felt, unnamed* question and the *resolved, named* relation.

§4's untyped link is the question in its **pre-articulate register**: "*I feel these belong together, but I'm still asking how.*" It is the question you **cannot yet name** — diffuse, whole-note, not yet pointed at a specific interrogative target. §4 calls it "the live edge of thinking."

But §4's closing move is precisely that inquiry *can* resolve: "Typed links are how an inquiry resolves — **or honestly refuses to**." The eight answer-acts are how it resolves into an *assertion*. The Question family is how a question **becomes articulate without yet becoming an answer** — the moment you can finally *name what you are asking* and *at which note you are aiming it*: "*Note X **problematizes** note Y — specifically, X raises the question of whether Y's mechanism survives this counter-case.*"

That is no longer the felt, unnamed pull of §4's untyped link. It is a **named, directed, articulate question** — and naming the articulate question is exactly the kind of resolution §4 blesses. The two are not rivals; they are the **pre-articulate** and **articulate** registers of the single interrogative mood §4 enshrines. The untyped link stays *exactly* what §4 made it (whole-note, unnamed). The Question family captures what §4 has no surface for yet: the *named, edge-level* question between two specific notes.

The Five Acts make this unmistakable. Constellation's arc is **Observation → Connection → Tension → Synthesis → Conviction**. §4's untyped link lives at **Connection** ("I sense these belong"). The eight answer-acts cluster at **Synthesis/Conviction** (the resolved stance, with `supersedes` as the terminus). **Tension** — the act of *putting a formulation in question* — is, by PJ-067's own admission, the thinnest family. The directed question-relation *is the canonical Tension move*: to problematize Y is to open Tension on it. The Question family gives Tension its own typed vocabulary, anchored to its own Act, instead of smuggling a Tension-move into the Synthesis catalogue (the 8) where it does not belong.

### 2.3 §6 and §7 are arguments *for* the family, read honestly

§6 says the eight are "a **vocabulary**, not a ranked hierarchy." §7 ratifies a *derived* order — "*derived*, not chosen" — whose entire logic is the **answer-arc**: "from the immediate epistemic stance (do I affirm or deny?) outward through explanation, abstraction, lineage, and composition, ending at succession — the terminus where inquiry concludes in a new settled stance."

Notice what that derivation *is*: a trajectory through **kinds of answering**, terminating in conviction. A question has **no place on that arc** — it is not a weaker affirm, not a stronger deny, not an explanation or a lineage. Forcing `problematizes` into the §7 order (the NINTH option's obligation) means either (a) inventing a rank for a thing the arc was never built to hold — destroying §7's "derived, not chosen" integrity — or (b) declaring it order-exempt, which is just admitting it isn't really one of the eight after all. Either way, the §7 arc *itself* testifies that the question doesn't belong in the set. **The cleanest respect for §7 is to leave it untouched and give the question its own family with its own (trivial, two-member) order: `problematizes · answers`.**

### 2.4 "Formulation, not Management" — the family *is* the mission

Constellation's founding refusal is **Knowledge Formulation, not Management**. A management tool stores answers. A *formulation* tool must be able to hold an **open, directed question as a first-class, persistent, queryable object** — because formulation *is* the work of carrying questions until they resolve. IBIS's deepest insight (next section) is exactly this: the *Issue* — the question — is the **primary unit**, and everything else hangs off it. A vocabulary that can type eight ways of answering but cannot type a single way of asking is, at its root, an answer-management vocabulary wearing a formulation badge. The Question family is the structural admission that **asking is a first-class cognitive operation**, not the absence of one. That is the mission, made typeable.

## 3. Prior-art backing — the field separates asking from answering at the type level

Eisa's own Working Agreement #5 demands the battle-tested pattern over the inventive one. Here the battle-tested pattern is overwhelmingly on the family's side: **the mature systems that take questions seriously give questions their own categorial home — distinct from the relations that answer them.**

### 3.1 IBIS (Rittel & Kunz, 1970s) — the Issue is its *own node kind*, and pro/con are a *different relation family*

IBIS does not make "raises-a-question" one option among supports/opposes. It makes the **Issue (the question) a distinct node type**, and *positions* respond to issues while *arguments* (pro/con) relate to positions. The asking layer and the answering layer are **architecturally separate strata** of the notation — questions are not a flavour of argument; arguments hang beneath questions. ([Issue-based information system — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Issue-based_information_system); [The IBIS Notation — Vithanco](https://vithanco.com/notations/IBIS/index/))

The ratified paper *already cites IBIS approvingly* (§2, §4: "the question as the primary unit") and §4 explicitly claims Constellation "arrived at IBIS's Issue independently." The Question family is simply *finishing that arrival*: IBIS keeps its issue-layer separate from its argument-layer; the family keeps the interrogative edge separate from the eight answer-acts. The NINTH option, by folding the question into the answer-set, is the *one* move IBIS — the cited authority — specifically refuses.

### 3.2 QUD (Roberts 1996) — the discourse is *structured by* questions distinct from the moves that answer them

In the QUD framework, "each sentence in discourse addresses a (often implicit) QUD either **by answering it, or by bringing up another question that can help** answering that QUD." ([Question under discussion — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Question_under_discussion); [Roberts, *Information Structure in Discourse*](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/0163853X.2017.1316038))

QUD draws the **exact** two-way cut the family proposes, as a formal primitive: a move either **answers** a question or **raises** one. These are not two species of one relation; they are the two fundamental discourse operations, and QUD's entire account of relevance, focus, and structure rests on keeping them distinct. Constellation's eight acts are the *answering* operations. `problematizes` is the *raising* operation. QUD says: type them separately or your model of inquiry can't even represent "this opens a sub-question" — which is half of all discourse. The family is QUD's two-operation cut, made into two relation-families.

### 3.3 KR / ontology practice — distinct relational *natures* get distinct families, not one flat list

WordNet and the wordnet tradition do not pour every relation into one undifferentiated bag. Meronymy (part-of), hyponymy (is-a), causation, antonymy, and entailment are organized as **distinct relation families with distinct formal properties** (transitivity, symmetry, inverses differ by family). ([On wordnets and relations — Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10579-013-9247-y); [Meronomy — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meronomy)) The governing principle of KR family-modeling is: **when a relation's logical nature differs in kind from the others, you give it its own family rather than flattening it into a sibling list** — because a flat list silently asserts that all members share a nature they don't.

The interrogative edge differs in kind along the most basic axis there is — **mood** (interrogative vs declarative) — and along its formal behaviour: an answer-act has a truth-apt assertion at both ends; a question-edge asserts nothing at its source, it *opens* something at its target. That is precisely the signal KR practice reads as "different family." PJ-067 itself already applied this rule twice — the **Structural** parent/TOC link (PJ-065) was ruled a *distinct kind* (structural vs epistemic), and the **Thematic** family is framed as "a second seed-family." The vocabulary is *already* multi-family by ratified precedent. The Question family is the third application of a rule Constellation has already adopted — not a new doctrine.

### 3.4 The convergent verdict

| Tradition | How it treats "the question" relative to answering relations |
|---|---|
| **IBIS** | Distinct node-kind (Issue); answering hangs *beneath* it |
| **QUD** | One of two primitive discourse operations: *raise* vs *answer* — kept distinct |
| **WordNet/KR** | Relations of different logical nature → different families, never one flat list |
| **Constellation (already)** | Structural family + Thematic family already separate from the 8 |

Four independent authorities, one verdict: **type the asking, but keep it in its own family.** The family option is the only one of the three that *agrees with all four*. NINTH agrees with none (all four separate questions from answers). UNTYPED agrees only with "don't over-engineer," which none of these four actually say about the *primary unit of inquiry*.

## 5. Rebuttal of the two strongest objections

### 5.1 The NINTH option's best case — "it's a cognitive act too; a separate family is bureaucratic hair-splitting"

**Their strongest form:** *Asking is cognition. Problematizing a note is as real a cognitive move as contradicting it — arguably the deepest one, since §4 itself calls the question "the key" and "the most alive link." So it deserves full peer status with the 8, not a side-pen. A separate family is taxonomic bureaucracy that fragments one vocabulary of inquiry into arbitrary bins.*

**Answer.** I concede the premise entirely — *asking is cognition, and §4 makes it the highest cognition.* That concession is exactly why NINTH fails. **Precisely because the question is the deepest move, it must not be demoted to a peer of the answers.** The eight are siblings *of each other* because they share a mood and a role: they are all the *resolutions* of inquiry, the answers. The question is not their sibling — it is their **parent**, the thing they all resolve. IBIS encodes this literally: the Issue is not one argument among arguments; arguments hang *beneath* it. §4 encodes it in prose: "Typed links are how an inquiry **resolves**" — resolves *what?* The question. To list the question *alongside* its own resolutions is to flatten a parent into its children — the one structural mistake that makes the deepest move look like just-another-option in a dropdown. The family doesn't demote the question to a side-pen; it **protects the question's distinct rank** by refusing to file it under the things that answer it. "Eight answer-acts + a question family" is not hair-splitting — it is the single most important joint in the whole vocabulary, the asker/answerer joint that §4, IBIS, and QUD all draw. Bureaucracy would be inventing *families with no difference*; here the difference is grammatical mood, the most load-bearing difference inquiry has.

### 5.2 The UNTYPED option's best case — "§4 already gave the question a home; a typed question-edge re-introduces the 'upgrade the untyped link' backwardness §4 banned"

**Their strongest form:** *§4's whole achievement was to stop treating the untyped link as "incomplete — upgrade it" and to honour it AS the question. A typed `problematizes` edge re-opens that wound: now the untyped link is "just the vague question," and the real question is the typed one — exactly the demotion §4 forbade. Ship only undermines/undercuts + qualifies (genuine answer-acts, children of contradicts), and let the untyped link keep being the question. One question-home, not two.*

**Answer.** This is the most principled objection, and it deserves a precise answer, because it is *right about the danger and wrong about the cure.* The danger — re-introducing "the untyped link is just an inferior draft of a typed one" — is real, and the family is explicitly built to **prevent** it, not cause it. The §4 untyped link and the Question family are **not the same object at different completeness levels**; they are **different objects** (§2.2): one is *whole-note and unnamed* ("I sense these belong"), the other is *edge-level, directed, and named* ("X raises *this* question of Y"). The untyped link is never "upgraded into" a `problematizes` edge — they coexist, the way §4's felt-question and the eight named-answers coexist. A note can carry a vague untyped pull *and* a sharp directed question to a different note, simultaneously, neither subordinate to the other.

The deeper reply: the UNTYPED option's "one question-home" is *not actually a home for the directed question at all* — it's a home for the **whole-note, undirected** question only. The thing R4 is deciding is whether the *directed note-to-note interrogative edge* gets typed. UNTYPED's answer is "no — let a generic untyped link serve." But a generic untyped link **cannot carry direction or interrogative force** — it can't distinguish "X asks something of Y" from "X merely associates with Y," and it can't express the *inverse* (`answers`) at all. So UNTYPED doesn't preserve §4's question-home; it *refuses to build the new home §4's logic calls for* and hopes the old, undirected home covers a case it structurally cannot. Meanwhile its actual additions — undermines/undercuts/qualifies — are all *answer-acts* (they assert "Y's warrant is weak"), which only deepens the catalogue of answering while leaving asking untyped. That is the answer-management bias §4 fought, re-entering through the back door. The family ships UNTYPED's good additions too (qualifies, undermines belong in the answer-set as contradicts-children) **and** builds the directed asking-home — strictly more faithful to §4, not less.

## 6. The cost I accept

A genuine advocate names the price. The Question family's real cost is **one more concept the user and the codebase must hold**: the vocabulary stops being "the 8 acts + untyped" and becomes "the 8 answer-acts + the Question family + the Structural family + the Thematic family + untyped." That is a richer ontology, and richness has a tax — more to document, more to teach in the Living-Links Guide, more surface in the type-picker UI, a genuine risk that a user stares at `problematizes` and `contradicts` and asks "which one?" (the asker/answerer line is sharp in theory and can blur in a hurried moment of capture).

I accept this price for three reasons. **First**, the multi-family tax is *already paid* — PJ-065's Structural family and PJ-067's Thematic family already committed Constellation to a multi-family vocabulary; the Question family adds the *most justified* family to a structure that already exists, not a new structural burden. **Second**, the alternative costs are worse and less reversible: NINTH permanently corrupts the clean "the 8 = the answers" definition that §4 ratified — a *conceptual* debt that can never be paid down, only rephrased around; UNTYPED permanently forecloses the directed-question capability, a *capability* debt that blocks the single most formulation-native surface the app could build (the live-questions worklist). A richer-vocabulary tax is an ongoing, payable cost; a corrupted core definition and a foreclosed capability are sunk losses. **Third**, the "which one?" UI risk is exactly where the family *helps*: because the families are visibly separate in the picker (a "Questions" group sitting apart from the "Cognitive acts" group, the way Structural and Thematic already sit apart), the user's choice is scaffolded by the grouping — "am I asking or answering?" is the first fork, and it is a fork humans make natively. The tax buys the most legible joint in the vocabulary.

---

## 7. Why this wins

The Question family wins because it is the only one of the three options that is simultaneously **faithful to the ratified text, aligned with the field, and additive in cost.** §4 *defines* the eight as "declarations… the answers" and the question as "not an assertion" — so the NINTH option falsifies the paper's own spine, and the family is the only reading that keeps §4's defining sentence true. IBIS, QUD, and WordNet/KR all draw the identical asker/answerer (or raise/answer) cut at the type level, and Constellation has *already* gone multi-family twice (Structural, Thematic) — so the family is the third application of a rule the project has adopted, not a novelty. And it ships the new capability — a typed, directed, queryable, persistent open question, the very engine of the Five-Acts Tension stage — while touching **zero** ratified orderings, **zero** instances of the load-bearing word "eight," and reusing the **exact** registry mechanism already built. It honours §4's untyped link by *leaving it exactly as ratified* (the whole-note, unnamed question) and building the directed, named question its own home beside it, so the two coexist as the pre-articulate and articulate registers of the single interrogative mood §4 enshrined. In a system whose founding refusal is *Formulation, not Management*, the move that gives **asking** its own first-class typed home — without disturbing the catalogue of **answering** — is not one option among three. It is the one option that lets the vocabulary finally say, in its own grammar, the thing the whole app exists to do: *carry the question.*

# Concept Paper — The Question Is a Ninth Cognitive Act

### A principled defence of seating **problematizes / answers** as a top-level peer of the eight

**Option advocated: NINTH.** The directed, note-to-note interrogative edge joins the canonical set as a true peer. "The eight cognitive acts" becomes **nine**. §6 and §7 are re-derived to seat it. The registry seed, CLAUDE.md, and the orientation are updated in the same migration.

**Status:** Advocacy draft — the strongest case that exists for this option, written to be argued against.

## 1. The horse — the one-sentence concept

> **The question is not a *kind* of connection between two notes — it is the connection that *generates all the others*; in a tool whose entire thesis is "knowledge is formed at the connections, driven by inquiry," the act that opens an inquiry between two named ideas cannot sit in a side-room while the eight acts that merely *answer* it occupy the center — it must stand at the head of the canonical vocabulary, because it is what the vocabulary is *for*.**

The carriage is the `problematizes / answers` edge. The horse is this: **inquiry is primary, and the eight named acts are its products.** If Constellation is an inquiry-driven formulation tool — and §4 says it is, in load-bearing italics — then the relation that *performs inquiry between two notes* belongs not adjacent to the canonical set but *inside* it, re-ordered to lead it.

---

## 2. Philosophy backing — the ratified paper already crowned the question; it just never gave it a typed throne

This is the decisive move, and it must be made carefully, because the FAMILY and UNTYPED camps will both claim §4 for themselves. Read §4 again, exactly:

> "The engine of the continuum is not accumulation; it is **inquiry**. Knowledge does not flow on its own — it is **driven by questioning**... The eight typed names are all **declarations**... They record the **answers**. But the generative moment... is a **question**, not an assertion."

The ratified paper draws a line straight through its own vocabulary: on one side, **eight declarations that record answers**; on the other, **the generative question**. And then it makes an explicit ranking claim — not a neutral one:

> "The most *alive* link in a library may be the one not yet answered."

Stop on that sentence. The paper's own thesis (§1) is that the link carries "the state of an understanding" and that weight is "how *alive*" a connection is. §4 then names the **most alive** category in the whole system — and it is the **question**. The eight acts are, by the paper's own framing, the *cooling* of inquiry into settled declaration. **The hottest thing in the library is the question, and the canonical vocabulary does not contain it.** That is the anomaly NINTH exists to correct.

Now the §2 lineage clinches it. The paper names IBIS as one of its four founding ancestors and quotes the operative fact directly:

> "**Horst Rittel's IBIS (1970s)** organized knowledge around **Issues — questions — as the primary unit**, with positions and pro/con arguments."

And §4 closes: *"This is IBIS's 'Issue' arrived at independently: the question as the primary unit."*

Here is the philosophical pivot the other two options must avoid and I must press: **the paper claims IBIS as an ancestor and the question as "the primary unit" — but then seats every typed relation as a child of the *answer*, and gives the primary unit no typed seat at all.** That is not faithfulness to IBIS. That is borrowing IBIS's authority while inverting its architecture. In IBIS the Issue is the *node everything hangs from*; positions answer it, arguments support/oppose the positions. Constellation's vocabulary, post-§7, is **eight position/argument acts with the Issue demoted to an untyped wisp.** NINTH is the only option that actually honors the ancestor the paper invokes: it puts the question back where IBIS put it — at the head of the structure, a first-class relation, not a felt absence.

**The Five Acts seal it.** Constellation's mission is the Five Acts of Knowledge Creation: **Observation → Connection → Tension → Synthesis → Conviction.** Where does a question live? It is not Connection (the felt pull — that's §4's untyped whole-note link). It is **Tension**: the directed moment where note X *interrogates* note Y, where "is this even right?" becomes an edge you can draw, traverse, and answer. The PJ-067 work itself identifies Tension and Synthesis as the **thinnest** families in the current vocabulary. The eight canonical acts give Tension exactly *one* member — `contradicts` — and §6 already strains to call it "**the engine**." But contradiction is only one mode of tension, and the *narrowest* one: it asserts "X is false." The question is the **broader, prior, more generative** tension: "what about X is in question?" To leave the engine of the Five Acts represented by a single declarative act (`contradicts`) while the *interrogative* act — the one that opens tension without yet committing to a verdict — is exiled to a side-family is to under-build the exact act the mission says is thinnest. **NINTH puts a second, more fundamental member into Tension and makes it canonical.**

## 3. Prior-art backing — every field that took the question seriously made it a peer, not a parenthesis

**IBIS (Kunz & Rittel, 1970).** The Issue is "the primary organizational atom... formulated as questions." Positions *answer* issues; arguments *support or oppose* positions. The architecture is not "eight relations plus an implicit question" — it is **the question as the load-bearing node type**, with everything else defined *relative to it*. Every modern descendant — Compendium, gIBIS, Discourse Graphs — preserves the Issue/Question as a first-class typed element, never as an untyped default. If Constellation claims IBIS lineage (it does, in §2), the faithful implementation makes the question a peer.

**QUD (Roberts, 1996).** This is the stronger card, because QUD is a *structural* theory, not just a node taxonomy. Roberts' claim: **every unit of discourse stands in one of exactly two relations to a Question Under Discussion — it either *answers* it, or it *raises a sub-question that helps answer* it.** Discourse is a *stack of questions*; the question is the spine, and assertions are *moves relative to questions*. Map that onto Constellation's vocabulary and the result is striking: **`answers` is one of the two primitive discourse relations in the most influential formal theory of how connected thought is structured.** It is not a niche edge. It is, in QUD, *the* edge — the thing that makes a sequence of notes a *line of inquiry* rather than a pile. A vocabulary that types `part-of` and `supersedes` as canonical acts but routes `answers` to a side-family has mis-ranked its primitives against the field's own ranking.

**Argumentation theory (Toulmin; Walton).** §2 already names Toulmin. Toulmin's schema is *answers to implicit questions*: the warrant answers "why does the data support the claim?", the rebuttal answers "under what conditions would it not?" Walton's argumentation schemes are explicitly **question-driven** — each scheme comes with a set of *critical questions* that probe it. In both, the question is not decoration around the argument; it is the **generative frame** the argument is an answer *to*. Argumentation theory consistently treats the question as ontologically prior to the claim. NINTH aligns the canonical set with that priority.

**KR / ontology practice on categorically-different relations.** Here the FAMILY camp will say: "when a relation is categorically different from the rest, ontologies put it in its own family — see how PJ-065 made the structural parent/TOC link a *distinct kind*, not a 9th act." I accept the precedent and turn it. The PJ-065 ruling was **structural vs epistemic** — the TOC link carries no cognitive act, it's filesystem-shaped, so it was correctly fenced off from the epistemic vocabulary. But the question is **the most epistemic relation there is.** It is not categorically *outside* the cognitive vocabulary the way a TOC link is; it is categorically *central to it*. The KR lesson is not "anything different goes in a side-family" — it is "**partition by the dimension that matters.**" The dimension that matters in Constellation's link vocabulary is *cognitive act*. The question is a cognitive act — the founding one. By the very logic that exiled the TOC link, the question is *admitted*: it is on the epistemic side of the structural/epistemic cut, and it is the act the whole inquiry arc is built to serve. Ontologies reserve top-level peer status for the relations a system is *about*. Constellation is about inquiry. The interrogative relation is a top-level peer.

## 4. Implementation & consequence fit — what it costs, what it gives

**The registry.** One new top-level typed relation: `problematizes` with inverse `answers` (directed, note-to-note). It seeds at the head of the canonical set, not under `contradicts`. Cost: one registry entry promoted from child-of-contradicts to peer. The undermines/undercuts/qualifies additions PJ-067 proposes still nest under `contradicts` as one-level children — NINTH does **not** touch those; it only elevates the *question* itself.

**The §7 canonical order — re-derived, and the re-derivation is a feature, not a tax.** The current arc is: *immediate stance → explanation → abstraction → lineage → composition → succession*, ending at `supersedes` because "inquiry concludes in a new settled stance." NINTH adds the **opening** the arc currently lacks. The re-derived arc:

> ### problematizes · supports · contradicts · causes · exemplifies · generalizes · derives-from · part-of · supersedes

The narrative becomes *complete*: **inquiry opens** (`problematizes` — the question is posed) → stance is taken (`supports · contradicts`) → mechanism, abstraction, lineage, composition → **inquiry closes** (`supersedes`). Today §7's arc starts *in medias res*, at the stance, with no account of where the stance came from. The paper itself says (§4) the declarations "record the answers" — but an answer with no question in the vocabulary is a reply to nobody. **Seating `problematizes` first gives the canonical order the bookend it is missing: the arc now runs question → answer → settlement, mirroring IBIS and QUD exactly.** The re-derivation is not damage to §7; it is §7 finally closing its own loop.

**CLAUDE.md / orientation.** "The 8 cognitive acts" becomes "the 9 cognitive acts" (with `associative` still the null default — 9 typed-semantic names, the same bookkeeping shape as today's "8 + associative"). This is a real edit across the canon. I do not minimize it. But it is a *one-time* truth-correction, and it makes every future statement of the vocabulary *self-consistent with the paper's own §4 thesis* — which today it is not.

**The untyped link survives, fully.** Critical, and I'll say it loudly because the UNTYPED camp's whole case rests on conflating two things: **NINTH does not touch the §4 untyped whole-note question.** Those are *different objects*. The untyped `[[wikilink]]` is the *felt, unnamed, single-note* pull — "I sense these belong together but can't yet say how." `problematizes` is the *named, directed, note-to-note* interrogative — "X specifically calls Y into question." One is pre-linguistic Connection (Act 2); the other is articulated Tension (Act 3). A library keeps both: the wisp you haven't resolved, *and* the sharp question you have drawn. NINTH **adds** the second without removing the first. The user who only ever draws untyped links loses nothing; the user ready to *name* a question gains a canonical home for it.

## 5. Rebuttal of the two strongest objections

### Objection A (the FAMILY camp): "The question is categorically different — interrogative, not declarative — so by the §6 definition ('each typed link is a declaration of a cognitive act') and the PJ-065 precedent, it belongs in its own *Question family*, not inside the eight. Promoting it forces a re-derivation of a freshly-ratified §7 for a relation that doesn't even share the eight's grammatical mood."

This is the best case against me, and it has real force. Two answers.

**First — "declarative mood" is a description of the *current* eight, not a *criterion* for membership.** §6 says each act is "a distinct cognitive act — a different way one idea can stand in relation to another." *Asking* is a way one idea stands in relation to another — the most generative way. The eight happen to be declarative because they are the *answers*; that they are all answers is precisely the gap §4 identifies, not a wall the question must stay behind. To say "the question can't join because the others are declarations" is to make the *symptom* of the gap into the *rule* that preserves it. The mood difference is real, but it is an argument that the set is **incomplete** (missing its interrogative member), not that the question belongs elsewhere.

**Second — the FAMILY solution recreates the exact pathology §4 diagnosed.** §4's complaint about the *old* treatment of the untyped link was: *"Calling it 'incomplete — upgrade it' is backwards."* The question was being treated as lesser. The FAMILY option does a subtler version of the same demotion: it grants the question a typed edge but *fences it out of the canonical center*, declaring by structure that it is "not one of the real acts." That is the question relegated to a side-room — honored in name, demoted in rank. If the question is "the primary unit" (§4) and "the most alive link" (§4), then a model that seats it *outside* the canonical eight is in tension with the paper's own valuation. NINTH is the only option whose *structure* matches the paper's *stated ranking*. On PJ-065: that precedent cuts structural-vs-epistemic; the question is on the epistemic side (see §3 above), so PJ-065 admits it rather than exiling it.

### Objection B (the UNTYPED camp): "§4 *already* solved this. 'The untyped link IS the question.' You don't need a typed interrogative edge at all — the generic untyped link, possibly directed, already carries the question. Adding a 9th act over-engineers the vocabulary, violates 'Constraint as Design' / 'when in doubt, do less,' and re-opens a ratified paper to introduce a type the paper explicitly said was unnecessary."

The strongest objection, because it wields Constellation's own minimalism rule against me. Three answers.

**First — §4's "untyped link is the question" is about a *whole-note* felt pull, not a *directed note-to-note* interrogative. They are not the same object** (see §4 of this paper). The untyped link answers "*these two feel related, how?*" — it does not encode "**X problematizes Y, and here is Z that answers it.**" QUD's whole structural power is *directionality*: a unit either *answers* a QUD or *raises a sub-question under* it. A symmetric, type-less wikilink cannot express "Z answers the question X raised about Y" — it has no slot for *which* is the question, *which* is the answer, or *that an answer exists*. The untyped link is a *mood* (open inquiry); `problematizes/answers` is a *structure* (an inquiry with direction and resolution-state). To say the untyped link already covers it is to claim a fog already covers what a vector expresses. It does not.

**Second — "do less" is not "do nothing where the mission says do most."** Constraint as Design forbids features that don't justify their existence. But the question's justification is the *strongest in the entire vocabulary*: it is the unit the paper calls primary, the act the Five Acts call the engine, the relation IBIS and QUD both make foundational. Minimalism rightly kills *decorative* additions; it does not kill the *load-bearing center*. Applying "do less" to the question is applying it at exactly the wrong joint — it would be like cutting the horse to lighten the carriage. The Concept-Before-Function rule asks for the clearest possible concept statement before building; the question's concept (§1's horse) is the *clearest and soundest in the set*. If anything earns its existence, it does.

**Third — leaving it untyped quietly enables the failure mode §5 warns against, inverted.** §5 guards against a "tyranny of doubt." But there's a symmetric failure: a **tyranny of premature answers**. If the only typed Tension act is `contradicts` (a verdict: "X is false"), the vocabulary nudges the user to *jump to a declaration* when the honest move is to *hold a question open between two named notes*. A typed `problematizes` lets the user say "X puts Y in question — I haven't decided" as a *first-class, traversable, surfaceable* relation, not a guilt-laden untyped backlog item. That is more faithful to §4's "the most alive link is the one not yet answered" than burying it in the generic untyped pile, where it is indistinguishable from every "I'll get to this" wisp. The typed question *protects* the open inquiry by giving it a name that says "this openness is deliberate."

## 6. The cost I accept — and why it is worth paying

I will not pretend NINTH is free. It costs the most of the three options, and honesty requires naming each price:

1. **It re-opens a freshly-ratified §6/§7.** Eisa signed off on "the eight" and the derived order on 2026-05-30. NINTH says that ratification was *correct for its moment but incomplete* — it canonized the answers and left the question in §4's prose rather than §6's table. Re-deriving a ratified order is the heaviest move on the board. **Why worth it:** the §7 arc is *demonstrably open at the front* — it begins at "immediate epistemic stance" with no account of what *raised* the stance. The re-derivation doesn't overturn §7; it *completes* it, giving it the question→answer→settlement bookend that IBIS and QUD both have and §7 currently lacks. A ratification that the paper's own §4 thesis contradicts is not a ratification to defend — it's one to finish.

2. **It changes the canon's headline number** — "the 8 cognitive acts" is a phrase that appears in CLAUDE.md, the orientation, the spec, and the code's `KNOWN_LINK_TYPES`. NINTH touches all of them. **Why worth it:** the number was always a *consequence* of the vocabulary, never a sacred constant. The migration that updates "8 → 9" is mechanical and one-time; the alternative is a canon that says "inquiry is primary" while its own enumerated vocabulary omits the interrogative act forever.

3. **It blurs the clean "every act is a declaration" story.** The eight had a tidy property: all declarative. NINTH breaks it — one act is interrogative. **Why worth it:** that tidiness was the *fingerprint of the gap*. A vocabulary of inquiry whose every member is an answer was never describing inquiry — it was describing inquiry's *aftermath*. Admitting one interrogative act trades a false symmetry for a true completeness. The set stops being "the eight settled moves" and becomes "the full arc from question to settlement" — which is what §1 promised the Living Link would carry all along.

The price is real. But every line of it is paid to fix the *same* anomaly: the paper's most load-bearing claim — *the question is the primary unit, the most alive link* — has no corresponding seat in the paper's canonical vocabulary. You cannot call something primary and then leave it out of the enumeration of what your system is made of. NINTH pays to end that contradiction.

---

## Why this wins

Constellation's founding sentence is not "knowledge is stored" — it is "**knowledge is formed at the connections, and the connection must be alive.**" The whole edifice rests on inquiry being primary: §4 says so in italics, the Five Acts say so by structure, and the paper names IBIS and QUD as ancestors precisely because *they made the question the primary unit.* Every other option treats that primacy as something to honor *around* the canonical set — a side-family beside it, or a fog of untyped links beneath it. Only NINTH treats it as what the paper actually said it was: **the primary unit, seated at the head of the vocabulary it generates.** The eight acts record how inquiries *end*; the ninth records how they *begin* — and a tool that types the endings but not the beginnings has built a vocabulary of conclusions for an app whose entire thesis is the openness that precedes them. Growing "the eight" to nine is not vocabulary bloat. It is the canonical set finally containing the act it was built to serve. The question is too important to relegate to a side-family precisely *because* it is the one relation every other relation is an answer to — and you do not put the thing your system is *about* in the margins of your system's own grammar.

# Defence C - UNTYPED (no new type - the untyped link is the question)

# Concept Paper — Defence of **NO NEW TYPE: The Untyped Link IS the Question**

### Decision R4 of PJ-067 (Relationship Model v2): where does `problematizes/answers` go?
### Advocated option: **UNTYPED.** Add only `undermines/undercuts` + `qualifies`. The canonical 8 stay frozen. The directed note-to-note interrogative edge is **not typed** — §4's untyped link already carries it.

*Status: steelman advocacy. Written for the R4 ruling.*

---

## 1. The Horse — the one-sentence concept

> **A question is not a relationship you assert between two notes — it is the felt pull you have not yet resolved into one; so the right grammar for the question-relation is the absence of a type, because the moment you give the question a typed edge you have already answered it.**

The untyped `[[wikilink]]` is not a *missing* type awaiting promotion. It is the **positive, designed expression of inquiry** — the one mark in the whole vocabulary that means "I sense a connection here and I am not yet ready to name what it is." That is *exactly* what a question is. To mint `problematizes/answers` as a typed edge is to build a carriage for a horse that is already pulling: the untyped link *is* the question, already, canonically, in §4.

---

## 2. Philosophy backing — the ratified paper argues FOR this option

### 2.1 §4 is not silent on this question — it *settles* it

The Living Link Concept Paper, §4 (load-bearing), states:

> "The eight typed names are all **declarations** — X supports Y, X contradicts Y. They record the **answers**. But the generative moment — the connection you sense but cannot yet name — is a **question, not an assertion**. **The untyped [[wikilink]] IS the question itself.**"

Read this precisely. The ratified paper has already drawn the line R4 asks us to draw — and drew it on the side of *no type*. The typed names are the answer-language; the untyped link is the question-language. This is not an accident of phrasing the v2 work is free to revise; it is the **central architectural identity** the paper assigns to the untyped link. The other two R4 options (FAMILY, NINTH) cannot be adopted without **overruling a load-bearing sentence of a ratified document** — and they should not pretend otherwise. The burden of proof sits with the side that wants to *un-ratify* §4, not with the side that wants to honour it.

### 2.2 "Knowledge Formulation, not Management" forbids premature formalization

Constellation's founding refusal is *Management*. Management catalogues; Formulation *inquires*. A management system would, of course, give questions a field — questions are data, data wants a column. But the whole point of §4 is that the generative moment of thought is **pre-categorical**. The instant a user must choose "is this a `problematizes` edge or an `answers` edge?" they have been pulled out of formulation and into filing. They must *resolve the ambiguity to record it* — which means the system can only record questions the user has **already half-answered enough to classify**. The questions that matter most in knowledge work — the vague, unnameable, productive ones — are precisely the ones a typed interrogative edge **cannot hold**, because they have no direction yet, no "this problematizes that," only "these two things will not leave each other alone."

The untyped link holds exactly those. A typed `problematizes/answers` cannot. So the typed edge doesn't *extend* the question-capacity of the system — it **narrows** it, by admitting only the subset of questions that have already curdled into a directed claim.

### 2.3 The Five Acts: Tension lives in the untyped link, not beside it

The Five Acts run Observation → Connection → **Tension** → Synthesis → Conviction. The v2 work rightly identifies Tension and Synthesis as the thinnest families. But the proposed cure misreads where Tension *lives*. **Connection in the Five Acts IS the untyped link** — the bare `[[wikilink]]` is the canonical Act-2 artefact. Tension is what that untyped connection *feels like before it resolves*: an unanswered pull. The question-relation is not a *sixth* thing sitting between Connection and Tension — it is the **phenomenology of an untyped link that has not yet found its type.** Constellation already renders that state: the open `[[link]]`. Adding `problematizes/answers` doesn't fill a gap in the Five Acts; it duplicates Act 2 in interrogative costume.

What Tension genuinely lacks is *answer-language for opposition* — typed ways to record a contradiction once you can name it. That is `undermines`, `undercuts`, `qualifies`. Those are real **declarations** (Toulmin rebuttal, defeater-attacks-inference) and they belong in the typed vocabulary as *children of `contradicts`*, exactly per the registry's one-level nesting. **This option ships them.** It declines only the one proposed addition that is not a declaration at all.

### 2.4 §6 and §7 stay clean — and that is a feature, not a side-effect

§6 says the 8 are "a vocabulary, not a ranked hierarchy." §7 ratifies the derived order Stance → Explanation → Abstraction → Lineage → Composition → Succession. Both describe a vocabulary of **acts of assertion** — every member answers something. A `problematizes` edge is categorically not an act of assertion; it is the *withholding* of one. Forcing it into §7's order is incoherent: where does "I am not asserting" rank among ranked assertions? The honest answer is *nowhere* — which is precisely why §4 put it outside the typed set in the first place. Keeping §7 frozen isn't conservatism; it's keeping the order **type-pure**: it ranks answers because answers are all it contains.

## 3. Prior-art backing — the field's best practice favours restraint here

### 3.1 IBIS/QUD make questions first-class — and that *helps* this option, correctly read

The FAMILY and NINTH advocates will cite [IBIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Issue-based_information_system) (Kunz & Rittel) and [QUD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Question_under_discussion) (Roberts 1996) as proof that questions must be *typed* first-class objects. Read the sources more carefully and they argue the opposite for Constellation's grammar.

In IBIS, the **Issue is a node, not an edge.** "Issues, stated as questions, are the heart of this method... the notation treats issues, positions/ideas, and arguments as equal, interconnected components" ([CogNexus, *IBIS: A Tool for All Reasons*](https://www.cognexus.org/IBIS-A_Tool_for_All_Reasons.pdf); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Issue-based_information_system)). The question is a *thing*, and Positions *respond to* it. **Constellation already has this exact shape** — and got it right at a deeper level than IBIS. In Constellation, a **note is the Issue** (a question can be a whole note: "Does X cause Y?"), and the untyped `[[link]]` is the response-relation that QUD calls "addressing." §4's "the untyped link IS the question" is Constellation's *node-level* IBIS — the Issue is first-class **as a note**, not smuggled in as a fifth edge-color.

QUD reinforces this. In Roberts' framework, "each sentence in discourse addresses a (often implicit) QUD either by answering it, or by bringing up another question that can help answer it" ([Annual Reviews](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-linguistics-011516-033952)). Note the word **implicit**. The QUD is *structurally present but rarely uttered as an explicit typed relation* — it is the silent organizing pull behind a sequence of moves. The untyped link is Constellation's implicit QUD: present, structuring, unspoken. Reifying it into an explicit `problematizes` edge is precisely the over-specification QUD theory shows is *unnecessary* — the relevance relation does its work without being named.

So the prior art the opponents lean on actually says: **questions organize discourse as implicit nodes/relevance, not as one more explicit typed predicate.** Constellation's untyped-link-as-question is the *more faithful* implementation of IBIS+QUD, not the deficient one.

### 3.2 KR practice: parsimony — don't reify a distinction the existing vocabulary already carries

Ontology engineering's mature guidance is to **avoid unnecessary reification**: "a parsimonious approach means existing classes and relations have proved to be largely sufficient... avoiding unnecessary commitments and new entity creation," and "each form of reification requires an extra relationship," trading expressivity against vocabulary complexity ([W3C Property Reification](https://www.w3.org/wiki/PropertyReificationVocabulary); [Ontogenesis](https://ontogenesis.knowledgeblog.org/993/)). The relevant test for minting a new relation type is **not** "is this distinction real?" (it is — questions differ from claims) but "does the *existing vocabulary already carry it*, and does naming it cost more than it gives?" Here the existing vocabulary — the untyped link, explicitly charged with the question-role in §4 — already carries it. Reifying `problematizes/answers` is the textbook "extra relationship" KR practice warns against: it pays in user-classification burden and registry/order complexity for expressivity the untyped link already supplies.

### 3.3 Zettelkasten: link *types are emergent, after-the-fact descriptions* — questions precede them

The Zettelkasten community converged on the same insight from practice: "link types... are descriptions (**after the fact**) of what happens to pieces of knowledge when you bring them together," and over-typing is an anti-pattern — "if tagging starts to replace linking, step back and reconnect" ([Zettelkasten Forum, *Link types*](https://forum.zettelkasten.de/discussion/2023/link-types); [*The Nature of Links*](https://forum.zettelkasten.de/discussion/2149/the-nature-of-links)). A type is a *retrospective* naming of a settled relationship. A question is, by definition, *not settled*. Typing a question is a category error in the literature's own terms: you cannot write the after-the-fact description of a relationship that is still open. The untyped link is the *before-the-fact* state — and that state is permanent for genuinely open questions. The field's hardest-won lesson is that "ambiguity in linking enables a more organic knowledge formulation process where connections and their meanings emerge through the act of knowledge work itself." That is §4, found independently, twice.

The other two options cross into §6/§7, CLAUDE.md's "8 cognitive acts" wording, the orientation, every locale, and the editor's type-rendering — a far larger blast radius for a distinction §4 already encodes.

## 5. Rebuttal of the two strongest objections

### Objection A (from NINTH / FAMILY): "The untyped whole-note question and the directed note-to-note question are genuinely different objects. §4 only covers the first. You're conflating them."

This is the opponents' best shot, and it deserves a forceful answer rather than a dodge.

**Concede the distinction; deny that it requires a type.** Yes — §4's untyped link is a felt pull on a *single* note's web of associations, while `problematizes` is a *directed edge between two named notes*. Those are different. But "different" does not entail "needs its own typed edge." Direction and target are properties the untyped link **already expresses structurally**: a `[[link]]` in note X pointing at note Y *is* a directed, X-to-Y, note-to-note relation. The wikilink has a source (the note it lives in) and a target (the note it names). The "directedness" the question-relation needs is already in the data model — it is the link's natural orientation. What the opponents are really asking for is not *direction* (have it) or *targeting* (have it) but a **label** — `problematizes` vs `answers` — and a label on an open question is the exact thing §2.2 and §3.3 show you cannot supply without closing the question.

And the inverse — "Z **answers** Y" — proves the point against typing. The moment a note *answers* Y, the user has the **full answer-vocabulary already**: it `supports` Y, or `contradicts` Y, or `supersedes` Y, or `derives-from` Y. "Answers" is not a missing type; it is the *genus of all eight existing types*. To answer is to assert one of the 8. So the `answers` half of the proposed pair is **redundant with the entire canonical set**, and the `problematizes` half is the untyped link doing its §4 job. Split cleanly, the proposed pair dissolves into "things we already have." That is the strongest evidence that R4's correct answer is UNTYPED.

### Objection B (from FAMILY): "Multi-family is already the trend — PJ-065's structural link and the thematic seed-family show the vocabulary is going multi-family anyway. A Question family is just the next honest seed. You're being a reactionary."

**Answer: precedent cuts the other way — and the trend is a reason for *more* discipline at the boundary, not less.** PJ-065's structural/TOC link was ruled a distinct kind for a principled reason: it is **structural, not epistemic** — it carves at a *different joint of reality* (document architecture vs. knowledge claims). The thematic family (`used-for`, `precedes`, `near`) is likewise a coherent *new joint*: pragmatic/temporal/topical proximity, none of which the epistemic 8 or the untyped link express. Each new family earns its place by carving **a joint nothing else carves.**

The question-relation **fails that test** precisely because §4 already carves its joint — the untyped link *is* the question joint. A "Question family" would not be a new joint; it would be a **second name for an existing one.** The multi-family trend is healthy *because each family is non-redundant*; admitting a redundant family is how a clean multi-family vocabulary rots into a synonym pile (the Zettelkasten over-typing anti-pattern, §3.3). Being disciplined about *this* boundary is what keeps the *real* new families (structural, thematic) credible. Far from reactionary, defending §4 here is what **protects** the multi-family direction from its own dilution.

---

## 6. The cost I accept — and why it's worth paying

**The honest price:** users who *want* an explicit, queryable, visually-distinct "this note raises a question about that note" edge will not get one. Some genuinely directed problematizations — "this experiment **problematizes** that theory," meaning specifically *casts doubt without yet refuting* — will be recorded either as an untyped `[[link]]` (losing the explicit interrogative flavour in search/Sight) or, once the doubt sharpens, as `undermines`/`undercuts` (which this option *does* ship). There will be a thin band of "doubt that is directed but not yet a defeater" that the typed vocabulary cannot name. The user feels it as the gap between an open `[[link]]` and a hard `contradicts`.

**Why the price is worth it.** That thin band is *supposed* to be unnamed — it is Tension itself, the live Act-3 state before it resolves toward Synthesis. A system that lets you **file** your tensions invites you to stop **feeling** them; a labelled question is a question you have started to answer, and the most valuable questions are the ones you must sit with unlabelled. The cost of UNTYPED is that Constellation refuses to let you prematurely close an open question by typing it. That refusal is not a deficiency — it is the franchise. Every other option buys queryability at the price of **inviting the user to resolve inquiry into filing**, which is the one thing "Formulation, not Management" exists to forbid. I accept losing the explicit edge to keep the open question *open*.

I also accept that this option does the *least* of the three — and I claim that as a virtue. Constraint as Design: "every feature must justify its existence." `problematizes/answers` cannot justify its existence against an incumbent (§4) that already does its job; `undermines/undercuts/qualifies` can, and ship. The smallest diff that honours §4 and thickens Tension with real declarations is the correct diff.

---

## 7. Why this wins

This option wins because it is the only one of the three that does not have to **overrule a ratified, load-bearing sentence** to proceed — §4 already declares "the untyped link IS the question," and the field's own authorities agree with §4: IBIS makes the Issue a *node* (Constellation's note), QUD keeps the question *implicit*, KR practice forbids reifying a distinction the existing vocabulary carries, and Zettelkasten proves link-types are *after-the-fact* names that an open question can never receive. The `answers` half of the proposed pair is just the genus of the existing 8; the `problematizes` half is just the untyped link doing its job — so the new type adds **nothing the system lacks** while taxing the user to classify what should stay an open pull, bloating §6/§7 with a non-assertion that cannot be ranked among assertions, and diluting the multi-family vocabulary that stays credible only while every family carves its own joint. Ship `undermines`, `undercuts`, `qualifies` — the real, missing *declarations* of Tension — freeze the canonical 8 and the §7 order, and let the untyped `[[link]]` remain what it was ratified to be: **the question itself.** Less vocabulary, more inquiry. That is Constellation.

# §F. Findings (neutral) - the decisive cruxes, for the Boss to rule

No winner is picked here; these are the load-bearing disagreements the three defences expose. Rule the cruxes and the option falls out.

## The five cruxes

**Crux 1 - Is the *directed note-to-note* question a different OBJECT than §4's *untyped whole-note* question?**
- FAMILY / NINTH: **yes, distinct.** §4's untyped link is a felt, whole-note, *unnamed* pull ("I sense A and B belong"); `problematizes` is a *named, directed, two-note* interrogative ("X specifically calls Y into question").
- UNTYPED: concedes they differ, but argues the wikilink **already supplies direction + target** (a `[[link]]` in X pointing at Y *is* directed X->Y); what's really being added is only a **label**, and labeling an open question begins to close it.
- *Boss rules:* is the missing thing a genuine new object (a typed, queryable, surfaceable directed question), or merely a premature label on an open pull?

**Crux 2 - Is `answers` a real new relation, or redundant with the 8? (the sharpest single clash)**
- UNTYPED: **`answers` is the *genus* of the 8** - the moment a note answers Y it *supports / contradicts / supersedes / derives-from* Y; so `answers` adds nothing, and `problematizes` is just the untyped link. The pair dissolves into "things we already have."
- FAMILY / NINTH: **`answers` is the Q&A *structural* relation** (QUD: a move that *resolves a question*), distinct from the *stance* it takes. "Z answers the question Y raised" encodes *that an open question is being resolved* - which `supports`/`contradicts` do not say.
- *Boss rules:* this is the pivot. If `answers` is merely the genus of the 8, the case for any new type weakens (-> UNTYPED). If it is a distinct resolution relation, FAMILY/NINTH gain.

**Crux 3 - Whose reading of §4 governs?** (all three claim it)
- UNTYPED: §4 **settles** it - "the untyped link IS the question" -> no type; the other two must *overrule a ratified, load-bearing sentence*.
- FAMILY: §4 **defines** the 8 as "all declarations / answers" -> seating a question *inside* the 8 falsifies that definition; the question needs its own home (and §4 blesses *naming* an articulate question as a kind of resolution).
- NINTH: §4 **crowns** the question ("the primary unit," "the most alive link") -> you cannot call it primary and omit it from the enumerated vocabulary; seat it at the head.

**Crux 4 - The multi-family precedent (PJ-065 structural + the thematic family).**
- FAMILY: the vocabulary is *already* multi-family -> a Question family is the next honest seed.
- UNTYPED: each family must carve a **new joint**; §4 already carves the question joint -> a Question family is **redundant** (a synonym, not a new joint).
- NINTH: the question is **epistemic-central** (unlike PJ-065's structural link, which is non-epistemic) -> it is admitted *into* the epistemic set, not fenced into a side-family.

**Crux 5 - Cost / blast radius.**

|---|---|---|---|
| Canonical "8" | **frozen** | frozen (8 stay; a new family beside them) | **grows to 9** |
| §7 derived order | untouched | untouched | **re-derived** (seats `problematizes` first) |

| `undermines`/`undercuts`/`qualifies` | **ships** (children of `contradicts`) | ships | ships |

## The decision tree (the findings, distilled)

1. **Do you want a typed, queryable, directed "X problematizes Y / Z answers it" relation at all?**
   - **No** - the untyped link (+ note content / a "?" note) suffices, and typing an open question begins to close it -> **UNTYPED**. *(Ships `undermines`/`undercuts`/`qualifies`; freezes everything else.)*
   - **Yes** -> go to 2.
2. **Is the question epistemically CENTRAL - the primary unit that should *lead* the vocabulary - or categorically DIFFERENT in MOOD (it asks; the 8 answer)?**
   - **Central**, worth re-deriving the ratified §7 to seat it first -> **NINTH**.
   - **Different in mood**, keep the 8 pure and give asking its own home -> **FAMILY**.
