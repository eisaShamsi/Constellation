# Universal Epistemic Content Taxonomy
# تصنيف المحتوى المعرفي الكوني

A formal hierarchical reference distilled from the comparative civilizational essay on *Epistemic Content* (المحتوى المعرفي). It organizes the entire family of cognitive objects — idea, fact, data, knowledge, and their kin — into a single structured taxonomy with bilingual terminology, cross-civilizational concordance, and a complementary axis of sources/means.

---

## 0. Preface (تمهيد)

The taxonomy that follows is a **scholarly construction** synthesized from the convergent structure observed across five major epistemological traditions: Greek and Western analytic; Arabic-Islamic Sunni *kalām* / *uṣūl al-fiqh* / *falsafa*; Indian *pramāṇa-vāda*; classical Chinese (Mohist Canon, Confucian, Daoist, Neo-Confucian); and Persian-Islamic *Ishrāqī* (Illuminationism), with supplementary input from Jewish, Tibetan Buddhist, African, and Mesoamerican philosophy.

It is **not** the doctrine of any single school. It is a comparative scaffold: each node carries its native equivalents across the surveyed traditions, so the taxonomy can be read from any civilizational vantage point and still hold together.

The taxonomy has **two orthogonal axes**:

- **Vertical axis (Part I)** — the five primary branches of epistemic content as identified in §VII of the paper.
- **Horizontal axis (Part III)** — the sources or means by which content of each type is generated, derived from the convergent treatment of *masādir al-maʿrifah* (مصادر المعرفة) and *pramāṇa* (प्रमाण).

A single epistemic item is located by both axes: *what kind of content is it* (vertical) and *what source produced it* (horizontal). For example, "the cat is on the mat" is a **proposition** (vertical: §3.3) produced by **perception** (horizontal: §S1).

---

## I. Design Principles (مبادئ التصنيف)

The taxonomy obeys four principles, in order of priority:

1. **Civilizational neutrality** (الحياد الحضاري) — no single tradition's vocabulary is privileged as the canonical layer; every node carries paired equivalents.
2. **Convergence-anchored structure** (بِنية مُؤسَّسة على التقاطُع) — only categories independently identified by at least three of the five major traditions are admitted as primary branches.
3. **Two-axis representation** (تمثيل ثنائي المحاور) — content types and content sources are kept distinct, since conflating them is the most common analytic error in single-tradition taxonomies.
4. **Pluralism on contested categories** (التعدُّدية في المسائل الخلافية) — where traditions disagree (e.g., on revelation as a source, or non-apprehension as a means of knowledge), both positions are recorded with attribution.

---

## II. The Vertical Axis: Five Primary Branches of Epistemic Content

```
ROOT — Epistemic Content (المحتوى المعرفي)
│
├── 1. Sensory Inputs            (المُدخَلات الحسية)
├── 2. Symbolic Entities         (الكيانات الرمزية)
├── 3. Semantic Contents         (المحتويات الدلالية)
├── 4. Epistemic States          (الحالات المعرفية)
└── 5. Higher-Order Constructs   (التركيبات العُليا)
```

### Branch 1 — Sensory Inputs (المُدخَلات الحسية)

*Pre-conceptual inputs to cognition. Items at this level have not yet been interpreted as "about" anything.*

| # | English | Arabic | Definition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Signal | إشارة | A pattern of energy or change carrying potential information; pre-receptive. |
| 1.2 | Stimulus | مُنبِّه | Anything triggering a sensory response in a perceiver. |
| 1.3 | Sense-datum | مُعطى حِسِّي | The immediate content of a perceptual event before conceptual interpretation; modern equivalents include "qualia." |
| 1.4 | Percept | مُدرَك حِسِّي | A sense-datum that has been organized into a recognizable object of perception. |

**Cross-civilizational anchors:**
αἴσθησις (Greek *aisthēsis*) · الحِسُّ (Sunni *al-ḥiss*) · प्रत्यक्ष (Indian *pratyakṣa* — qua content, not source) · 親知 (Mohist *qīn zhī* — direct acquaintance).

---

### Branch 2 — Symbolic Entities (الكيانات الرمزية)

*Encoded representations: the syntactic level. Symbols stand for things by convention, natural connection, or formal rule.*

| # | English | Arabic | Definition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Sign | علامة | Anything that stands for something else by natural connection (smoke for fire) or convention (red for stop). |
| 2.2 | Symbol | رمز | A sign whose representational power rests entirely on convention or shared rule. |
| 2.3 | Datum / Data | بيان / بيانات | A recorded symbol or set of symbols representing observed properties; the lowest level of the Data-Information-Knowledge-Wisdom (DIKW) hierarchy. |
| 2.4 | Code | شِفرة / ترميز | A systematic mapping between symbol-sets, enabling encoding and decoding. |
| 2.5 | Inscription | تَدوين | A physical token of a symbol — written, recorded, stored. |

**Cross-civilizational anchors:**
λεκτόν (Stoic *lekton* — expressional aspect) · الدَّلَالَة (Sunni *al-dalālah* — the sign-relation) · 名 (Mohist *míng* — the name as opposed to the reality) · *śabda* as utterance (शब्द) before being a source of knowledge.

---

### Branch 3 — Semantic Contents (المحتويات الدلالية)

*Items with meaning attached: the dimension of "aboutness." This is the level at which the four terms of your original question — idea, fact, data (when interpreted), and knowledge (when stripped of its epistemic-state component) — primarily live.*

| # | English | Arabic | Definition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Concept | مفهوم | A single mental grasp of a meaning; *taṣawwur* in classical Arabic-Islamic terminology. |
| 3.2 | Idea | فكرة | A broader mental construct that may combine multiple concepts; the most general semantic-content term. |
| 3.3 | Proposition | قضية | A declarative content capable of bearing a truth-value; *qaḍiyyah* in Arabic-Islamic logic, *judgment* in Kantian terminology. |
| 3.4 | Information | معلومات | Data invested with meaning by context; the second tier of the DIKW hierarchy. |
| 3.5 | Fact | واقعة / حقيقة | A proposition whose correspondence to reality has been verified; truth-bearing semantic content. |
| 3.6 | Meaning | معنى | The semantic relation that ties a sign or symbol to what it represents. |
| 3.7 | Definition | تعريف / حدّ | A statement specifying the necessary and sufficient marks of a concept; *ḥadd* in Arabic-Islamic logic. |

**Cross-civilizational anchors:**
λεκτόν (Stoic *lekton* — content aspect) · تَصوُّر / قضية (Sunni *taṣawwur* / *qaḍiyyah*) · ज्ञान (Indian *jñāna* — cognition in the broadest semantic sense) · 實 (Mohist *shí* — reality as paired with the *míng* / name).

### Branch 4 — Epistemic States (الحالات المعرفية)

*A graded scale of mental stances toward a proposition, ordered by degree of justification or evidential weight. This branch is the most universally recognized across traditions and the most precisely calibrated.*

The scale runs from negative (defective) states through neutrality to positive (warranted) states:

```
NEGATIVE                NEUTRAL              POSITIVE
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Compound ignorance  →  Simple ignorance  →  Doubt  →  Opinion  →
Belief  →  Knowledge  →  Certainty
```

| # | English | Arabic | Definition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | Compound ignorance | جَهْل مُرَكَّب | Believing falsehood to be true; ignorance that is unaware of itself. The most defective state. |
| 4.2 | Simple ignorance | جَهْل بَسِيط | Knowing that one does not know; absence of cognition with self-awareness of the absence. |
| 4.3 | Illusion / false supposition | وَهْم | A weak inclination toward a position contrary to reality. |
| 4.4 | Doubt | شَكّ | Equipoise between two contradictory propositions; suspension of judgment. |
| 4.5 | Opinion / preponderant probable | ظَنّ | Rational preference for one of two contradictories without certainty; the standard epistemic status of much classical *fiqh* and inductive science. |
| 4.6 | Belief | اعتقاد / تصديق | Settled affirmation of a proposition; *taṣdīq* qua mental act of assent. |
| 4.7 | Knowledge | علم / معرفة | Justified true belief reliably connected to its truth-maker; the central category. |
| 4.8 | Certainty | يَقين | Knowledge whose contradictory is inconceivable; the apex state, especially as articulated by al-Ghazālī. |

**Cross-civilizational anchors:**
δόξα → ἐπιστήμη (Greek *doxa* / *epistēmē*) · شك → ظن → علم → يقين (Sunni *shakk* / *ẓann* / *ʿilm* / *yaqīn*) · प्रमा / अप्रमा (Indian *prama* / *aprama*, with *saṃśaya* / *viparyaya* / *tarka* as defective states) · 知 (Mohist *zhī* graded as direct, inferential, or testimonial).

**Modal sub-classification of knowledge (4.7):**

- **Acquired knowledge** (العلم الحُصولي, *al-ʿilm al-ḥuṣūlī*) — knowledge through a mental representation that stands for its object.
- **Presential knowledge** (العلم الحُضوري, *al-ʿilm al-ḥuḍūrī*) — direct, non-representational acquaintance, as in self-knowledge; the Suhrawardian distinction, paralleled by Russell's "knowledge by acquaintance" and certain Buddhist and Daoist registers.
- **Propositional knowledge** (knowledge-*that*) — knowing that *p* is the case.
- **Procedural knowledge** (knowledge-*how*) — knowing how to perform an action; *technē* in Greek, *ʿilm taṭbīqī* in modern Arabic.
- **Acquaintance knowledge** (knowledge-*of*) — knowing a particular person, place, or thing directly.
- **Tacit knowledge** (المعرفة الضِّمنية) — knowledge held but not fully articulable, after Polanyi (1958).

### Branch 5 — Higher-Order Constructs (التركيبات العُليا)

*Organized epistemic structures built from elements of the lower branches. These are the architectures of mature understanding.*

| # | English | Arabic | Definition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | Hypothesis | فَرَضِيَّة | A tentative proposition advanced for testing; epistemic status *ẓann* awaiting upgrade or refutation. |
| 5.2 | Theory | نَظَرِيَّة | An integrated explanatory system of propositions covering a domain. |
| 5.3 | Model | نَموذَج | A simplified structural representation of a system, used for reasoning or prediction. |
| 5.4 | Law | قانون | A universal regularity, usually formalized; in classical Arabic-Islamic usage, distinguished from *sharīʿah* (شريعة) divine law and *qānūn waḍʿī* (قانون وضعي) positive law. |
| 5.5 | Doctrine | مَذهَب | A coordinated system of beliefs adopted by a school. |
| 5.6 | Insight | بَصيرة | Direct, often non-discursive grasp of a deep pattern; *prajñā* (प्रज्ञा) in Indian/Buddhist traditions. |
| 5.7 | Wisdom | حِكمة | Integrated judgment about how and when to apply knowledge; the apex of the DIKW hierarchy and a recognized category in every surveyed tradition. |
| 5.8 | Worldview | رؤية كَونية | A comprehensive integration of theories, values, and orientations into a coherent stance toward reality. |

**Cross-civilizational anchors:**
σοφία / φρόνησις / τέχνη (Greek *sophia* / *phronēsis* / *technē*) · حكمة / بصيرة (Sunni *ḥikmah* / *baṣīrah*) · प्रज्ञा / विद्या (Indian *prajñā* / *vidyā*) · 知行合一 (Wáng Yángmíng's unity of knowing and acting).

## III. The Horizontal Axis: Sources / Means of Knowledge (مصادر المعرفة)

*The convergent treatment of how content of any of the above types is generated. Drawn primarily from the Indian* pramāṇa *framework, Sunni* masādir al-maʿrifah*, and the Mohist three-source typology, with refinements from other traditions.*

| # | English | Arabic | Sanskrit | Definition | Recognized by |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Perception / Sensation | الحِسّ | प्रत्यक्ष *pratyakṣa* | Direct sensory contact with an object. | All major traditions |
| S2 | Inference / Reason | العَقل | अनुमान *anumāna* | Derivation of conclusion from premises. | All major traditions |
| S3 | Testimony | الخَبَر | शब्द *śabda* | Reliable verbal report from another knower. | Sunni, Indian (*Nyāya*, *Mīmāṃsā*), Mohist, Confucian; rejected as primary by Cārvāka, Dignāga's Buddhists, Greek Pyrrhonists |
| S4 | Mass-transmission | التَّواتُر | (no exact equivalent) | Convergent reports by independent witnesses too numerous to collude on falsehood; yields necessary knowledge. | Distinctive to Sunni *uṣūl al-fiqh* |
| S5 | Comparison / Analogy | القياس | उपमान *upamāna* | Knowledge of an object via its similarity to a known object. | *Nyāya*, *Mīmāṃsā*, *Vedānta*, Sunni jurisprudence (*qiyās*); subsumed under inference by Buddhists |
| S6 | Postulation / Inference to Best Explanation (IBE) | الاستنباط الافتراضي | अर्थापत्ति *arthāpatti* | Inference of an unobserved fact required to explain an observed one. | *Mīmāṃsā*, *Vedānta*, modern philosophy of science |
| S7 | Non-apprehension | عَدَم الإدراك | अनुपलब्धि *anupalabdhi* | Knowledge of absence via the absence of perception. | *Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsā*, *Advaita Vedānta* |
| S8 | Memory | الذاكرة | स्मृति *smṛti* | Recall of previously cognized content. | All traditions, though some restrict its status as primary |
| S9 | Innate disposition / Intuition | الفِطرة / الحَدْس | (no single equivalent) | Pre-experiential cognitive endowment yielding direct knowledge of certain truths. | Sunni (*fiṭrah*), Greek (*nous* as intuition of first principles), Confucian (*liángzhī*), Mencian |
| S10 | Inspiration / Mystical Apprehension | الإلهام / الكَشْف | (varies) | Non-discursive apprehension claimed in mystical and spiritual traditions; status as a public source contested. | Some Sunni Sufi-influenced authors, Daoist, certain Buddhist schools; rejected by Mohists and most analytic philosophers |
| S11 | Revelation | الوحي | (cf. *āgama*, आगम) | Communication from a divine source, transmitted through prophets and scripture. | Sunni Islam, Judaism, *Mīmāṃsā* (Vedas), Christianity; rejected by Cārvāka and most secular analytic epistemology |

**Note on the IBE acronym:** "Inference to the Best Explanation" is the standard contemporary name for *arthāpatti*-style reasoning, popularized in the philosophy of science by Gilbert Harman in 1965.

## V. Notes on Contested Categories (مسائل خلافية)

Three controversies in particular shape any cross-civilizational use of this taxonomy:

**1. The status of testimony (S3) and revelation (S11).** The Indian *Cārvāka*, the Buddhist Dignāga school, and the Greek Pyrrhonists treat testimony as derivative — reducible to inference at best. The Sunni *uṣūlī* tradition, the Indian *Mīmāṃsā* and *Nyāya* schools, the Mohists, and most religious epistemologies treat it as a primary source. The taxonomy lists both because both positions are well-developed and well-defended; users of the taxonomy should note their tradition's stance.

**2. The status of non-apprehension (S7).** Accepted by *Bhāṭṭa Mīmāṃsā* and *Advaita Vedānta* as an irreducible source of knowledge of absence. *Prabhākara Mīmāṃsā* and Sunni *uṣūl* generally subsume it under perception or inference. Modern epistemology is largely silent on it.

**3. The status of presential knowledge (4.7 modal sub-classification).** The Suhrawardian *Ishrāqī* tradition, certain Buddhist registers, and Daoist epistemology treat non-representational presence (الحضور, *al-ḥuḍūr*) as a distinct mode of knowing. Mainstream Sunni *kalām* and most analytic epistemology treat all knowledge as representational — though Bertrand Russell's "knowledge by acquaintance" (1912) revives a structurally similar distinction.

**4. The relation of knowledge to action.** Greek *epistēmē*, mainstream Sunni *ʿilm*, and modern analytic propositional knowledge are all primarily contemplative-representational. Confucian *zhī* (especially in Wáng Yángmíng's *zhī xíng hé yī*), the Mohist conception, and the Sunni notion of *al-ʿilm al-nāfiʿ* (العلم النافع, beneficial knowledge) treat knowledge as completed only in right action. The taxonomy is silent on this — a complete account would add a third axis (active/contemplative), which is left for future work.
