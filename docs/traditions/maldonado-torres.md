# Maldonado-Torres

**Family**: Latin American decolonial · **Shape**: rings (3 concentric tiers)

## Hero metaphor

The dome divides into **three concentric rings**, each a tier of the
*coloniality* concept as Nelson Maldonado-Torres extended it from
Quijano and Mignolo:

- **Coloniality of power** — outermost ring. The structural matrix of
  global racial-capitalist organization (Quijano's foundational
  contribution).
- **Coloniality of knowledge** — middle ring. The eurocentric
  hegemony of categories, methodologies, and disciplinary forms
  (Mignolo's extension).
- **Coloniality of being** — innermost ring. The ontological
  dimension — the way coloniality reaches into selfhood and the
  experience of personhood (Maldonado-Torres's own contribution,
  in dialogue with Heideggerian fundamental ontology).

The Russian-doll geometry encodes a nesting argument: power is the
outer condition; knowledge is the medium through which power
reproduces itself; being is the ontological depth where the wound
lives.

## Scope

**When to use this tradition.** When working with content that
specifically engages the *coloniality* literature, or with critical
work where the question is *at what depth* coloniality is operating —
structural, epistemological, or ontological. Useful for critical race
theory, post-colonial philosophy, and decolonial-feminist work.

**When NOT to use this tradition.** Content without a critical-power
analysis dimension fits poorly. The three-tier rendering also
presupposes the decolonial framework's commitments — readers unfamiliar
with Quijano and Mignolo will find the ring labels opaque.

## Applicability

- Decolonial philosophy and critical race theory.
- Post-colonial and de-colonial readings of knowledge systems.
- Self-audit of one's own writing for unrecognized coloniality at
  the three tiers.

## Lineage

Caribbean-American decolonial philosophy. Nelson Maldonado-Torres (at
Rutgers University), building explicitly on Aníbal Quijano and Walter
Mignolo, articulated the *coloniality of being* in his 2007 essay of
that name as a third tier complementing the two earlier
formulations. The three-tier framing has become a standard reference
in decolonial-philosophy literature.

## Critique

The three-tier ring rendering, like Mignolo's hub-and-spoke, can
itself be critiqued for performing the totalizing gesture decolonial
theory resists — fixing the *coloniality* concept into a tidy
three-fold schema. From within decolonial scholarship, the
proliferation of "coloniality of X" formulations (coloniality of
gender, coloniality of nature, coloniality of sensibility) suggests
the three-tier framing may already be insufficient.

## Citation

**Primary.** Nelson Maldonado-Torres, "On the Coloniality of Being:
Contributions to the Development of a Concept," *Cultural Studies*
21, nos. 2–3 (2007): 240–70.

**Modern.** Nelson Maldonado-Torres, *Against War: Views from the

America," *Nepantla: Views from South* 1, no. 3 (2000): 533–80.
