# Mencian sprouts (四端)

**Family**: East Asian Confucian · **Shape**: sectoral (4 quadrants + optional central xìn ring)

## Hero metaphor

The dome divides into **four quadrants**, one per sprout (*duān*) of
the moral heart-mind that Mencius identified as innate in every human:

- **cèyǐn** 惻隱 (compassion) → flowers into **rén** 仁 (humaneness)
- **xiūwù** 羞惡 (shame) → flowers into **yì** 義 (rightness)
- **círàng** 辭讓 (deference) → flowers into **lǐ** 禮 (ritual propriety)
- **shìfēi** 是非 (sense of right and wrong) → flowers into **zhì** 智
  (wisdom)

A small central ring carries the Han-dynasty addition: **xìn** 信
(trustworthiness), the fifth constant virtue (*wǔcháng* 五常). The
sectors sit at 90° each, rotated +π/4 from the cardinal axes to clear
the vertical axis from stratum labels — the same Longino offset.

The "sprout" metaphor is the heart of the framework: these are not
finished virtues but seeds that must be cultivated. The visualization
encodes which sprout each note tends.

## Scope

**When to use this tradition.** When working with moral-psychological
content, ethical-character development, or applied Confucian ethics.
Useful for sorting notes by which virtue they explore or exemplify,
for self-cultivation journaling, and for comparative ethics.

**When NOT to use this tradition.** Purely descriptive empirical
content does not have a moral-sprout dimension. The framework is also
strongly ethical-normative — users skeptical of innate-virtue claims
may find the geometry tendentious.

## Applicability

- Confucian ethics, virtue ethics, character-formation writing.
- Comparative virtue ethics across traditions.
- Self-cultivation journaling using the four-sprouts checklist.

## Lineage

Classical Confucianism. Mencius (Mèngzǐ 孟子, c. 372–289 BCE), the
second-most-important Confucian classic after the *Analects*,
articulates the four sprouts in *Mengzi* 2A.6 — the famous "child
falling into a well" passage where the universal human response of
horror is offered as evidence that compassion is innate, not learned.
The Han-dynasty addition of *xìn* as a fifth virtue produced the
*wǔcháng* canon that became standard in subsequent Confucian
education.

## Critique

The innate-sprout thesis has been a Confucian-internal controversy
since Xunzi (3rd c. BCE), who argued the opposite — that human nature
is initially bad and virtues are cultural achievements. Modern critics
question whether the four sprouts are *empirically* universal or
culturally specific. The Han addition of *xìn* is also a doctrinal
move that not all interpreters accept; Constellation includes it as an
optional center ring rather than a fifth sector to keep both the
Mencian four-fold and the Han five-fold readings available.
