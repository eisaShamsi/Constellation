# PaRDeS (פרד״ס)

**Family**: Jewish (Abrahamic) · **Shape**: rings (4 concentric depth zones)

## Hero metaphor

The dome divides into **four concentric rings**, one per level of
interpretation in the classical Jewish exegetical scheme. The acronym
PaRDeS spells the Hebrew word for "orchard" — and the four levels are
the gates one enters in succession:

- **Pəshaṭ** (פשט) — outermost ring. The plain or literal sense of
  the text.
- **Remez** (רמז) — second ring. The hint or allegorical sense.
- **Drash** (דרש) — third ring. The interpretive or midrashic sense,
  homiletical and ethical.
- **Sod** (סוד) — innermost ring. The hidden or mystical sense.

The geometry encodes *depth of interpretation*: surface at the rim,
mystery at the core. This is the most faithful rendering — PaRDeS is
explicitly hierarchical-in-depth, not categorical.

## Scope

**When to use this tradition.** When working with text-interpretive
content where the same passage admits multiple layered readings.
Useful for Bible / Torah commentary, literary hermeneutics that take
inspiration from the rabbinic model, and any project where surface /
hint / interpretation / mystery is a useful sorting.

**When NOT to use this tradition.** When content has no
text-interpretive dimension — empirical, procedural, or descriptive
notes don't have a *pəshaṭ* in the relevant sense. Also a heavy
tradition to apply non-trivially: pəshaṭ is not "the easy reading"
and sod is not "what feels mystical to me."

## Applicability

- Torah / Bible commentary in the Jewish exegetical tradition.
- Literary hermeneutics modeled on PaRDeS.
- Layered-reading projects (legal text, poetry, sacred text in any
  tradition).

## Lineage

Medieval Jewish exegesis. The four levels themselves are present in
Talmud and Midrash; their canonical four-fold systematization is
attributed to Moses de León (13th c.) in the orbit of the *Zohar*,
the foundational Kabbalistic text. The framework became dominant in
both rabbinic and Kabbalistic exegesis and remains the standard
heuristic in contemporary Jewish hermeneutics.

## Critique

PaRDeS has been criticized for risking a "deeper-is-truer" reading
in which *sod* dominates *pəshaṭ* — modern Jewish scholars (Gershom
Scholem, Moshe Idel, and others) have stressed that the four levels
are best read as complementary modes rather than as a ladder. The
concentric-ring rendering visually privileges the depth metaphor; users
who prefer a flat-categorical reading may find it tendentious.
