# Ibn Khaldūn ʿumrān (علم العمران)

**Family**: Arabic / Islamic beyond uṣūl · **Shape**: binary-flow (horizontal, cyclic)

## Hero metaphor

The dome divides into **two horizontal bands** with cyclic flow arrows
connecting them — Ibn Khaldūn's foundational distinction in the
*ʿilm al-ʿumrān* (science of civilization):

- **ʿumrān ḥaḍarī** (top) — urban, sedentary life. Refined, dependent
  on division of labor, prone to luxury and the decay of *ʿaṣabiyya*
  (group solidarity).
- **ʿumrān badawī** (bottom) — rural, nomadic, tribal life. Hardy,
  cohesive, the source of *ʿaṣabiyya*. The dynastic-energy reservoir
  that produces the next ruling house.

The cyclic flow arrows (rotational — left-up + right-down) make the
*civilizational temporality* visible: badawī conquers ḥaḍarī, settles,
becomes ḥaḍarī, decays, is conquered by a new badawī wave. This is
not static category but dynamic transition.

## Scope

**When to use this tradition.** When your content has a
civilizational, macro-sociological, or longue-durée historical
dimension. Useful for political-theoretical work, philosophy of
history, anthropology, and comparative sociology where the
urban/rural binary and the rise-and-fall dynamic are organizing
axes.

**When NOT to use this tradition.** Awkward fit for legal, ritual,
or mystical content — the badawī/ḥaḍarī binary classifies modes of
*social existence*, not modes of knowing. Also a poor fit for
content with no temporal-civilizational dimension.

## Applicability

- Macro-sociological analysis using Ibn Khaldūn's framework.
- Comparative philosophy of history.
- Political-theoretical work on social cohesion and decay.

## Lineage

14th-century Maghribi historiography and proto-sociology. Ibn Khaldūn
(1332–1406), Tunisian-born Maliki jurist, polymath, and historian,
wrote the *Muqaddima* (1377) as the methodological introduction to a
projected universal history. The *ʿumrān* framework is the
methodological centerpiece — Ibn Khaldūn argued he was founding a
new science distinct from history-as-narrative and from political
philosophy-as-prescription. Modern readers (from Toynbee to Gellner to
contemporary Khaldunian scholars) have read it as an early
sociology of civilizations.

## Critique

The badawī/ḥaḍarī binary has been criticized as essentializing —
ascribing to "the nomad" and "the urbanite" stable cultural-psychic
traits that real history complicates. The *ʿaṣabiyya* concept, while
illuminating, has been used to justify a wide range of incompatible
political programs (from anti-colonial nationalism to authoritarian
realpolitik). Constellation's binary rendering is the most faithful
geometry, but it does flatten the four-facet expansion some
scholars draw out (geographic, anthropological, sociological,
dynastic).
