# Polanyi

**Family**: Modern Western · **Shape**: gradient (opacity overlay)

## Hero metaphor

The dome stays in its Aristotelian layout — but a **fog** is drawn over
it. Stars near the center are dim ("tacit", acknowledged but
inarticulable); stars near the rim are clear ("explicit", what you can
fully say). The metaphor inverts the usual Aristotelian reading: at the
center is not "foundation" but the **tacit dimension** — the
knowledge-in-skill, knowledge-in-judgment, knowledge-in-recognition
that we possess but cannot reduce to propositions.

Polanyi is a *modulation*, not a redistribution. Notes do not move; their
visibility shifts. This is the only tradition in the curated baseline
that overlays rather than re-positions.

## Scope

**When to use this tradition.** When you want to ask "how much of what
I know is in my skill rather than in my notes?" — when the audit
question is about articulability. Useful for craft, performance,
medical-clinical, scientific-experimental, and pedagogical content
where the gap between what one can do and what one can say is the
interesting variable.

**When NOT to use this tradition.** When all your content is
explicitly written-out propositions, the gradient won't surface much.
Also when you want a categorical (this-vs-that) lens — Polanyi's
continuous gradient is deliberately not categorical.

## Applicability

- Craft and skill traditions (medicine, science, music, woodworking).
- Tacit/explicit knowledge audits in organizational contexts.
- Phenomenology of knowing-how vs. knowing-that.

## Lineage

Modern Western pluralist epistemology. Michael Polanyi (1891–1976), a
Hungarian-British physical chemist who became a philosopher,
introduced the tacit/explicit polarity as a continuous spectrum.
*Personal Knowledge* (1958) is the systematic statement;
*The Tacit Dimension* (1966) is the canonical short distillation. The
key thesis: *we know more than we can tell* — and what we cannot tell
nevertheless does epistemic work.

## Critique

The tacit/explicit polarity has been criticized for being too neat —
critics from the empirical side (Harry Collins's typology of tacit
knowledge) argue Polanyi conflates several distinct phenomena. From the
phenomenological side, the gradient assumes a stable axis where there
may be incommensurable kinds of tacitness. Polanyi's broader
"emergence" metaphysics is not invoked in the Sight rendering — only
the tacit/explicit polarity is.

## Citation

**Primary.** Michael Polanyi, *The Tacit Dimension* (Garden City, NY:
Doubleday, 1966), ch. 1 ("Tacit Knowing"); *Personal Knowledge:
Towards a Post-Critical Philosophy* (Chicago: University of Chicago
Press, 1958), Part III.

**Modern.** Harry M. Collins, *Tacit and Explicit Knowledge* (Chicago:
University of Chicago Press, 2010); Stephen R. Turner, *Brains/Practices/
Relativism: Social Theory after Cognitive Science* (Chicago: University
of Chicago Press, 2002).
