# Aristotelian

**Family**: Western classical · **Shape**: sectoral (identity remap)

## Hero metaphor

The dome shows your universe as a **maturity gradient through time**. The
five concentric stratum bands (Foundation → Roots → Trunk → Branches →
Twigs → Edge of Knowing) read inward to outward; the angular sweep
encodes time of creation. Notes "rise" through the strata as your
understanding of them deepens. This is the default Sight grammar made
explicit as a choice — what would otherwise be an unmarked baseline is
named here as one frame among many.

## Scope

**When to use this tradition.** Always available as the default. Useful
when you want to see your universe's overall maturity profile — where
the bulk of your work sits, where the dense clusters are, what is at
the edge of knowing. Best when the question is "where am I in my
thinking?" rather than "what kind of knowledge is this?".

**When NOT to use this tradition.** When you need a categorical lens
(what *kind* of evidence supports a claim — testimony, demonstration,
analogy). Aristotelian flattens all knowledge to a single maturity
axis; categorical traditions (pramāṇa, masādir, Peirce) give you
proof-kind discrimination Aristotelian cannot.

## Applicability

- Default first-open view of any universe.
- Diagnosing universe shape: density per stratum, growth direction.
- Tracking the overall lifecycle of a knowledge project.

## Lineage

Aristotle's *Posterior Analytics* (4th c. BCE) and the long tradition
of taxonomic Western philosophy that followed it. The maturity-stratum
mapping itself is Constellation's choice — Aristotle did not assign
geometry, but his hierarchical model of knowing-by-demonstration
underlies the assumption that knowledge has depth-levels.

## Critique

This is the most Western-default of all the traditions. Making it
explicit as a choice (rather than smuggling it in as the unnamed
baseline) is the *point* — but the maturity-gradient assumption still
encodes a particular epistemology where knowledge progresses, deepens,
matures. Other traditions in this set (pluriversal, complementary,
relational) explicitly resist that assumption.

## Citation

**Modern.** G. E. R. Lloyd, *The Ambitions of Curiosity: Understanding
