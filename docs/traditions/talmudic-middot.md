# Talmudic 13 middot (שלוש עשרה מידות)

**Family**: Jewish (Abrahamic) · **Shape**: ladder (13-step spiral)

## Hero metaphor

The dome shows **thirteen hermeneutic rules** — Rabbi Ishmael's
*sheloshah ʿaśar middot*, the canonical Tannaitic toolkit for deriving
law from the Torah text. Rendered as a 13-step spiral ladder. The
middot are not a *content hierarchy* but a *toolkit*: each rule is an
inference-pattern (the *qal vaḥomer* a-fortiori, the *gezerah shavah*
verbal analogy, the *binyan av* paradigm-from-one-source, etc.).

The 13-step rendering is chosen because Rabbi Ishmael's enumeration
became the canonical liturgical and didactic version, recited daily in
the *Korbanot* section of the morning service. Hillel's earlier
seven-rule version is the alternative often cited as cleaner —
Constellation ships the 13-rule version because it is the most
historically prominent.

## Scope

**When to use this tradition.** When working with legal-hermeneutic
content where the question is *which inference pattern produced this
ruling*. Useful for Talmud and halakhic study, for the philosophy of
legal interpretation, and as a hermeneutic checklist (am I drawing
this conclusion by *qal vaḥomer* — and is that justified here?).

**When NOT to use this tradition.** Non-legal-hermeneutic content
doesn't sort meaningfully into the 13 rules. Thirteen is also a high
cardinality — most user content will cluster at one or two rules.

## Applicability

- Talmud study and halakhic analysis.
- Philosophy of legal interpretation (in any legal tradition with
  formal inference rules).
- Comparative analysis of Jewish and Islamic legal hermeneutics
  (*qiyās* vs. *qal vaḥomer*, etc.).

## Lineage

Tannaitic / early rabbinic Judaism, 2nd c. CE. Rabbi Ishmael ben
Elisha codified the thirteen rules as an expansion of Hillel's
earlier seven-rule list (the *baraita* of Hillel). The thirteen-rule
list is preserved in the *Sifra* on Leviticus and is recited daily in
traditional liturgy. Modern scholarship on Talmudic logic (notably
Louis Jacobs) has analyzed the rules in light of formal logic and
legal-theory frameworks.

## Critique

Thirteen is an awkward cardinality for clean visual sorting — the
Constellation ladder renders all thirteen steps but most user content
will not exercise more than three or four. Some scholars argue the
rules are *post-hoc* rationalizations of legal decisions rather than
generative procedures, in which case the toolkit framing is
misleading. The choice of Ishmael's 13 over Hillel's 7 is also a
contested editorial decision in the tradition itself.

## Citation

**Primary.** *Baraita de-Rabbi Ishmael*, preserved in the *Sifra* on
Leviticus, introduction. Available in the *Sifra*, ed. Isaac Hirsch
Weiss (Vienna: Schlossberg, 1862; reprinted New York: Om, 1947).

**Modern.** Louis Jacobs, *Studies in Talmudic Logic and Methodology*
(London: Vallentine Mitchell, 1961); David Weiss Halivni, *Midrash,
Mishnah, and Gemara: The Jewish Predilection for Justified Law*
(Cambridge, MA: Harvard University Press, 1986).
