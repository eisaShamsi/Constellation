# Korean Sŏngnihak (性理學 / 四七論辯)

**Family**: East Asian Confucian · **Shape**: sectoral (4 wedges encoding a 2×2 grid)

## Hero metaphor

The dome divides into **four wedges** encoding a 2 × 2 conceptual
grid from the Korean *Four-Seven Debate* (사칠논변, *sa-chil
nonbyŏn*):

- **Vertical axis**: **lǐ** 理 / **qì** 氣 (Korean *i / gi*) —
  *principle* vs. *material force*. The classical Neo-Confucian
  metaphysical pair.
- **Horizontal axis**: **four moral sprouts** (四端 *sa-dan*) vs.
  **seven feelings** (七情 *chil-chŏng*). The Mencian moral
  sprouts vs. the broader *Liji* (Book of Rites) catalogue of human
  emotional response.

The four resulting cells let you sort notes by both axes at once: is
this principle-sided or material-force-sided? Is this Mencian-sprout
material or seven-feelings material? The sectors sit at 90° each,
rotated +π/4 from the cardinal axes (Longino pattern) to clear the
vertical-axis stratum-label collision.

## Scope

**When to use this tradition.** When working with Korean Neo-Confucian
philosophy or with moral-psychological content that benefits from the
two-axis sort. Useful for distinguishing principle-discourse from
material-force discourse, and for distinguishing the morally-loaded
sprouts from the wider repertoire of human feeling.

**When NOT to use this tradition.** Non-Confucian moral-psychological
content can be force-fit into the grid but loses meaning. The
framework is specifically Chosŏn-period; applying it outside its
context risks anachronism.

## Applicability

- Chosŏn-dynasty Korean philosophy and the T'oegye/Kobong/Yulgok
  debate tradition.
- Comparative moral psychology across the East Asian Confucian world.
- Distinguishing principle-discourse from material-force discourse in
  any metaphysically-charged content.

## Lineage

Chosŏn-dynasty Korean Neo-Confucianism, 16th c. The *Four-Seven
Debate* arose from an exchange of letters between Yi Hwang (T'oegye,
李滉, 1501–1570) and the younger scholar Ki Daesŭng (Kobong, 奇大升,
1527–1572), and was later extended by Yi I (Yulgok, 李珥, 1536–1584).
The debate concerned whether the four Mencian sprouts arise from
*lǐ* (principle) while the seven feelings arise from *qì* (material
force), or whether *lǐ* and *qì* are inseparable in all moral
emotion. The debate became the longest-running and most-cited
philosophical controversy in Korean intellectual history.

## Critique

The Four-Seven Debate is famously dense and was contested in its own
time as much for what was at stake politically as for what was at
stake philosophically. The 2 × 2 grid is a clean visualization but
flattens the way the original debate moved fluidly between metaphysics,
ethics, and political theory. Sŏngnihak is also a deeply
*Korea-specific* configuration of Confucian thought; applying its
vocabulary to non-Korean Confucian material is interpretively risky.

## Citation

**Primary.** The T'oegye / Kobong / Yulgok correspondence, especially
Yi Hwang's *Sŏnghak sipto* (Ten Diagrams on Sage Learning). English:
Michael C. Kalton et al., trans., *The Four-Seven Debate: An
Annotated Translation of the Most Famous Controversy in Korean
Neo-Confucian Thought* (Albany: SUNY Press, 1994).

**Modern.** Edward Y. J. Chung, *The Korean Neo-Confucianism of Yi
T'oegye and Yi Yulgok: A Reappraisal of the "Four-Seven Thesis" and
Its Practical Implications for Self-Cultivation* (Albany: SUNY Press,
1995); Youngmin Kim, *A History of Chinese Political Thought* (Cambridge:
Polity, 2018), ch. on Korean reception.
