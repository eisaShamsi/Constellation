# Longino

**Family**: Modern Western · **Shape**: sectoral (4 wedges)

## Hero metaphor

The dome divides into **four norms for objective knowledge production**
— Helen Longino's Critical Contextual Empiricism (CCE), which argues
that objectivity is a social-procedural achievement, not an individual
cognitive virtue:

- **Venues for criticism** — recognized public forums where claims can
  be challenged.
- **Uptake of criticism** — actual responsiveness of communities to
  challenges that survive scrutiny.
- **Public standards** — shared evaluative criteria, however
  provisional.
- **Tempered equality of intellectual authority** — qualified
  participation across the community, against authority-by-rank alone.

The four sectors sit at 90° each, rotated +π/4 from the cardinal axes
(per the §δ.2 Longino offset) so dividers land at NE/SE/SW/NW —
clearing the vertical axis from stratum labels.

## Scope

**When to use this tradition.** When you are auditing the *social
process* by which a body of claims comes to count as objective — when
the question is not "is this true?" but "by what procedure did this
become defensible?". Excellent for science-studies work, peer-review
analysis, and reflection on one's own knowledge community's norms.

**When NOT to use this tradition.** This is a procedure register, not
a content register. If your notes are about subject-matter rather than
about *how claims are vetted*, the four-norm structure surfaces little.

## Applicability

- Philosophy and sociology of science.
- Peer-review and scholarly-community analysis.
- Methodological self-audit: are my conclusions held against the four
  norms, or only against some?

## Lineage

Feminist social epistemology and contextualist philosophy of science.
Helen Longino (b. 1944) developed CCE across *Science as Social
Knowledge* (1990) and *The Fate of Knowledge* (2002) as an alternative
to both naïve realism and strong-program social constructivism.
The four norms are a *necessary condition* for objectivity, not a
sufficient one: a community can satisfy all four and still be wrong;
a community can fail one and still be right by accident.

## Critique

Critics from the science-studies side (e.g., Steve Fuller) argue the
norms are too thin to constrain inquiry; critics from the rationalist
side (e.g., Susan Haack) argue they over-socialize the epistemic.
Longino has defended CCE as a procedural floor without claiming it
exhausts the conditions of good knowledge. The framework is
deliberately *post-positivist* and assumes the failure of
foundationalist alternatives.

## Citation

**Primary.** Helen E. Longino, *Science as Social Knowledge: Values
and Objectivity in Scientific Inquiry* (Princeton: Princeton
University Press, 1990); *The Fate of Knowledge* (Princeton:
Princeton University Press, 2002).

**Modern.** Miriam Solomon, *Social Empiricism* (Cambridge, MA: MIT
Press, 2001); Kristina Rolin, "Science as Collective Knowledge,"
*Cognitive Systems Research* 9, nos. 1–2 (2008): 115–24.
