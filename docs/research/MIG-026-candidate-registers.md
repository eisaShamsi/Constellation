# Constellation Register Candidates — Cross-Tradition Survey

**Compiled 2026-05-16. Scope: candidates for additional registers beyond the current 6 baseline.**

---

## §1 Executive Summary

This survey identifies **~25 strong candidates** for additional Constellation registers across the gap-areas Eisa flagged, distributed roughly as follows:

- **Arabic/Islamic heritage beyond masādir**: 7 candidates (Muʿtazilī, Ibn Rushd's burhān ladder, maqāṣid al-sharīʿa, Ikhwān al-Ṣafāʾ tripartite, Ibn Khaldūn ʿumrān, modern Arab hermeneutics cluster, Twelver Shīʿī uṣūl)
- **African epistemologies**: 4 candidates (Akan Wiredu/Gyekye, Yoruba Hallen/Sodipo + Ifá, Bantu Tempels four categories, Igwebuike/Ibuanyidanda complementarity)
- **Latin American decolonial**: 4 candidates (Quijano coloniality matrix, Mignolo pluriversal/border, Dussel transmodernity, Maldonado-Torres coloniality of being) — but see §3 caveats
- **East Asian beyond Mohist**: 5 candidates (Mencian four sprouts, Wang Yangming liángzhī, Huáyán four dharmadhātu, Tiāntāi three truths, Korean Sŏngnihak Four-Seven li/qi)
- **Indigenous knowledge frameworks**: 4 candidates (Māori whakapapa/mātauranga, Inuit Qaujimajatuqangit 8 principles, Andean sumak kawsay triad, North American Medicine Wheel)
- **Modern Western beyond Polanyi**: 5 candidates (Peirce three categories, Dewey inquiry pattern, Husserl regional ontologies, Habermas knowledge-constitutive interests, Longino CCE four norms)
- **Jewish**: 3 candidates (PaRDeS four, Maimonidean prophecy hierarchy, Talmudic 13 middot)
- **Hindu beyond Nyāya**: 3 candidates (Mīmāṃsā six pramāṇas, Advaita three-or-six pramāṇas, Sāṃkhya 25 tattvas)
- **Buddhist beyond Mohist contemporaries**: 3 candidates (Dharmakīrti two pramāṇas, Madhyamaka catuṣkoṭi, Zen sudden/gradual)
- **Feminist/standpoint**: 2 candidates (Collins's matrix-of-domination four domains; Haraway/Harding standpoint as a non-geometric register)

**Distribution by region** (very rough): West Asia/Islam 7, South Asia 6, East Asia 5, sub-Saharan Africa 4, Indigenous (Americas/Pacific/Arctic) 4, Latin America (decolonial) 4, modern West 7, Judaic 3.

**Traditions that resist the register format** (detailed §3): Dussel/Mignolo (process not category), Yoruba Ifá (combinatorial 256-cell rather than spatial), Aboriginal Australian songlines (relational/cartographic not categorical), Daoist wuwei/ziran (anti-categorical by design), Hountondji-style critical universalism (rejects culturally-bounded "ethnophilosophy" as register-able).

## §2 Candidate Registers

### Arabic / Islamic heritage beyond masādir

#### Muʿtazilī tripartite proof structure (al-ʿaql wa-l-naql)

1. **Name**: Muʿtazilī rational theology — three proof types (الأدلة الثلاثة: عقلي / سمعي / لغوي)
2. **Tradition**: 8th–10th c. CE, Baṣra and Baghdad schools of kalām. Founders Wāṣil ibn ʿAṭāʾ (d. 748) and ʿAmr ibn ʿUbayd. Major figures: Abū al-Hudhayl, al-Naẓẓām, al-Jāḥiẓ, al-Qāḍī ʿAbd al-Jabbār.
3. **Frame structure**: Tripartite proof — **rational (ʿaqlī)**, **revelational/transmitted (samʿī)**, **linguistic (lughawī)** — each with distinct domain of authority. The famous "Five Principles" (al-uṣūl al-khamsa: tawḥīd, ʿadl, al-waʿd wa-l-waʿīd, al-manzila bayn al-manzilatayn, al-amr bi-l-maʿrūf) are doctrinal commitments not epistemic categories — the *register* candidate is the threefold proof typology, which is genuinely epistemic.
4. **Geometric implications**: Three radial sectors at 120° each, with ʿaqlī as innermost ring (foundational), samʿī as middle, lughawī as outer methodological. Or three pie slices. The five-principles could appear as five sub-arcs in samʿī.
5. **Citation**: ʿAbd al-Jabbār al-Hamadhānī, *al-Mughnī fī abwāb al-tawḥīd wa-l-ʿadl* (ed. various Cairo, 1960s–70s). Modern: Richard C. Martin, Mark R. Woodward, and Dwi S. Atmaja, *Defenders of Reason in Islam: Muʿtazilism from Medieval School to Modern Symbol* (Oxford: Oneworld, 1997).
6. **Scope**: Sunni rationalist theology / kalām debate; later adopted in modified form by Zaydī and some Twelver Shīʿī kalām. Does **not** apply to fiqh derivation (use masādir for that) or to mystical/Sufi content.

#### Ibn Rushd's ladder of demonstration (burhān / jadal / khaṭāba / shiʿr)

1. **Name**: Marātib al-burhān (مراتب البرهان) — Averroes's hierarchy of demonstration
2. **Tradition**: 12th c. Andalusian falsafa. Ibn Rushd (Averroes, 1126–1198), Córdoba.
3. **Frame structure**: Four-tier ladder of discourse adapted from Aristotle's *Organon* (*Posterior Analytics*, *Topics*, *Rhetoric*, *Poetics*): **burhān** (apodictic demonstration — philosophers), **jadal** (dialectic — theologians), **khaṭāba** (rhetoric — preachers/jurists), **shiʿr** (poetic image — the common public). Each addresses a different epistemic audience. Note also Ibn Rushd's internal trio of burhān types: *burhān muṭlaq*, *burhān al-wujūd*, *burhān al-ʿilla*.
4. **Geometric implications**: Concentric rings (innermost burhān, outermost shiʿr) is the most faithful — the structure is genuinely hierarchical not coordinate. Could also be horizontal bands like Mohist sān biǎo.
5. **Citation**: Ibn Rushd, *Faṣl al-maqāl fī mā bayn al-sharīʿa wa-l-ḥikma min al-ittiṣāl* (Decisive Treatise). Modern: Charles E. Butterworth, trans., *Averroës: Decisive Treatise and Epistle Dedicatory* (Provo: Brigham Young University Press, 2008). SEP article "Ibn Rushd [Averroes]" by Fouad Ben Ahmed and Robert Pasnau.
6. **Scope**: Philosophical-theological-rhetorical content stratified by audience. Maps well onto a knowledge-formulation app where the same idea has lay/dialectical/demonstrative variants. Doesn't cover fiqh derivation or mystical experience.

#### Maqāṣid al-sharīʿa (Shāṭibī's three tiers × five necessities)

1. **Name**: Maqāṣid al-sharīʿa (مقاصد الشريعة) — three-tier × five-necessity grid
2. **Tradition**: 8th c. AH / 14th c. CE Maliki Andalusian uṣūl. Abū Isḥāq al-Shāṭibī (d. 790/1388), Granada. Builds on al-Juwaynī and al-Ghazālī.
3. **Frame structure**: **Three concentric tiers** by importance: *ḍarūriyyāt* (essentials), *ḥājiyyāt* (needs), *taḥsīniyyāt* (embellishments/luxuries). Within ḍarūriyyāt: **five necessities** (al-kulliyyāt al-khams) — *dīn* (religion), *nafs* (life), *ʿaql* (mind), *nasl* (lineage), *māl* (property). Total 3 × 5 = 15-cell grid.
4. **Geometric implications**: This is the cleanest geometric fit of any Islamic register. Three concentric rings, each divided into 5 sectors = 15 distinct cells. Visually intuitive and well-attested. Alternatively three rings with 5 radial spokes.
5. **Citation**: Abū Isḥāq al-Shāṭibī, *al-Muwāfaqāt fī uṣūl al-sharīʿa*, ed. ʿAbd Allāh Darrāz (Cairo: al-Maktaba al-Tijāriyya al-Kubrā, n.d.). Modern: Jasser Auda, *Maqasid al-Shariah as Philosophy of Islamic Law: A Systems Approach* (London: IIIT, 2008).
6. **Scope**: Sunni Islamic legal-philosophical framework for evaluating rulings and policy. Has been extended to contemporary ethics, finance, environmental policy, but its native domain is legal-ethical reasoning.

#### Ikhwān al-Ṣafāʾ tripartite classification of sciences

1. **Name**: Tartīb al-ʿulūm (ترتيب العلوم) — Brethren of Purity's three-tier hierarchy
2. **Tradition**: 10th c. CE, anonymous esoteric fraternity in Baṣra and Baghdad. Often associated with Ismāʿīlī milieu but disputed.
3. **Frame structure**: Three hierarchical kinds — **propaedeutical** (*al-riyāḍiyya*: reading, writing, grammar, arts, trades, history), **religious** (*al-sharʿiyya al-waḍʿiyya*: those imposed by religious Law), **philosophical** (*al-falsafiyya al-ḥaqīqiyya*: full comprehension of truth). The 52 Epistles themselves are then sub-grouped into four classificatory parts (mathematics, logic, natural philosophy, theology/metaphysics) — so a register could carry the 3-tier outer × 4-section inner.
4. **Geometric implications**: Three vertical bands or three radial tiers. With the four-part inner subdivision, you get a 3 × 4 grid, but the outer 3-tier is the conceptual primary.
5. **Citation**: Ikhwān al-Ṣafāʾ, *Rasāʾil Ikhwān al-Ṣafāʾ wa Khillān al-Wafāʾ*, ed. Buṭrus al-Bustānī, 4 vols. (Beirut: Dār Ṣādir, 1957). Epistle 7 contains the classification (pp. 266–274). Modern: Nader El-Bizri, ed., *Epistles of the Brethren of Purity: The Ikhwān al-Ṣafāʾ and Their Rasāʾil — An Introduction* (Oxford: Oxford University Press, 2008).
6. **Scope**: Encyclopedic — actually attempts to organize all knowledge of its age. Among the broader Islamic registers in scope.

#### Ibn Khaldūn's ʿumrān frame

1. **Name**: ʿIlm al-ʿumrān (علم العمران) — science of civilization
2. **Tradition**: 14th c. CE, Tunis / Cairo. Ibn Khaldūn (1332–1406), *Muqaddima* (1377).
3. **Frame structure**: Two-fold core distinction — **ʿumrān badawī** (rural/nomadic life) vs. **ʿumrān ḥaḍarī** (urban/sedentary life), governed by ʿaṣabiyya (group solidarity) and a cyclical dynastic theory. Some scholarly treatments expand to four facets: ʿumrān al-ʿālam (geo-historical), ʿumrān al-bāsharī (anthropological), and the badawī/ḥaḍarī sociological pair. A genuine 2-cell core with optional 4-facet expansion.
4. **Geometric implications**: Two horizontal bands (badawī below, ḥaḍarī above) with a cycle-arrow connecting them is the most faithful — the dynamic is *transition*, not static category. Or a yin-yang-like binary with a cyclical halo. Constellation would need to honor the cyclical-civilizational temporality, which is not categorical.
5. **Citation**: Ibn Khaldūn, *Muqaddimat Ibn Khaldūn*, ed. ʿAlī ʿAbd al-Wāḥid Wāfī, 4 vols. (Cairo: Lajnat al-Bayān al-ʿArabī, 1957). English: Franz Rosenthal, trans., *The Muqaddimah: An Introduction to History*, 3 vols. (Princeton: Princeton University Press, 1958; abridged ed. N.J. Dawood, 1967).
6. **Scope**: Macro-sociological / philosophy of history. Best applied to civilizational, geopolitical, anthropological content. Awkward fit for legal, ritual, or mystical knowledge.

#### Modern Arab hermeneutics cluster (Abū Zayd, Arkoun)

1. **Name**: Pair register — "al-fait coranique vs. al-fait islamique" (Arkoun) // "al-naṣṣ wa-l-siyāq" (Abū Zayd)
2. **Tradition**: Late 20th–early 21st c. modernist Arab Qurʾānic hermeneutics. Naṣr Ḥāmid Abū Zayd (1943–2010, Egypt/Netherlands); Mohammed Arkoun (1928–2010, Algeria/France).
3. **Frame structure**: Arkoun's distinction between **fait coranique** (the Qurʾānic linguistic event in Muḥammad's lifetime) vs. **fait islamique** (the closed corpus + tradition) — a 2-cell historicist register. Abū Zayd's text/context distinction layered with structuralism, discourse analysis, sociology of knowledge. Both can be amalgamated to a **historicist register** with 2 or 3 cells: text-as-event / text-as-corpus / interpretive-community.
4. **Geometric implications**: Two horizontal bands (event below, corpus above) with a permeable historical boundary. Or a Venn-style overlap. Honestly more diagrammatic than register-like — the *content* of this tradition is reading-stance, not knowledge category, so the geometric fit is weaker than for masādir or maqāṣid.
5. **Citation**: Mohammed Arkoun, *Pour une critique de la raison islamique* (Paris: Maisonneuve et Larose, 1984). Naṣr Ḥāmid Abū Zayd, *Mafhūm al-naṣṣ: Dirāsa fī ʿulūm al-Qurʾān* (Beirut: al-Markaz al-Thaqāfī al-ʿArabī, 1990); English: *Rethinking the Qurʾān: Towards a Humanistic Hermeneutics* (Utrecht: Humanistics University Press, 2004).
6. **Scope**: Qurʾānic interpretation and Islamic intellectual history. Useful for users doing critical-theological work. **Honest limitation**: this is a stance-register, not a category-register; it sorts by reading-method, not by content-kind.

#### Twelver Shīʿī uṣūl al-fiqh (Uṣūlī school)

> **FILTERED OUT** by religious-lineage rule (Sunni-only for Islamic). Preserved here as scholarly reference.

1. **Name**: Uṣūl al-fiqh al-shīʿī (أصول الفقه الشيعي) — Four sources, Shīʿī variant
2. **Tradition**: 10th c. onwards; canonized in the Uṣūlī school after Waḥīd al-Bihbahānī's 18th c. victory over the Akhbārī tendency. Modern reference: Murtaḍā al-Anṣārī, Muḥammad Bāqir al-Ṣadr, ʿAlī al-Sīstānī.
3. **Frame structure**: **Four sources**: *al-Qurʾān*, *al-Sunna* (extended to include traditions of the twelve Imāms), *al-ijmāʿ* (recognized only insofar as it reveals an Imām's word), ***al-ʿaql*** (reason — replacing Sunni *qiyās*). The "reason" pillar opens onto four *uṣūl ʿamaliyya* (practical principles): *barāʾa* (immunity), *iḥtiyāṭ* (precaution), *takhyīr* (selection), *istiṣḥāb* (continuity).
4. **Geometric implications**: Four sectors (same geometry as masādir, but with the fourth sector ʿaql instead of qiyās), plus four chips for the practical principles in the ʿaql sector. Visually parallel to masādir, which is pedagogically useful — users see what changes when Shīʿī uṣūl differs from Sunni.
5. **Citation**: Muḥammad Bāqir al-Ṣadr, *Durūs fī ʿilm al-uṣūl*, 4 vols. (Beirut: Dār al-Kitāb al-Lubnānī, 1978–80); English: Roy Mottahedeh, trans., *Lessons in Islamic Jurisprudence* (Oxford: Oneworld, 2003). Modern: Robert Gleave, *Scripturalist Islam: The History and Doctrines of the Akhbārī Shīʿī School* (Leiden: Brill, 2007).
6. **Scope**: Twelver Shīʿī legal-scholarly content. Same scope-class as masādir.

### African epistemologies

#### Akan epistemic register (Wiredu)

1. **Name**: Nokware / Ahonyam / Adwene (Akan truth–opinion–thought)
2. **Tradition**: Contemporary academic Akan philosophy. Kwasi Wiredu (1931–2022, Ghana); Kwame Gyekye (1939–2019, Ghana).
3. **Frame structure**: Wiredu's analysis of the Akan language argues that "truth" (*nokware*) is fundamentally a property of utterances/persons (truthfulness), not propositions — so the Western T/F binary doesn't translate. The honest register-candidate is the **Akan conceptual contrast** between *nokware* (truth as truthfulness), *ahonyam* (opinion/conjecture), and the cognitive faculty *adwene* (mind/thought).
4. **Geometric implications**: This is hard to map cleanly to a register because the framework is *deflationary* — Wiredu's project is conceptual decolonization, not building an alternative grid. A two-cell register (truthful utterance vs. opinion) is faithful but thin. **Honest limitation**: better suited as a *commentary register* than a primary frame.
5. **Citation**: Kwasi Wiredu, "The Concept of Truth in the Akan Language," in *Philosophy in Africa: Trends and Perspectives*, ed. Peter O. Bodunrin (Ile-Ife: University of Ife Press, 1985); Kwame Gyekye, *An Essay on African Philosophical Thought: The Akan Conceptual Scheme*, rev. ed. (Philadelphia: Temple University Press, 1995).
6. **Scope**: Linguistic-conceptual analysis of one African worldview. Best for users working with African philosophy or comparative epistemology.

#### Yoruba ìmọ̀ / ìgbàgbọ́ register (Hallen & Sodipo)

1. **Name**: Ìmọ̀ vs. Ìgbàgbọ́ — Yoruba knowledge / belief contrast
2. **Tradition**: Yoruba oral-philosophical tradition, formalized analytically by Barry Hallen and J. Olubi Sodipo (1986).
3. **Frame structure**: **Two-tier epistemic hierarchy** — *ìmọ̀* (knowledge: first-hand perception with conscious comprehension) above *ìgbàgbọ́* (belief: second-hand information via books, others, oral tradition, media). The intermediate movement is *ìwádìí* (testing/inquiry) by which *ìgbàgbọ́* can become *ìmọ̀* through verification. Some scholars add *àjùmọ̀ìmọ̀* (collective knowledge / consensus) as a third cell.
4. **Geometric implications**: Two horizontal bands (ìmọ̀ above, ìgbàgbọ́ below) with arrows of ìwádìí connecting them, or two concentric rings (verified core, peripheral testimony). Genuinely 2-cell.
5. **Citation**: Barry Hallen and J. Olubi Sodipo, *Knowledge, Belief, and Witchcraft: Analytic Experiments in African Philosophy* (London: Ethnographica, 1986; repr. Stanford: Stanford University Press, 1997). Routledge Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry "Yoruba epistemology."
6. **Scope**: General epistemic stance, applicable beyond Yoruba content. Translates well to a "verified knowledge / testimonial belief / inquiry-process" structure usable by any user.

#### Ifá divination register (256-odù matrix)

> **FILTERED OUT** by religious-lineage rule (Yoruba divinatory religion, non-Abrahamic). Preserved here as scholarly reference. Also format-fit caveat in §3.

1. **Name**: Odù Ifá — 256-cell divination corpus
2. **Tradition**: Yoruba religious-intellectual tradition, attributed mythologically to the òrìṣà Orunmila; UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity (2005).
3. **Frame structure**: 16 principal odù (*Ojú Odù*) generated by binary divination signs, expanding combinatorially to 256 derivative odù (*Ọmọ Odù*), each containing hundreds of *ese* (verses) covering history, ethics, cosmology, medicine.
4. **Geometric implications**: This is **not a sectoral register** — it's a 16 × 16 matrix or a 256-cell grid. Could be visualized as a chord diagram between the 16 principals, but would require a different visualization paradigm than the current registers. Listed here for completeness; see §3.
5. **Citation**: Wande Abimbola, *Ifá: An Exposition of Ifá Literary Corpus* (Ibadan: Oxford University Press Nigeria, 1976). Modern: Jacob K. Olupona and Rowland O. Abiodun, eds., *Ifá Divination, Knowledge, Power, and Performance* (Bloomington: Indiana University Press, 2016).
6. **Scope**: Yoruba religious-divinatory practice; analytically also a knowledge taxonomy.

#### Bantu four categories (Tempels / Kagame)

> **FILTERED OUT** by religious-lineage rule (religious cosmology; also contested by Hountondji as ethnophilosophy). Preserved here as scholarly reference.

1. **Name**: Muntu / Kintu / Hantu / Kuntu (the four -ntu categories)
2. **Tradition**: Central, East, and Southern African Bantu-language families. Formalized in Placide Tempels, *La philosophie bantoue* (1945), and refined by Alexis Kagame, *La philosophie bantu-rwandaise de l'Être* (1956). **Crucial caveat**: Hountondji and others have critiqued Tempels as "ethnophilosophy" (see §3); register users should be told the framework is contested.
3. **Frame structure**: Four categories of being, all sharing the *-ntu* root (force/being): **muntu** (forces with intelligence — humans, ancestors, spirits), **kintu** (forces without agency — things, plants, minerals), **hantu** (place-time — spatiotemporal forces), **kuntu** (modality — manner, including language).
4. **Geometric implications**: Four quadrants with the central *ntu* as unifying force at the center. Geometrically very clean. Honors the etymological unity.
5. **Citation**: Placide Tempels, *La philosophie bantoue* (Élisabethville: Lovania, 1945); English: *Bantu Philosophy*, trans. Colin King (Paris: Présence Africaine, 1959). Alexis Kagame, *La philosophie bantu-rwandaise de l'Être* (Brussels: Académie Royale des Sciences Coloniales, 1956). Critical: Paulin Hountondji, *African Philosophy: Myth and Reality*, trans. Henri Evans (Bloomington: Indiana University Press, 1983).
6. **Scope**: General ontological frame. Apply with the Hountondji caveat surfaced in the register description.

#### Ibuanyidanda / Igwebuike complementarity (Asouzu / Kanu)

1. **Name**: Ibuanyidanda — complementary reflection
2. **Tradition**: Contemporary Igbo (Nigerian) philosophy. Innocent Asouzu (University of Calabar); Ikechukwu Anthony Kanu (Igwebuike Research Institute).
3. **Frame structure**: Not strongly geometric; the core thesis is that being is *complementary* — every entity is "a missing link of reality." Could be rendered as a network/web register where every node is partial without the others. The closest categorical structure is Asouzu's analysis of complementary "modes of existence" — but this is a *relational* not *spatial* register.
4. **Geometric implications**: Better suited to a node-link visualization than a sectoral one. Flagged as candidate but format-fit moderate.
5. **Citation**: Innocent I. Asouzu, *Ibuanyidanda: New Complementary Ontology Beyond World-Immanentism, Ethnocentric Reduction and Impositions* (Münster: LIT Verlag, 2007). Ikechukwu A. Kanu, *Igwebuike: An African Trans-Cultural Philosophy of Being* (Bloomington: AuthorHouse, 2017).
6. **Scope**: Contemporary African systematic philosophy. Useful for users working in network/complementary frames.

### Latin American decolonial frames

**Note (also §3)**: Decolonial thinkers are largely critical of *categorical* knowledge organization itself — putting them inside a register risks performing the very gesture they critique. The candidates below are listed for completeness but Eisa should weigh §3 caveats before adoption.

#### Quijano's coloniality matrix

1. **Name**: Colonialidad del poder — coloniality of power matrix
2. **Tradition**: Aníbal Quijano (1928–2018, Peru); seminal essay 2000.
3. **Frame structure**: Coloniality operates across four interlocking domains: **labor/economy**, **authority/state**, **gender/sexuality**, **knowledge/subjectivity** — all organized around the **idea of race** as foundational classification. A 4-quadrant register with race as central axis is the cleanest fit, but Quijano's matrix is dynamic not static.
4. **Geometric implications**: Four quadrants with a central race/coloniality axis. Geometrically fits, but ideologically a tension (see scope).
5. **Citation**: Aníbal Quijano, "Coloniality of Power, Eurocentrism, and Latin America," *Nepantla: Views from South* 1, no. 3 (2000): 533–80. Reprinted in Walter D. Mignolo and Catherine E. Walsh, eds., *On Decoloniality: Concepts, Analytics, Praxis* (Durham: Duke University Press, 2018).
6. **Scope**: Critical-analytical lens for power, race, and knowledge production in the modern/colonial world system. Best for users doing critical-social work.

#### Mignolo's pluriversal / border-thinking register

1. **Name**: Pluriversality / pensamiento fronterizo
2. **Tradition**: Walter D. Mignolo (b. 1941, Argentina/Duke); developed in *Local Histories / Global Designs* (2000).
3. **Frame structure**: Resists categorical schemas in principle. The closest register-shape is a **center-versus-border** binary (modern/colonial center vs. epistemic border) with multiple subaltern positions on the outside. Not a clean 4-cell or 5-cell frame.
4. **Geometric implications**: A central modernity disc with multiple border epistemes around it as separate clusters; or a non-centered network. Either honors pluriversality awkwardly.
5. **Citation**: Walter D. Mignolo, *Local Histories / Global Designs: Coloniality, Subaltern Knowledges, and Border Thinking* (Princeton: Princeton University Press, 2000; 2nd ed. 2012). Walter D. Mignolo, *The Darker Side of Western Modernity: Global Futures, Decolonial Options* (Durham: Duke University Press, 2011).
6. **Scope**: Meta-epistemological stance. Better as a *meta-register* (a way of describing how registers themselves are situated) than as a primary frame — see §3.

#### Dussel's transmodernity / analectic method

1. **Name**: Transmodernidad / método analéctico
2. **Tradition**: Enrique Dussel (1934–2023, Argentina/Mexico); Philosophy of Liberation 1970s onwards.
3. **Frame structure**: Core binary — **totality** (Eurocentric modernity's closed system) vs. **exteriority** (the standpoint of the excluded — colonized, poor, marginalized). The analectic moves from exteriority back into totality to critique it. A 2-cell register with directional flow.
4. **Geometric implications**: Inner disc (totality) + outer ring (exteriority) with arrows flowing inward; or two adjacent regions with a permeable border. Cleanly fits as a 2-cell + flow.
5. **Citation**: Enrique Dussel, *Filosofía de la liberación* (México: Edicol, 1977); English: *Philosophy of Liberation*, trans. Aquilina Martinez and Christine Morkovsky (Maryknoll, NY: Orbis, 1985).
6. **Scope**: Critical philosophical method, especially for ethics and political-theological work.

#### Maldonado-Torres's coloniality of being (three-tier coloniality)

1. **Name**: Coloniality of power / knowledge / being
2. **Tradition**: Nelson Maldonado-Torres (Rutgers); building on Quijano.
3. **Frame structure**: Three nested coloniality dimensions — **power** (Quijano's structural matrix), **knowledge** (Mignolo's eurocentrism), **being** (Maldonado-Torres's ontological/Heideggerian extension). Three-tier register with downward weighting.
4. **Geometric implications**: Three concentric rings (power outermost, being innermost) — a clean Russian-doll geometry.
5. **Citation**: Nelson Maldonado-Torres, "On the Coloniality of Being: Contributions to the Development of a Concept," *Cultural Studies* 21, nos. 2–3 (2007): 240–70.
6. **Scope**: Critical-philosophical lens. Same scope-class as Quijano's matrix.

### East Asian beyond Mohist

#### Mencian four sprouts → five constant virtues

1. **Name**: Sì duān 四端 / Wǔcháng 五常
2. **Tradition**: Classical Confucianism. Mencius (Mèngzǐ 孟子, c. 372–289 BCE).
3. **Frame structure**: Four sprouts (端) of the heart-mind: **cèyǐn 惻隱** (compassion → *rén* 仁), **xiūwù 羞惡** (shame → *yì* 義), **círàng 辭讓** (deference → *lǐ* 禮), **shìfēi 是非** (right/wrong → *zhì* 智). Han dynasty added a fifth constant: **xìn 信** (trustworthiness). The 4-or-5 sprout/virtue register fits a quadrant + center, or pentagonal layout.
4. **Geometric implications**: Four quadrants with central *xìn* (5-fold), or just four quadrants (Mencius's original).
5. **Citation**: *Mèngzǐ* 孟子, 2A.6 (the famous "child falling into a well" passage establishing the four sprouts). Modern translation: Bryan W. Van Norden, trans., *Mengzi: With Selections from Traditional Commentaries* (Indianapolis: Hackett, 2008).
6. **Scope**: Moral psychology and ethics. Strong fit for ethical/character-formation content; less natural for purely descriptive epistemics.

#### Wang Yangming's liángzhī / zhī-xíng héyī

1. **Name**: 良知 / 知行合一 — innate moral knowing / unity of knowledge and action
2. **Tradition**: Ming-dynasty Neo-Confucianism. Wáng Yángmíng (王陽明, 1472–1529), founder of Xīnxué (心學, "School of the Heart-Mind").
3. **Frame structure**: Not strongly categorical — Wang's distinctive doctrine is the *unity* of knowledge and action against Zhu Xi's separation. Register-candidate: a **2-pole register with a unifying axis**: zhī (knowing) ↔ xíng (acting), with *liángzhī* as the central reservoir feeding both.
4. **Geometric implications**: Two hemispheres unified by central liángzhī, or a Möbius-like loop. Conceptually elegant but geometrically less obvious than other candidates.
5. **Citation**: Wáng Yángmíng, *Chuánxí lù* (傳習錄, Instructions for Practical Living). English: Wing-tsit Chan, trans., *Instructions for Practical Living and Other Neo-Confucian Writings by Wang Yang-ming* (New York: Columbia University Press, 1963).
6. **Scope**: Moral epistemology and practice. Best for users whose notes connect insight to action.

#### Huáyán four dharmadhātu

> **FILTERED OUT** by religious-lineage rule (Mahāyāna Buddhist religious metaphysics). Preserved here as scholarly reference.

1. **Name**: 四法界 sì fǎjiè — four realms of reality
2. **Tradition**: Chinese Huáyán Buddhism, founded by Dùshùn 杜順 (557–640), systematized by Făzàng 法藏 (643–712).
3. **Frame structure**: Four cognitive approaches/realms: **shì fǎjiè 事法界** (realm of phenomena), **lǐ fǎjiè 理法界** (realm of principle), **lǐshì wú'ài fǎjiè 理事無礙法界** (non-obstruction of principle and phenomena), **shìshì wú'ài fǎjiè 事事無礙法界** (non-obstruction of phenomena with each other — universal interpenetration). A clear 4-tier hierarchy.
4. **Geometric implications**: Four nested rings (outer = pure phenomena, innermost = perfect interpenetration), or four ascending zones. Beautiful candidate for a "deepening insight" register.
5. **Citation**: Făzàng, *Huáyán wǔjiào zhāng* 華嚴五教章. English: Thomas Cleary, trans., *The Flower Ornament Scripture: A Translation of the Avatamsaka Sutra* (Boston: Shambhala, 1993).
6. **Scope**: Mahāyāna Buddhist metaphysics and contemplative phenomenology.

#### Tiāntāi three truths (sāndì)

> **FILTERED OUT** by religious-lineage rule (Mahāyāna Buddhist metaphysics). Preserved here as scholarly reference.

1. **Name**: 三諦 sāndì — empty, provisional, middle
2. **Tradition**: Chinese Tiāntāi Buddhism. Zhìyǐ 智顗 (538–597).
3. **Frame structure**: Three truths perfectly interfusing — **kōng 空** (emptiness), **jiǎ 假** (provisional/conventional positing), **zhōng 中** (the middle). Crucially, each implies the other two; this is not a stack but a *yuánróng* (perfect-fusion) triad.
4. **Geometric implications**: A Borromean triangle (each ring through the other two) honors the interfusion. Or a triangular trefoil. **Honest note**: rendering them as 3 separate sectors would betray the doctrine — they must visually interpenetrate.
5. **Citation**: Zhìyǐ, *Móhē zhǐguān* 摩訶止觀. Modern: Brook Ziporyn, *Emptiness and Omnipresence: An Essential Introduction to Tiantai Buddhism* (Bloomington: Indiana University Press, 2016).
6. **Scope**: Mahāyāna contemplative/philosophical content.

#### Korean Sŏngnihak (Four-Seven debate, li-qi structure)

1. **Name**: 性理學 / 四七論辯 — Cheng-Zhu School / Four-Seven Debate
2. **Tradition**: Chosŏn-dynasty Korean Neo-Confucianism, 16th c. Yi Hwang (T'oegye, 1501–1570); Ki Daesŭng (Kobong, 1527–1572); Yi I (Yulgok, 1536–1584).
3. **Frame structure**: Two-axis register: **lǐ 理 / qì 氣 (Kor. *i / gi*)** vertical axis (principle vs. material force) × **four moral emotions (四端) / seven feelings (七情)** horizontal axis. A 2 × 2 grid (or 2 × 11 if counting individual emotions).
4. **Geometric implications**: A 2 × 2 quadrant grid is clean. A more elaborate version sub-divides each quadrant by individual emotion.
5. **Citation**: Michael C. Kalton et al., trans., *The Four-Seven Debate: An Annotated Translation of the Most Famous Controversy in Korean Neo-Confucian Thought* (Albany: SUNY Press, 1994).
6. **Scope**: Korean Neo-Confucian moral psychology. Specialized; users with moral-emotion or character-formation focus.

### Indigenous knowledge frameworks

> **All 4 candidates below are FILTERED OUT** by religious-lineage rule (each carries religious-cosmological dimensions; strict reading excludes them). Preserved here as scholarly reference. Eisa flagged "strict — if the lineage is religious, it's out" via AskUserQuestion.

#### Māori whakapapa / mātauranga

1. **Name**: Mātauranga Māori (anchored by whakapapa)
2. **Tradition**: Māori (Aotearoa / New Zealand).
3. **Frame structure**: Not categorical — *whakapapa* (genealogical relationality) is the organizing principle: every entity (human, non-human, physical, metaphysical) sits in a relational web. *Mātauranga* (knowledge) is generated through whakapapa; *tikanga* is the procedural ethics. The candidate register is **relational-not-categorical**: a network where the primary question is "what is its whakapapa?"
4. **Geometric implications**: A whakapapa register would look like a genealogical tree / network graph, not a sector or band. Maps poorly onto sectoral registers; better as a relational-overlay register that shows note connections through whakapapa-like links. **Honest note**: this is closer to a Sky View / graph-style frame than to the current registers.
5. **Citation**: Mason Durie, *Te Mana, Te Kāwanatanga: The Politics of Māori Self-Determination* (Auckland: Oxford University Press, 1998). Linda Tuhiwai Smith, *Decolonizing Methodologies: Research and Indigenous Peoples*, 2nd ed. (London: Zed Books, 2012).
6. **Scope**: Māori knowledge production. Useful conceptually but architecturally needs a relational, not sectoral, visualization.

#### Inuit Qaujimajatuqangit (8 principles)

1. **Name**: ᐃᓄᐃᑦ ᖃᐅᔨᒪᔭᑐᖃᖏᑦ — Inuit Qaujimajatuqangit (IQ)
2. **Tradition**: Inuit, codified by the Government of Nunavut, 2013.
3. **Frame structure**: Eight principles: **Inuuqatigiitsiarniq** (respect/care), **Tunnganarniq** (welcoming spirit), **Pijitsirniq** (service), **Aajiiqatigiinniq** (consensus decision-making), **Pilimmaksarniq/Pijariuqsarniq** (skill development through practice), **Piliriqatigiinniq/Ikajuqtigiinniq** (collaborative work), **Qanuqtuurniq** (innovation/resourcefulness), **Avatittinnik Kamatsiarniq** (environmental stewardship).
4. **Geometric implications**: Eight sectors (octant) is clean and faithful. Could also be an 8-petaled rosette around a central "Inuit way of being" core.
5. **Citation**: Government of Nunavut, *Incorporating Inuit Societal Values* (Iqaluit: Department of Culture, Language, Elders and Youth, 2013). Frank Tester and Peter Irniq, "Inuit Qaujimajatuqangit: Social History, Politics and the Practice of Resistance," *Arctic* 61, suppl. 1 (2008): 48–61.
6. **Scope**: Indigenous governance, environmental management, education. Broader than category — it's a *values-and-practice* register.

#### Andean sumak kawsay

1. **Name**: Sumak Kawsay — three balance domains
2. **Tradition**: Quechua and Aymara Andean cosmovision; reformulated politically in Ecuador (2008 constitution) and Bolivia.
3. **Frame structure**: Three domains in balance: **Allin Munay** (feeling/loving well), **Allin Yachay** (thinking/knowing well), **Allin Ruay** (doing/acting well) — all within *Pacha* (cosmos/Mother Universe). A clean 3-cell triangle with cosmic context.
4. **Geometric implications**: Triangle inscribed in a circle (the circle = Pacha; the three vertices = the three "Allin" domains).
5. **Citation**: Catherine Walsh, "Development as Buen Vivir: Institutional Arrangements and (De)colonial Entanglements," *Development* 53, no. 1 (2010): 15–21. Eduardo Gudynas, "Buen Vivir: Today's Tomorrow," *Development* 54, no. 4 (2011): 441–47.
6. **Scope**: Andean political-ecological-ethical frame.

#### North American Medicine Wheel (4 / 7 directions)

1. **Name**: Medicine Wheel — Four Sacred Directions (some traditions: Seven)
2. **Tradition**: Multiple Plains, Ojibwe (Anishinaabe), Lakota, and other Indigenous traditions of North America. **Important caveat**: there is no single "Medicine Wheel" — different nations have different teachings.
3. **Frame structure**: **Four Sacred Directions** (E/S/W/N) typically colored yellow/red/black/white, mapped to seasons, life stages, faculties (often: emotional/physical/spiritual/intellectual). Some Anishinaabe traditions add three more (sky, earth, center) for seven directions.
4. **Geometric implications**: Four-quadrant compass (or 7-direction with sky/earth/center) is the foundational geometric shape.
5. **Citation**: Vine Deloria Jr., *God Is Red: A Native View of Religion*, 30th anniv. ed. (Golden, CO: Fulcrum, 2003). Edward Benton-Banai, *The Mishomis Book: The Voice of the Ojibway* (Hayward, WI: Indian Country Communications, 1988).
6. **Scope**: Indigenous personal/communal balance, life-stage, healing frameworks.

### Modern Western beyond Polanyi

#### Peirce's three categories

1. **Name**: Firstness / Secondness / Thirdness
2. **Tradition**: American pragmatism. Charles Sanders Peirce (1839–1914).
3. **Frame structure**: Three phenomenological categories: **Firstness** (quality, feeling, possibility), **Secondness** (action, reaction, brute fact), **Thirdness** (mediation, law, habit, sign). Universal categories underlying experience, semiotics, and reasoning.
4. **Geometric implications**: Three sectors at 120° each. Or three layered rings (1st innermost as raw quality, 3rd outermost as mediation).
5. **Citation**: Charles S. Peirce, "On a New List of Categories" (1867), in *Writings of Charles S. Peirce*, vol. 2, ed. Edward C. Moore et al. (Bloomington: Indiana University Press, 1984).
6. **Scope**: Universal phenomenology. Among the most widely-applicable Western registers — works for almost any content domain.

#### Dewey's pattern of inquiry

1. **Name**: Pattern of Inquiry — five stages
2. **Tradition**: American pragmatism. John Dewey (1859–1952).
3. **Frame structure**: Five-stage cycle: **(1) indeterminate situation**, **(2) problem formulation**, **(3) hypothesis (idea)**, **(4) reasoning**, **(5) testing/judgment** — leading back to a new situation. A *cyclic* register, not static.
4. **Geometric implications**: Five-segment ring with arrow flow (clockwise pentagon-arc) honors the inquiry cycle. Sector view loses the temporality.
5. **Citation**: John Dewey, *Logic: The Theory of Inquiry* (New York: Henry Holt, 1938), esp. chap. 6.
6. **Scope**: Methodology of inquiry. Works for research-process notes; less natural for static content categorization.

#### Husserl's regional ontologies

1. **Name**: Regionale Ontologien — formal vs. regional ontologies
2. **Tradition**: German phenomenology. Edmund Husserl (1859–1938).
3. **Frame structure**: **Formal ontology** (essence of objectivity in general) vs. **regional ontologies** (the essences specific to each region of being — material nature, animal nature, spirit, etc.). Each region has its own *a priori* essential structures.
4. **Geometric implications**: A central disc (formal) with multiple petals (regional). Number of regions varies by Husserl text (often 3: material nature, animal nature, spirit).
5. **Citation**: Edmund Husserl, *Ideen zu einer reinen Phänomenologie und phänomenologischen Philosophie. Erstes Buch* (Halle: Niemeyer, 1913); English: *Ideas Pertaining to a Pure Phenomenology and to a Phenomenological Philosophy: First Book*, trans. F. Kersten (The Hague: Nijhoff, 1982).
6. **Scope**: General phenomenology and philosophy of science. Best for users sorting notes by domain-of-being (natural / vital / spiritual / cultural).

#### Habermas's three knowledge-constitutive interests

1. **Name**: Erkenntnisleitende Interessen — three cognitive interests
2. **Tradition**: German critical theory. Jürgen Habermas (b. 1929), *Erkenntnis und Interesse* (1968).
3. **Frame structure**: Three interests grounding three classes of science: **technical** (empirical-analytic sciences, instrumental control of objectified processes), **practical** (historical-hermeneutic sciences, intersubjective understanding), **emancipatory** (critical sciences, self-reflection and Mündigkeit/autonomy).
4. **Geometric implications**: Three sectors at 120° each, or three vertical bands. Could honor the emancipatory tier as "above" the other two.
5. **Citation**: Jürgen Habermas, *Erkenntnis und Interesse* (Frankfurt: Suhrkamp, 1968); English: *Knowledge and Human Interests*, trans. Jeremy J. Shapiro (Boston: Beacon Press, 1971).
6. **Scope**: Universal — Habermas argues these underlie all forms of human knowledge production. Strong general-purpose Western register.

#### Longino's Critical Contextual Empiricism (four norms)

1. **Name**: CCE four norms
2. **Tradition**: Feminist social epistemology / philosophy of science. Helen Longino (b. 1944).
3. **Frame structure**: Four norms for objective knowledge-production: **venues for criticism**, **uptake of criticism / responsiveness**, **public standards (shared values)**, **tempered equality of intellectual authority**.
4. **Geometric implications**: Four sectors. Useful as a *process/practice* register rather than content category — sorts notes by which norm they exemplify or violate.
5. **Citation**: Helen E. Longino, *Science as Social Knowledge: Values and Objectivity in Scientific Inquiry* (Princeton: Princeton University Press, 1990).
6. **Scope**: Philosophy and sociology of science. Strong for users doing scientific-methodological work.

### Jewish epistemology (Abrahamic — all eligible under religious-lineage rule)

#### PaRDeS (four levels of exegesis)

1. **Name**: PaRDeS — פרד״ס
2. **Tradition**: Medieval Jewish exegesis, formalized in Kabbalistic literature (attributed often to Moses de León, 13th c.); the four levels themselves were established earlier in Talmud and Midrash.
3. **Frame structure**: Four levels of interpretation: **Pəshaṭ פשט** (plain/literal), **Remez רמז** (hint/allegorical), **Drash דרש** (interpretive/midrashic), **Sod סוד** (mystical/esoteric). The acronym spells "orchard."
4. **Geometric implications**: Four concentric rings (peshaṭ outermost as surface, sod innermost as hidden core) is the most faithful — the structure is depth-of-interpretation.
5. **Citation**: *Zohar*, multiple loci. Modern: Gershom Scholem, *Major Trends in Jewish Mysticism* (New York: Schocken, 1941; rev. 1995). Moshe Idel, *Kabbalah: New Perspectives* (New Haven: Yale University Press, 1988).
6. **Scope**: Hebrew Bible / Torah exegesis. Adaptable as a general hermeneutic register (surface / hint / interpretation / mystery).

#### Maimonidean prophecy / knowledge hierarchy

1. **Name**: Mishnah ha-Nevuʾah — Eleven (or twelve) levels of prophecy
2. **Tradition**: Andalusian/Egyptian Jewish philosophy. Moses Maimonides (Rambam, 1138–1204).
3. **Frame structure**: Eleven graduated levels of prophetic knowledge, with Moses's prophecy as a special twelfth beyond the highest.
4. **Geometric implications**: An eleven-step ladder, or eleven concentric rings deepening toward direct union. Eleven is awkward for clean sectoring — better as a ladder.
5. **Citation**: Moses Maimonides, *Moreh Nevukhim* (Guide for the Perplexed), part II, chs. 32–48. English: Shlomo Pines, trans., *The Guide of the Perplexed* (Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 1963).
6. **Scope**: Medieval Jewish religious epistemology. Specialized; best for users with religious-philosophical content.

#### Talmudic 13 hermeneutic middot

1. **Name**: Sheloshah ʿaśar middot — Rabbi Ishmael's 13 rules
2. **Tradition**: Tannaitic / rabbinic Judaism, codified by Rabbi Ishmael (early 2nd c. CE), expanding Hillel's earlier seven rules.
3. **Frame structure**: Thirteen interpretive rules. Thirteen is unwieldy for a sectoral register; the *seven middot of Hillel* may be a cleaner alternative cell-count.
4. **Geometric implications**: 13 is too many for clean sectoring. The seven-Hillel version fits a heptagonal register. Or render as a *toolkit* register where each rule is a chip applied to a passage.
5. **Citation**: *Baraita de-Rabbi Ishmael*. Modern: Louis Jacobs, *Studies in Talmudic Logic and Methodology* (London: Vallentine Mitchell, 1961).
6. **Scope**: Rabbinic legal interpretation. Strong fit for legal-textual users; conceptually adaptable as a hermeneutic toolkit register.

### Hindu beyond Nyāya

> **All 3 candidates below are FILTERED OUT** by religious-lineage rule (all are religious-Vedic-authority frames, non-Abrahamic). Preserved here as scholarly reference.

#### Mīmāṃsā six pramāṇas (Bhāṭṭa school)

1. **Name**: Ṣaṭ-pramāṇa (षट्प्रमाण) — six valid sources of knowledge
2. **Tradition**: Pūrva Mīmāṃsā. Kumārila Bhaṭṭa (7th c. CE) and Prabhākara Miśra (7th c.); the Bhāṭṭa sub-school accepts six pramāṇas.
3. **Frame structure**: **pratyakṣa** (perception), **anumāna** (inference), **upamāna** (comparison/analogy), **śabda** (verbal testimony, esp. Vedic), **arthāpatti** (postulation), **anupalabdhi** (non-apprehension / proof from absence). Six is a clean hexagonal register.
4. **Geometric implications**: Six sectors at 60° each.
5. **Citation**: Kumārila Bhaṭṭa, *Ślokavārttika*, ed. Swāmī Dvārikādāsa Śāstrī (Varanasi: Tara Publications, 1978).
6. **Scope**: Vedic exegesis and orthodox Hindu philosophy.

#### Advaita Vedānta pramāṇa frame

1. **Name**: Advaita pramāṇas — accepts Mīmāṃsā's six but reserves śabda as primary for *brahmajñāna*
2. **Tradition**: Advaita Vedānta. Śaṅkara (Ādi Śaṅkarācārya, c. 788–820).
3. **Frame structure**: Two-tier: 6-fold empirical layer + 1-fold ultimate (śabda only) layer.
4. **Geometric implications**: Two concentric rings — outer hexagonal (6 pramāṇas for empirical), inner single-cell (śabda for absolute).
5. **Citation**: Śaṅkara, *Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya*.
6. **Scope**: Non-dualist Hindu philosophy.

#### Sāṃkhya 25 tattvas

1. **Name**: Pañcaviṃśati-tattva — 25 enumerated principles
2. **Tradition**: Classical Sāṃkhya. Īśvarakṛṣṇa (c. 4th–5th c. CE), *Sāṃkhyakārikā*.
3. **Frame structure**: 25 tattvas in evolutionary cascade.
4. **Geometric implications**: 25 is heavy — a 5-tier register with 5 cells per tier. Awkward for clean sectoring; rich for nested visualization.
5. **Citation**: Īśvarakṛṣṇa, *Sāṃkhyakārikā*, ed. and trans. Gerald J. Larson, *Classical Sāṃkhya*, 2nd rev. ed. (Delhi: Motilal Banarsidass, 1979).
6. **Scope**: Classical Hindu cosmology and psychology.

### Buddhist beyond Mohist contemporaries

> **All 3 candidates below are FILTERED OUT** by religious-lineage rule (all Buddhist religious lineage). Preserved here as scholarly reference.

#### Dharmakīrti's two pramāṇas

1. **Name**: Dvi-pramāṇa — perception and inference
2. **Tradition**: Yogācāra-Sautrāntika Buddhist epistemology. Dharmakīrti (c. 600–660 CE), *Pramāṇavārttika*.
3. **Frame structure**: Strictly two valid means of knowledge: **pratyakṣa** (perception — non-conceptual, bound by causality, of unique particulars *svalakṣaṇa*) and **anumāna** (inference — conceptual, linguistic, of universals *sāmānyalakṣaṇa*).
4. **Geometric implications**: Two hemispheres or two arcs of 180°.
5. **Citation**: Dharmakīrti, *Pramāṇavārttika*. Modern: John D. Dunne, *Foundations of Dharmakīrti's Philosophy* (Boston: Wisdom Publications, 2004).
6. **Scope**: Buddhist epistemology / pramāṇavāda.

#### Madhyamaka catuṣkoṭi (tetralemma)

1. **Name**: Catuṣkoṭi — four corners
2. **Tradition**: Madhyamaka Buddhism. Nāgārjuna (c. 150–250 CE), *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā*.
3. **Frame structure**: Four corners exhausting (and dialectically negating) all positions on a proposition: **A**, **¬A**, **A ∧ ¬A**, **¬(A ∨ ¬A)**.
4. **Geometric implications**: Four quadrants (classic 2×2 logic square).
5. **Citation**: Nāgārjuna, *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā*. English: Jay L. Garfield, trans., *The Fundamental Wisdom of the Middle Way* (New York: Oxford University Press, 1995). Logic-focused: Graham Priest, "The Logic of the Catuṣkoṭi," *Comparative Philosophy* 1, no. 2 (2010): 24–54.
6. **Scope**: Buddhist metaphysics / philosophical logic.

#### Zen sudden / gradual (Rinzai / Sōtō register)

1. **Name**: Tongo / Zengo (頓悟 / 漸悟) — sudden / gradual enlightenment
2. **Tradition**: Chan/Zen Buddhism. Japanese codification: Eisai (Rinzai), Dōgen (Sōtō, 1200–1253).
3. **Frame structure**: Binary register: **tongo** (sudden — Rinzai, *kōan* practice) vs. **zengo** (gradual — Sōtō, *shikantaza* / silent illumination).
4. **Geometric implications**: Two-pole register (left/right or top/bottom).
5. **Citation**: Dōgen, *Shōbōgenzō*. Foundational study: Peter N. Gregory, ed., *Sudden and Gradual: Approaches to Enlightenment in Chinese Thought* (Honolulu: University of Hawai'i Press, 1987).
6. **Scope**: Zen/Chan contemplative practice.

### Feminist / Standpoint

#### Collins's matrix of domination (four domains)

1. **Name**: Matrix of domination — four domains of power
2. **Tradition**: Black feminist thought. Patricia Hill Collins (b. 1948).
3. **Frame structure**: Four interlocking domains of oppression/power: **structural** (organizing institutions), **disciplinary** (managing oppression — bureaucratic practices), **hegemonic** (legitimizing ideology), **interpersonal** (everyday interactions and consciousness). Plus intersectionality of race/class/gender/sexuality cutting across all four.
4. **Geometric implications**: Four quadrants with intersectional axes overlaying.
5. **Citation**: Patricia Hill Collins, *Black Feminist Thought: Knowledge, Consciousness, and the Politics of Empowerment*, 2nd ed. (New York: Routledge, 2000).
6. **Scope**: Critical social epistemology and power analysis.

#### Standpoint epistemology (Harding/Haraway) — non-geometric

1. **Name**: Situated knowledges / strong objectivity
2. **Tradition**: Feminist epistemology. Sandra Harding (b. 1935); Donna Haraway (b. 1944).
3. **Frame structure**: Not categorical — the closest register-shape is *not a cell-grid but a coordinate system*: every note has a "from where" coordinate. Could be implemented as a meta-axis applied to any other register, not a standalone register.
4. **Geometric implications**: A register that re-positions notes by their *standpoint* rather than sorting them into cells. More like a sorting *lens* than a categorical *frame*. See §3.
5. **Citation**: Sandra Harding, "Rethinking Standpoint Epistemology: What Is 'Strong Objectivity'?" in *Feminist Epistemologies*, ed. Linda Alcoff and Elizabeth Potter (New York: Routledge, 1993), 49–82. Donna Haraway, "Situated Knowledges," *Feminist Studies* 14, no. 3 (1988): 575–99.
6. **Scope**: Universal meta-epistemic.

## §3 Traditions that DON'T fit the register format — and why

Several traditions surveyed above resist the register format in a way that is itself informative for Constellation's architecture:

**1. Pluriversal / decolonial frames (Mignolo, Dussel, Quijano partly).** These traditions are explicitly *anti-categorical*. Mignolo's pluriversality argues *against* the one-world organizing logic that any closed register imposes. Putting Mignolo "inside" a register risks performing the very colonial gesture his work critiques.

**2. Wiredu's conceptual-decolonization project.** Wiredu's claim that Western T/F binaries don't map onto Akan *nokware* is *itself* an argument against universalizing any single register.

**3. Hountondji's critical universalism.** Hountondji's *African Philosophy: Myth and Reality* (1983) is a sustained critique of the move to bundle culturally-bounded "ethnophilosophy" as Africa's philosophy.

**4. Daoism (wuwei / ziran).** Daoist epistemology is *constitutively* anti-categorical — the *Zhuangzi*'s "pursuing knowledge has no end" passages and the *Daodejing*'s "the dao that can be named is not the eternal dao" make any categorical register a violation of the tradition.

**5. Māori whakapapa / Aboriginal Australian songlines.** These are *relational-cartographic* not categorical. Whakapapa organizes by genealogical connection; songlines organize knowledge by spatial-narrative trajectory through Country.

**6. Yoruba Ifá 256-odù corpus.** Combinatorial-divinatory, not sectoral.

**7. Inuit Qaujimajatuqangit (8 principles).** These are *values-and-practice* principles, not knowledge categories. Tag/chip overlay fits better than a sectoral register.

**8. Standpoint epistemology / situated knowledges.** Not categories but coordinates — a meta-register, not a primary one.

**9. Talmudic 13 middot.** 13 is the wrong cardinality for clean sectoring; Hillel's 7 might work; but conceptually these are *operations* (how to derive law from text), not *kinds of content*.

**Common thread**: the register format works best for traditions that themselves enumerate *kinds of valid knowledge* (pramāṇa traditions, masādir, Aristotelian categories, Peirce). It works less well for traditions that organize knowledge by *practice*, *relationship*, *standpoint*, or that *critique categorization itself*. Constellation should consider whether to expand the architecture to support **multiple register-shapes**: sectoral (current), tag/chip overlay (for values/operations), relational-graph (for whakapapa-style), coordinate (for standpoint), and matrix (for Ifá-style combinatorial).

## §4 Cross-cultural-pluralism literature

**(a) Indigenous Data Sovereignty and the CARE Principles.** CARE (Collective benefit, Authority to control, Responsibility, Ethics) developed 2018 in Gaborone by the International Indigenous Data Sovereignty Interest Group within RDA. CARE complements FAIR by inserting community-control and ethics into data governance. *Reference*: Stephanie Carroll Rainie et al., "The CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance," *Data Science Journal* 19, no. 43 (2020).

**(b) Decolonial Computing.** Syed Mustafa Ali's "A Brief Introduction to Decolonial Computing" (2014, *XRDS: Crossroads*) argues that computational infrastructures — including ontologies, taxonomies, and user-interface metaphors — carry colonial assumptions about universality, objectivity, and the geometry of knowledge.

**(c) Pluralistic Ontology Design.** Recent work in *Information Systems Journal* and *Technological Forecasting and Social Change* describes "pluriversal technologies" as those "co-designed, co-produced, and co-owned across epistemic communities." *Reference*: Maha Chughtai, "Decoloniality and Information Systems," *Information Systems Journal* (2025).

**(d) Library Science and Controlled Vocabulary Pluralism.** Hope Olson's *The Power to Name: Locating the Limits of Subject Representation in Libraries* (Dordrecht: Kluwer, 2002) is foundational; she shows how universal classification systems systematically marginalize non-Western and non-elite knowledge.

**(e) Anthropology of Knowledge Systems.** Helen Verran's *Science and an African Logic* (Chicago: University of Chicago Press, 2001) — "disconcerting moments" methodology where the friction between two knowledge systems is held open rather than resolved. **Implication for Constellation: the transition between registers is itself a knowledge event.**

**(f) Critical Catalog Theory.** Emily Drabinski, "Queering the Catalog: Queer Theory and the Politics of Correction," *The Library Quarterly* 83, no. 2 (2013): 94–111 — argues against the assumption that any catalog (or register set) can be made "neutral" by adding more categories. The alternative is to make the *historicity and ideology* of each category visible.
